# UVCE Rocketry Club — Propellant Handling Standard Operating Procedure (KNSB)

**Document:** `40_SAFETY/Propellant_Handling_SOP.md`
**Parent document:** `40_SAFETY/Safety_Manual_Master.md`
**Applies to:** handling, grinding, sifting, weighing, mixing, casting, curing, demoulding, storing and
transporting **KNSB propellant** (65% potassium nitrate / 35% sorbitol by mass) and its raw materials
**Propellant formulation:** KNSB, 65% KNO₃ / 35% sorbitol by mass. **No other formulation is
authorised.** See §12.

> **Read this document in full before your first session. Then read it again before every session.
> Do not work from memory.**
>
> **The three-item rule applies to every step of this SOP, from the moment a propellant container is
> opened: a dunk bucket full of water, a fire extinguisher, and a planned escape route, all present
> and verified.**

## 1. Scope, definitions, and the governing numbers

### 1.1 What this SOP covers

| In scope | Out of scope (prohibited — see §12) |
|---|---|
| Grinding potassium nitrate | Grinding or milling **mixed** propellant |
| Sifting and (optionally) desiccating sorbitol | Any propellant other than 65/35 KNSB |
| Weighing, blending, and tumble-mixing the dry powder | Perchlorate or chlorate propellants |
| Melt-casting into moulds | Any additive beyond 0.1% SLES without written authorisation |
| Curing, demoulding, and grain quality control | Case-bonded grains |
| Storage, labelling, and transport | Propellant work outside the sanctioned location |

### 1.2 The governing numbers — memorise these

| Parameter | Value | Consequence of getting it wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Formulation** | **65% KNO₃ / 35% sorbitol by mass** | Off-ratio propellant burns unpredictably |
| **Casting temperature** | **120–130 °C** | Too cold: unmelted lumps and voids. Too hot: decomposition |
| **Temperature tolerance** | **± 5 °C** | An uncontrolled heater has no upper bound |
| **Auto-ignition temperature** | **> 300 °C** | Above this, the propellant ignites without any external ignition source |
| **Decomposition onset (colour change)** | **~175 °C** | First visible warning |
| **Visible smoking, bubbling, strong odour** | **~250 °C** | Second warning — this is an emergency |
| **Ideal KNSB density** | **1.841 g/cm³** | Lower density = voids = over-pressurisation risk |
| **Surfactant dose (SLES)** | **0.1% by mass of molten propellant** | Too much = unpredictable behaviour |
| **Curing before demoulding** | **1–2 days** | Too soon = distorted grain and a stuck coring rod |
| **Excess allowance for small batches** | **20–25%** | Insufficient mix to fill the mould = a wasted, unsafe casting |
| **Energy content** | **~2.7 kJ/g** | 250 g of propellant = **675 kJ** = a 1000 W heater for **11 minutes**, released in seconds |

### 1.3 Definitions used in this SOP

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dry mixed powder** | Potassium nitrate and sorbitol blended together, before melting. **Readily combustible.** |
| **Slurry / melt** | Molten propellant at 120–130 °C |
| **Dunk bucket** | A ≥ 20 L bucket of clean water, within 2 m of the workstation, for immediate immersion of a burned limb or extinguishing a small propellant piece |
| **Shunt** | Twisting the bared ends of igniter leads together so no current can reach the bridgewire |
| **Batch** | One complete casting run from one weighing-out. Identified by a unique batch number. |
| **Grain** | A cast propellant segment |

## 3. Grinding potassium nitrate

### 3.1 Purpose

Potassium nitrate as obtained is granular. Grinding it produces a finer oxidiser, which gives a more
complete and efficient combustion — but a **thicker slurry** that must be scooped rather than poured.
Grinding time directly controls slurry viscosity and therefore casting difficulty.

**Target sieve analysis after a normal 15–30 second grind: approximately 50% of particles below
74 µm**, with most of the remainder below 234 µm.

**Trade-off, stated plainly:**
- **Finer grind** → better performance, higher effective burn rate, **thicker slurry**
- **Shorter grind (≤ 10 s) or "as obtained" fine granular KN** → more fluid slurry, easier casting,
  **at the cost of reduced performance**: less efficient combustion, lower chamber pressure, slightly
  longer burn

For a first batch, **a shorter grind and an easier casting is the correct choice.** A grain you can
actually cast without voids is worth more than a 3% performance gain.

### 3.2 Equipment

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Grinder | **Electric coffee grinder (blade type), dedicated to potassium nitrate only.** Never used for anything else, ever |
| Loading | **~2 tablespoons (30 ml) per load.** Small loads. Never fill the grinder |
| Time | **15–30 seconds**, slowly "gyrating" (rocking/swirling) the grinder during operation to keep material moving |
| Cooling | **Let the grinder rest and cool for 5 minutes between every load.** Heat is the enemy |
| PPE | Chemical splash goggles or safety glasses, **dust mask**, nitrile gloves, cotton clothing |
| Location | **Minimum 3 m from any open sorbitol, mixed powder, or other fuel powder** |
| Grounding | Ground the grinder body to the grounding rod |

### 3.3 The procedure

1. **Verify the pre-work checklist is signed.** Do not proceed without it.
2. **Clear the area.** No fuel powder, no mixed powder, no paper, no cloth, no solvent within 3 m. No
   ignition sources within 10 m.
3. **Put on PPE**: goggles/safety glasses, **dust mask**, nitrile gloves.
4. **Confirm the grinder is clean and dry**, with no residue from a previous batch and no moisture.
5. **Load ~2 tablespoons (30 ml) of potassium nitrate.** Do not overfill.
6. **Seal the lid fully** and confirm it is latched.
7. **Run for 15–30 seconds**, gyrating the grinder gently to keep the powder moving and prevent
   caking at the blades.
8. **Stop. Let the grinder cool for 5 minutes.** Feel the body — if it is warm to the touch, wait
   longer. **A hot grinder is a grinder that can ignite the next load.**
9. **Empty the ground KN into its labelled, airtight container** using a **non-sparking scoop**.
   Seal immediately. **Potassium nitrate is hygroscopic — ground material picks up moisture fast and
   moisture degrades performance.**
10. **Repeat** for the required mass, 30 ml at a time, with a 5-minute cooling period between loads.
11. **Clean the grinder** after the session with a **soft dry brush** — never water, never a cloth that
    will be reused for fuel. Store it sealed and labelled.
12. **Record**: mass ground, grind time per load, batch number, date, operator.

### 3.4 Why you NEVER grind mixed propellant

> ### 🔴 THE ABSOLUTE RULE
> **Grind the oxidiser and the fuel SEPARATELY. NEVER grind, mill, blend, or agitate the mixed
> propellant in any powered device.**

**The reason:** once potassium nitrate and sorbitol are blended, the powder is a **complete
fuel-oxidiser mixture**. It is **readily combustible** — Nakka's explicit warning. In that state, any
localised heat or a single spark can initiate it.

A grinder is a machine that:
- **generates friction heat** at the blades and between particles
- **can produce sparks** if a foreign object (a stone, a piece of metal, a hard granule) is present
- **concentrates material** in a small volume where heat cannot escape
- **suspends dust** in air inside a confined housing — a dust explosion requires only a dispersed
  combustible dust and an ignition source

Any one of those alone is survivable. Inside a grinder, they occur together. **Grinding mixed
propellant is how people lose hands.**

**The correct sequence is always:**

```
1. Grind the OXIDISER alone         → store sealed
2. Sift/process the FUEL alone      → store sealed
3. Weigh each separately
4. Mix by TUMBLING (no blades, no grinding) — Section 5
```

### 3.5 Grinding prohibitions

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Grind mixed propellant** | Readily combustible; friction, heat and sparks in a confined machine |
| **Grind potassium nitrate in a grinder that has held sorbitol, charcoal, or any fuel** | Cross-contamination creates a combustible mixture inside a heat-generating machine |
| **Grind potassium nitrate that may contain fuel residue** | Same reason |
| **Use a mortar and pestle, ball mill, or blender** | Impact and friction; ball mills generate heat and sparks; a blender is not sealed against dust |
| **Grind continuously without cooling breaks** | Heat accumulates. **5 minutes between loads, always** |
| **Grind in a confined, unventilated space** | Dust accumulation and a dust-explosion risk |
| **Grind near an open container of fuel powder** | Cross-contamination and a combined ignition source |
| **Grind KN indoors in a room with other people present** | Dust exposure to uninvolved people; nobody without PPE should be in the area |
| **Smoke, vape, or have any ignition source nearby** | Obvious, and still a common cause of accidents |
| **Use a plastic-bodied grinder with visible static build-up** | Static is a genuine ignition path for dust. **Ground the grinder** |

## 4. Sorbitol: sifting and desiccation

### 4.1 Sifting — mandatory

**Why:** powdered sorbitol **cakes into hard clumps** in storage. Those clumps do not melt properly
during casting, leaving unmelted lumps and voids in the grain. Voids and lumps are among the leading
causes of motor failure.

**How:**

1. Put on a **dust mask**, goggles, and nitrile gloves.
2. Break up the bulk of the caking by hand, in the sealed container, or by pressing through a coarse
   strainer.
3. **Sift through a flour sifter or fine kitchen strainer** into a clean, dry, labelled container.
4. **Discard or re-process the retained lumps** — do not add them to the sifted material.
5. **Seal the sifted sorbitol immediately.** Sorbitol is hygroscopic; as-obtained, it holds more
   moisture than sucrose or dextrose.
6. **Clean the sifter** after use with a dry brush. **Do not use the same sifter for potassium nitrate
   without a full clean** — cross-contamination.

### 4.2 Desiccation — optional, only if maximising performance

**Why:** residual moisture in sorbitol **suppresses the burn rate** by reducing the burn-rate
coefficient `a`. It does not change the pressure exponent `n`. Removing moisture gives more predictable
and slightly higher performance.

**How:**

1. **Sift the sorbitol into a shallow pan**, spreading it no more than 2–3 cm deep to maximise surface
   area.
2. **Place the pan in a desiccator for 1–2 days.**
3. **A desiccator** = an airtight container (a large Tupperware works) lined with a **2–3 cm layer of
   fresh calcium chloride** beneath a perforated shelf. The sorbitol must not contact the calcium
   chloride directly.
4. **Replace the calcium chloride when it becomes damp, clumped, or liquid.** Damp desiccant does
   nothing. Fresh desiccant per batch is the practical rule.
5. **Record the desiccation time and the desiccant condition** in the batch record.

> ### ⚠️ The desiccation trade-off — read this before deciding
> Desiccated sorbitol produces a **stiffer, more viscous slurry that is significantly harder to cast**.
> For a first batch, or for any batch where casting quality is uncertain, **skip desiccation.** An
> easy-to-cast, void-free grain made with as-obtained sorbitol will outperform a void-riddled grain
> made with perfectly dry sorbitol every single time. **Casting quality beats material purity.**

### 4.3 Moisture check (optional quantitative check)

1. Weigh a **10.0 g** sample of sifted sorbitol on the precision scale. Record the mass.
2. Dry it in an oven at **60 °C for 60 minutes** — **never near propellant, and never in equipment
   used for propellant.** Use a separate, dedicated oven, or skip this check.
3. Cool in a desiccator and reweigh.
4. **Mass loss above 0.5%** indicates significant moisture. Desiccate further, or accept reduced
   performance and adjust expectations for the burn rate.

## 5. Weighing, blending, and mixing

### 5.1 Weighing — accuracy is mandatory

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Scale resolution | **0.01 g** minimum for batch work |
| Scale capacity | Sized to the batch — do not weigh 5 g on a 5 kg-capacity scale |
| Calibration | **Verified against a known mass at every session.** Record the check |
| Formulation accuracy | **65% KNO₃ / 35% sorbitol by mass**, accurate to **± 0.5%** of each component |
| Worked example (250 g batch) | KNO₃ = **162.5 g**, sorbitol = **87.5 g**. Tolerance ± 0.8 g on each |
| Excess allowance | Prepare **20–25% excess** for small batches, to account for casting waste. For a 250 g target, weigh **300–312 g** total |
| Recording | Every mass recorded in the batch record, with the scale's calibration check value |

**Why the excess matters:** molten KNSB is viscous and clings to the vessel, the stirrer, and the
funnel. A batch that is exactly the mould volume will leave you with a **partially filled mould** —
meaning a grain with an internal void or an incomplete burn surface. Both are dangerous. **Always mix
more than you need and discard the residue safely.**

**Why the accuracy matters:** the 65/35 ratio sets the oxygen balance. A significant deviation changes
the burn rate, the chamber pressure, and the combustion temperature — and those are the numbers the
motor case and nozzle were designed around.

### 5.2 The blending procedure

1. **Verify the pre-work checklist is signed.**
2. **Weigh the potassium nitrate** into its labelled container. Record the mass.
3. **Weigh the sorbitol** into its labelled container. Record the mass.
4. **Verify both masses** against the target. **Have a second person verify the arithmetic and read the
   scale.** This is a 10-second check that prevents a wasted or dangerous batch.
5. **Blend the two together in one clean, dry container.** Use a **gentle folding or stirring motion**
   with a non-sparking scoop or a gloved hand — **not** a powered device.
6. **Complete mixing is required** for optimum and consistent performance. Incomplete mixing gives
   localised oxidiser-rich and fuel-rich regions, which burn unevenly.

> ### 🔴 From this moment, the powder is a complete propellant
> **The mixed powder is READILY COMBUSTIBLE.** Treat it exactly as you would treat loose propellant —
> because it is. Keep it away from all ignition sources. Transfer it to a closed container the moment
> mixing is done. **Never leave mixed powder sitting open on a bench while you do something else.**

### 5.3 Tumble mixing — the safe method for achieving uniformity

**Reference method (Nakka):** a **rotating tumbler at 30 RPM for at least 30 minutes**. Nakka's later
version runs at approximately **100 RPM with a 1 kg capacity**.

**Why tumbling and not grinding:** a tumbler mixes by **gentle, low-energy agitation**. There are no
blades, no impacts, and very little friction heat. This is the difference between mixing and milling,
and it is a safety-critical distinction. **Tumbling is how you achieve uniformity without creating an
ignition source.**

| Tumbler requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Speed | **30 RPM** for the standard method; up to 100 RPM for the higher-capacity version |
| Duration | **30 minutes minimum** for 30 RPM |
| Container | Sealed, non-sparking interior (HDPE), with a lid that cannot work loose |
| Fill level | **No more than 50–60% of container volume**, so the powder actually tumbles rather than sitting in a lump |
| Grounding | **Ground the tumbler body to the grounding rod.** Static build-up in a rotating plastic container of dust is a real ignition path |
| Location | Isolated — no other activity in the area while the tumbler runs |
| Attendance | **Never leave a running tumbler unattended.** Someone stays with it and watches it |
| After tumbling | **Transfer the mixed powder immediately to a closed, labelled container** |

**If you do not have a tumbler**, hand-blending with a gentle folding motion for **at least 10 minutes**
is acceptable for small batches, with a second person verifying visual uniformity (no streaks, no
colour variation). **Record which method was used.**

### 5.4 Mixing prohibitions

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Grind, mill, or blend the mixed powder in any powered device with blades** | Friction, heat, sparks, and confined dust |
| **Use a kitchen mixer, blender, food processor, or paint shaker** | All of them generate heat and impact |
| **Tumble without grounding the container or tumbler** | Static discharge |
| **Leave a tumbler running unattended** | If something goes wrong, nobody is there |
| **Exceed 250 g of mixed powder at one time** | Quantity discipline. Small quantities limit the consequence of any event |
| **Leave mixed powder open on the bench** | It is a complete propellant. It gets sealed immediately |
| **Store mixed powder for more than 24 hours before casting** | Moisture pickup; the material degrades and the burn rate drops |
| **Mix in a container that held fuel only or oxidiser only without cleaning** | Contamination and clumping |
| **Smoke, vape, use a phone near the mixing area** | Ignition sources and distraction |

## 6. Casting — the most hazardous step

> **Everything up to this point was preparation. This is the step where the propellant is hot, fluid,
> energetic, and closest to your body. Read every word of this section before your first casting, and
> re-read it before every casting.**

### 6.1 The heating vessel — non-negotiable

> ### 🔴 USE ONLY A THERMOSTATICALLY TEMPERATURE-CONTROLLED HEATING VESSEL WITH NO EXPOSED ELEMENTS
> A thermostatically controlled electric deep fryer or skillet is the reference solution.
>
> **The critical intent: NO EXPOSED HEATING SURFACE SIGNIFICANTLY HOTTER THAN THE PROPELLANT'S
> MELTING POINT.**

### 6.2 Why an exposed-element heater is genuinely dangerous

This is not a stylistic preference. It is the difference between a controlled process and an
uncontrolled one.

**The failure mode, stated step by step:**

1. An exposed-element heater (an open coil hotplate, a resistive coil, a gas flame) has surfaces that
   reach **several hundred degrees Celsius** — well above the propellant's **auto-ignition temperature
   of > 300 °C**.
2. If the vessel is heated **before the powder is added**, those surfaces are already hot.
3. Powder falls onto, or touches, a surface that is hotter than the auto-ignition temperature.
4. **Ignition can occur immediately** — with no warning, before the bulk has even begun to melt.

Nakka's warning is explicit: if the wrong vessel is used and surfaces get hot *before* adding powder,
**ignition can occur immediately.**

> ### ⚠️ The tolerance is NOT a licence
> KNSB has a documented tolerance to overheating. Nakka's deliberate overheat test — a 16 g sample
> heated with a forced-air heat gun, monitored with a type-K thermocouple — found that the material
> **failed to ignite even at over 300 °C**, despite severe charring.
>
> **That tolerance applies to ALREADY-MOLTEN slurry being gradually overheated.**
> It is **not** permission to use any heating vessel. It is a **margin of safety**, nothing more.
> The correct reading is: *if something goes wrong with a properly controlled vessel, we have room.*
> It is **not**: *we have so much room that the vessel does not matter.*

**The correct vessel characteristics:**

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| **Thermostatic control** with an adjustable setpoint | You must be able to hold 120–130 °C, ±5 °C |
| **No exposed heating element** in contact with, or reachable by, the powder | Powder contacting a several-hundred-degree surface ignites |
| **Stable temperature hold** — verified by test | A thermostat that overshoots by 40 °C is not a thermostat |
| **Deep enough** that the melt is contained and cannot spill over the rim | Molten propellant spilling onto a hot base is a fire |
| **Non-stick or stainless interior** | Easier to empty fully; less material retained |
| **A lid available** that can be placed over the vessel | To smother the vessel contents if they ignite |
| **Grounded** electrical connection, on an **RCD/ELCB** | Electrical fault protection |

**Before the first use, and at every session, test the vessel with water only:**
set it to the intended casting temperature and verify with the IR thermometer that it holds within
± 5 °C of the setpoint, with no overshoot. **Record the test.**

### 6.3 Temperature control during casting

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Casting temperature** | **120–130 °C** |
| **Tolerance** | **± 5 °C** — check the melt temperature **every 2 minutes** and record it |
| **Melting point of sorbitol** | 110–112 °C (this is why 120–130 works) |
| **Auto-ignition temperature** | **> 300 °C** |
| **Decomposition onset (visible colour change)** | **~175 °C** |
| **Visible smoking, bubbling, strong burning-caramel odour** | **~250 °C** |
| **Monitoring instrument** | Infrared thermometer (most convenient, non-contacting, no contamination). A digital probe thermometer as a cross-check. **Both verified at the start of the session** |

### 6.4 The casting procedure

**Step 1 — Set up (before any propellant is opened)**

1. Verify the pre-work checklist is signed.
2. Position the **dunk bucket within 2 m**, the **fire extinguisher within 5 m**, and confirm the
   **escape route is clear**.
3. Confirm the **fire blanket** and **sand bucket** are within reach.
4. Put on: **chemical splash goggles, full face shield, leather apron, heat-resistant gloves over
   nitrile gloves, cotton/FR clothing, closed shoes, hair tied back.**
5. Lay out: **silicone or wooden stirrers** (never metal), **non-sparking scoop/spatula**, the
   **precision scale**, both **thermometers**, the **mould**, the **lubricated coring rod**, and
   **silicone grease**.
6. Have the **mould** clean, dry, and at room temperature, positioned on a stable, non-combustible
   surface that can be **firmly tapped** against a hard base.
7. Have the **surfactant (SLES)** measured and ready — **0.1% by mass of the molten propellant**. For a
   250 g batch that is **0.25 g**. Weigh it; do not estimate it.

**Step 2 — Preheat the vessel, empty**

8. Switch on the **thermostatically controlled vessel** and set it to the casting temperature.
9. **Let it reach and stabilise at temperature. Verify with the IR thermometer. Record the reading.**
10. **Do NOT add powder to a vessel that has not stabilised**, and **never** to a vessel with exposed
    hot elements.

**Step 3 — Melt**

11. Add **about half** of the weighed powder mix to the vessel.
12. **Stir often** with a silicone or wooden stirrer to assist melting and to distribute heat.
13. Once the first half has melted, **add the remaining half** gradually.
14. **Check the temperature every 2 minutes.** If it rises above **135 °C, reduce the setpoint or switch
    off briefly.** Record each reading.
15. **Monitor colour continuously:**
    - Starts **nearly colourless**
    - Turns **white** as it becomes fully melted
    - If the sorbitol had residual moisture, melting is rapid and the slurry looks **pure white or
      almost translucent with tiny bubbles** as water boils off
    - As water is driven off, the colour shifts to **light ivory**
16. **Continue heating and stirring for an additional 5–10 minutes** to reach casting temperature and
    eliminate lumps. **Do not shortcut this** — unmelted lumps become voids in the grain.

**Step 4 — Add the surfactant**

17. Once the propellant is **fully molten and at 120–130 °C**, add the pre-weighed **SLES at 0.1% by
    mass** (0.25 g for a 250 g batch).
18. **Stir gently and observe.** The viscosity drops **immediately** — Nakka describes the result as
    "readily pourable, having a viscosity similar to maple syrup."
19. **Do not add more than 0.1%.** More surfactant does not make it better; it changes the material's
    behaviour in ways we have not characterised.

> ### Surfactant notes
> - Use **Sodium Laureth Sulfate (SLES)**, 70–80% liquid concentrate. Cheap and widely available.
> - ⚠️ **Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS) is a different product and may not work as effectively.** Check
>   the label.
> - A retail **foaming bath gel** works if its main listed ingredient (other than water) is SLES — but
>   it contains appreciable water, so more may be needed and extra heating time is required to drive
>   the water off. **Prefer the concentrate.**

**Step 5 — Pour and load the mould**

20. **If the slurry is fluid enough** (with surfactant it usually is), pour steadily into the mould.
21. **If it is still too thick to pour** — which is normal without surfactant — **pour and scoop
    simultaneously** with a spoon or silicone spatula. **Do not wait for it to become pourable.** Waiting
    means overheating.
22. **Fill the mould completely**, slightly overfull at the top, so that shrinkage during cooling does
    not leave a void at the top surface.
23. **Immediately and firmly tap the mould bottom against a hard surface, repeatedly.** **This is an
    important step** — it forces trapped air to the surface. Tap for a minimum of **30 seconds**, and
    keep tapping as the material stiffens. **Air bubbles and voids significantly increase the burning
    surface area and lead to over-pressurisation or CATO.**
24. **Immediately insert the lubricated coring rod** (silicone grease / Super Lube), making certain it
    is **fully inserted all the way down**. Do not push it in and out repeatedly — one clean insertion.
25. **Top up** the mould if the level has dropped.
26. **Set the mould aside to cool** in a stable position, away from draughts, on a non-combustible
    surface, **out of anyone's path**. Mark it clearly as containing hot propellant.

**Step 6 — Shut down**

27. **Switch off the heating vessel.**
28. **Do not clean the vessel while it is hot.** Let it cool. **Never scrape hot propellant residue with
    a metal tool.**
29. **When the vessel is cool**, clean residue into a labelled waste container. **Residue is propellant**
    — it is stored and disposed of as propellant, never washed down a drain and never put in general
    waste.
30. **Record the batch**: mass cast, ratio, temperatures observed throughout, surfactant mass, melt
    time, tap time, coring rod diameter, mould identification, operators, date, batch number.

### 6.5 Casting prohibitions

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Use a heating vessel with an exposed element** | Powder contacting a several-hundred-degree surface ignites immediately |
| **Add powder to a vessel that has not stabilised at temperature** | Same |
| **Use a gas flame, an open fire, a microwave, or an induction hob with a thin pan** | No thermostatic control; surfaces far above auto-ignition |
| **Use a vessel with a failed, bypassed, or defeated thermostat** | Uncontrolled heating has no upper bound |
| **Exceed 130 °C** | Decomposition onset is around 175 °C, and the margin is not for spending |
| **Leave the melt unattended, even for a moment** | Every fire scenario starts with "I only turned away for a second" |
| **Use metal utensils in the vessel** | Scratches the coating → hot spots → local overheating |
| **Cast in the presence of any ignition source** | Obvious, and still done |
| **Cast alone** | If you are burned you cannot help yourself |
| **Cast without the dunk bucket, extinguisher, and escape route in place** | The three-item rule |
| **Cast in synthetic clothing** | It melts into skin |
| **Cast more than 250 g of molten propellant at one time** | Quantity discipline |
| **Add anything to the melt other than the authorised 0.1% SLES** | Uncharacterised additives cause catastrophic failures of proven designs |
| **Use a glass mould or glass container** | Shatters |
| **Pour molten propellant into a wet or damp mould** | Steam explosions and spattering |
| **Try to extinguish a burning melt with a small amount of water** | Spatters and spreads. Use large volumes of water, or sand |

### 6.6 Warning signs and the emergency response during casting

| Observation | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Melt colour shifting from white/ivory towards **yellow or amber** | Decomposition beginning (~175 °C) | **Reduce heat immediately.** Check temperature. If above 135 °C, switch off and let it cool. **Do not continue to cast this batch without the Safety Officer's assessment** |
| **Greyish-amber** colour | Serious overheating (~225 °C) | **Switch off. Evacuate the immediate area. Do not approach until cool.** The batch is scrapped |
| **Bubbling, puffs of smoke, strong burning-caramel odour** | ~250 °C — advanced decomposition | **Evacuate. Withdraw to at least 10 m. Prepare for ignition.** Do not attempt to move the vessel |
| **Visible flame, hissing, bright light** | **Ignition** | Follow `Safety_Manual_Master.md` §7.5: turn your face away, move away at right angles to the plume, do not retrieve anything, account for everyone, treat injuries |
| **Any smell you do not recognise** | Unknown | **Stop. Switch off. Investigate from a distance.** Do not put your face over the vessel |

**On the moment you suspect something is wrong — the correct instinct is to step back, not to lean in.**
You cannot fix a batch that is already overheating. You can only avoid being next to it. **The batch
is replaceable. You are not.**

## 7. Curing and demoulding

### 7.1 What happens during curing

- Cast KNSB stays **pliable** after cooling. A grain can deform if handled carelessly.
- After **one day**, the exposed surfaces become quite hard; the inside stays "waxy" but is rigid
  enough to handle.
- **KNSB cures faster than KNSU or KNDX — 1 to 2 days.**
- Full hardening may take **up to a week**, but it is acceptable to fire a grain any time after
  demoulding.

### 7.2 Demoulding procedure

1. **Wait 1–2 days.** The exact time depends on residual moisture and grain size.
2. **Do not demould too soon.** Demoulding early **distorts the grain when extracting the coring rod**
   — and a distorted core means a non-uniform burning surface and an unpredictable pressure curve.
3. **Warm the mould slightly if the grain sticks** — a warm water bath around the outside of the mould,
   **never a flame, never a heat gun, never an oven**.
4. **Extract the coring rod with a slow, straight, twisting pull.** Never lever it sideways.
5. **Do not force it.** If it will not come out, wait another day. **Force is how grains get cracked,
   and cracks are the number one cause of CATO.**
6. **Handle the grain with both hands, supporting it along its length.** Until fully cured, KNSB
   **chips easily** — noticeably more so than KNSU or KNDX when cut with a hand saw. A coarse blade
   causes chipping.
7. **Place the grain on a soft, clean, non-shedding surface** for inspection. Never on bare metal, never
   on grit, never on a surface with swarf or debris.

### 7.3 Grain quality control — two mandatory checks, on every segment

**Perform these on every grain segment. A grain that fails either check is rejected and the batch is
investigated.**

#### Check 1 — Density check

**Purpose:** detects air bubbles and voids. Air inclusion of a few percent is negligible, but
**sizeable bubbles, many bubbles, or trapped voids significantly increase the burning surface area,
leading to over-pressurisation or CATO.**

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Weigh the grain segment on the precision scale. Record the mass |
| 2 | Measure its volume — **calipers are more accurate than a ruler** |
| 3 | Compute **density = mass ÷ volume** |
| 4 | Compare against **ideal KNSB density = 1.841 g/cm³** |
| 5 | The **ratio of actual to ideal density is the quality benchmark** |

**Notes:**
- The core diameter need not be measured explicitly — it equals the **coring rod diameter**.
- If the grain is integral with its casting tube, **subtract the tube's mass and thickness**.

**Acceptance criteria (measured values — see `config/engineering_constants.md`):**

| Density ratio | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **≈ 0.95 (95% of ideal)** | **Typical for a good cast** using the described method | Accept |
| **0.90 – 0.95** | Some air inclusion | Investigate. If the flaw is **external**, it may be repaired |
| **< 0.90 with an external bubble** | Void visible on the surface | **Repair**: open the cavity and fill with molten propellant |
| **< 0.90 with no visible external flaw** | **Hidden voids or porosity** | 🔴 **DISCARD THE GRAIN** |

> 🔴 **Why < 0.90 means discard:** hidden voids increase the burning surface area unpredictably,
> which raises chamber pressure above the design value. Firing such a grain **risks a CATO from
> unpredictable burning-rate behaviour.** There is no way to inspect a cast grain internally, so
> density is the only proxy you have. **Do not talk yourself into firing a marginal grain.**

- **A grain may not be released on density alone** — it must also pass the tap test below.

#### Check 2 — Tap test (coin tap test)

**Applicability:** only for a grain with an **integral casting tube acting as the inhibitor**. The
inhibitor **must** be intimately bonded — any disbonding lets hot gas seep between the inhibitor and
the propellant, **increasing the burning area** and over-pressurising the motor.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Only perform after full cure** — otherwise disbonding indications may be false. Residual moisture in the bondline can cause a false failure; a few days in a desiccator often converts a fail to a pass |
| 2 | Tap the inhibitor **gently with a coin** (a larger coin works better) at points **all around** the segment |
| 3 | The sound and feel should be a **solid tap**, not a dull one |
| 4 | **Listen to the *relative* tap sounds.** Every tap should sound identical. Any point that sounds distinctly different — duller, like tapping hard rubber instead of hard plastic — signals possible disbonding |

> ### 🔴 What to actually watch for
> **A different tap response BETWEEN GRAIN SEGMENTS OF THE SAME BATCH.** If one segment sounds
> distinctly different and less solid, its inhibitor bond may be flawed.
> **REJECT THAT SEGMENT.**
>
> A single isolated odd spot is generally not a problem.
> **A different tap sound at the seam of a rolled casting tube is normal.**
>
> ⚠️ **The difference is hard to describe in words and takes experience to recognise.** Practise on
> known-good grains.

### 7.4 Grain handling and storage after curing

- **Label every grain immediately** with batch number, mass, density ratio, tap test result, date, and
  operator — **on the grain or on its container, not on a loose piece of paper**.
- **Store in a sealed, labelled container** with a desiccant sachet where available.
- **Never drop, knock, or stack grains.** A cracked grain is a CATO.
- **Any grain that has been dropped is scrapped.** No exceptions, no inspection-based reprieves. A
  crack inside a brittle grain may not be visible.
- **Any grain that smells strongly, is discoloured, or has a soft or tacky surface** is quarantined and
  assessed by the Safety Officer.

### 7.5 Grain priming (required for KNSB — do this before firing, not before storage)

Bare KNSB is **reluctant to ignite**. A **thin** coat of ignition primer on the exposed grain surfaces
— the **core and both end faces** — is required.

> ⚠️ **A HEAVY coat defeats the purpose and causes delayed ignition.** Thin means thin.
>
> ⚠️ **Nakka's documented pyrolant coating uses potassium perchlorate + sulphur + epoxy — a
> PERCHLORATE formulation. Per `Safety_Manual_Master.md` §3.4, perchlorates are for the experienced
> only. This club does not start there.** A nitrate-based primer must be researched, authorised, and
> tested before use. **Until then, grains are fired unprimed, with an appropriately sized igniter and
> the expectation of a slower, mountain-shaped pressure curve.**

## 8. Storage

### 8.1 Quantity limits

| Material | Maximum stored |
|---|---|
| **Finished KNSB propellant (all grains, all batches)** | **3 kg total** |
| Oxidiser (potassium nitrate), ground or unground | **2 kg** |
| Fuel (sorbitol) | **2 kg** |
| Mixed dry powder | **250 g and stored for no more than 24 hours** |
| Ignition powder (80% KNO₃ / 20% charcoal) | **50 g** |
| Waste propellant and residue awaiting disposal | **250 g, in a labelled sealed container** |

**These limits are absolute until the Safety Committee raises them in writing**, based on documented
heritage.

### 8.2 Containers

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Material | **HDPE or polypropylene**, airtight, screw lid. **Opaque or stored in the dark** where possible |
| Condition | Clean, dry, undamaged, with an intact lid seal. **Replace any container with a cracked body or a hardened seal** |
| Size | **Sized to the contents** — a half-empty large container holds a large volume of humid air |
| ❌ Never | **Glass** (shatters), **metal that can spark** (use HDPE), **open bags**, **cling film**, **food or drink containers**, **unlabelled containers** |
| Desiccant | A sachet of fresh silica gel in containers of grain or mixed powder where available |

### 8.3 Labelling — mandatory on every container

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ⚠  KNSB PROPELLANT — OXIDISER/FUEL MIXTURE                 │
│     Flammable solid. Keep away from heat, sparks, flame.    │
│                                                             │
│  Material:      KNSB 65% KNO3 / 35% sorbitol                │
│  Batch no.:     KNSB-2027-003                                │
│  Mass:          242 g                                        │
│  Date cast:     14 May 2027                                  │
│  Density ratio: 0.98                                         │
│  Tap test:      PASS                                         │
│  Responsible:   <name>                                       │
│  Store:         Locked cabinet, dry, < 30 °C, away from fuel │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**A container without a label is an unlabelled energetic material, and that is a NO-GO condition.**
Label the container **before** it is filled, never after.

### 8.4 Storage conditions

| Parameter | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | **Below 30 °C**, stable. **Never in direct sunlight, never in a vehicle in the sun, never near a heat source.** Fluctuating temperature causes thermal cycling and can crack grains |
| **Humidity** | **Below 50% RH** where achievable. **KNSB is hygroscopic** — moisture degrades performance and promotes bondline failures. A sealed container with desiccant is the practical answer |
| **Separation — mandatory** | **Oxidisers stored separately from fuels**, and both separately from **mixed powder, finished propellant, and ignition powder**. Never in the same container. Never in the same cabinet compartment without a physical divider |
| **Separation from other materials** | Away from **solvents, adhesives, oils, aerosol cans, LPG cylinders, batteries, and anything electrical**. Away from **tools** (impact and sparks). Away from **general workshop and household chemicals** |
| **Access** | **Locked cabinet. Access controlled. Access logged.** Only authorised members hold the key. **The key register is maintained by the Safety Officer** |
| **Location** | A dedicated, dry, cool, well-ventilated space. **Never in a residential room, never in a hostel, never in a shared cupboard, never in a vehicle overnight** |
| **Housekeeping** | **No packaging, paper, cloth, or loose combustible material in the storage cabinet.** Nothing that could act as kindling |
| **Signage** | The cabinet bears a clear external marking: **"DANGEROUS — OXIDISER AND PROPELLANT — NO IGNITION SOURCES — AUTHORISED ACCESS ONLY"**, with the Safety Officer's contact number |
| **Inventory** | A **stock register** records every addition and removal, with the date, mass, batch, and person responsible. **The register is reconciled at every monthly club meeting** |
| **Firefighting** | A **correct fire extinguisher within 5 m** of the storage cabinet, and a **water source accessible** |

### 8.5 Storage prohibitions

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Store oxidiser and fuel in the same container** | That container is a propellant |
| **Store propellant in a hostel room, residence, or any occupied space** | An occupied space is the worst possible place for it. There is no version of this that is acceptable |
| **Store in a vehicle** | Heat, uncontrolled, and unattended |
| **Store above the quantity limits** | Quantity discipline limits the consequence of every possible event |
| **Store unlabelled or mislabelled containers** | The next person will not know what it is |
| **Store propellant in glass** | Shatters |
| **Store mixed powder for more than 24 hours** | Moisture pickup and degradation |
| **Store propellant near an ignition source, electrical panel, or battery** | Obvious |
| **Leave the storage cabinet unlocked, "just for a minute"** | The one minute is the one that matters |
| **Allow an unauthorised person to access the cabinet** | Access control is the entire point of the lock |
| **Store scraped propellant residue in general waste** | It is propellant. It is stored and disposed of as propellant |

### 8.6 Shelf life and periodic inspection

- **Mixed dry powder:** use within **24 hours**. Beyond that, re-verify or discard.
- **Cast KNSB grains:** inspect at every use. Look for **cracks, chips, discolouration, soft or tacky
  surfaces, and inhibitor separation**.
- **Monthly inspection** of all stored material by the Safety Officer: condition, labels, quantities,
  container integrity, cabinet lock, and extinguisher status. **Recorded.**
- **Any material showing discolouration, a strong or unusual odour, or surface tackiness is
  quarantined** and assessed before use.
- **Oldest stock is used first.**
- **Anything that has been in storage for more than 12 months** is inspected by the Safety Officer and
  either re-verified or disposed of.

### 8.7 Disposal

- **Never** burn propellant in the open as "disposal", **never** put it in general waste, **never**
  pour it down a drain, **never** bury it casually.
- Small quantities of residue and waste propellant are **accumulated in a labelled, sealed container**
  (≤ 250 g), and disposed of by **burning in small, separated lots at the sanctioned test site**,
  under Safety Officer supervision, following the fire precautions of a static test — **dunk bucket,
  extinguisher, escape route, distance, no dry vegetation.**
- **Disposal is a documented operation**: what, how much, where, when, who supervised. It is logged.
- **If in doubt about how to dispose of something, ask the Safety Officer. Do not improvise.**

## 9. Transport

### 9.1 When transport is necessary

Propellant and raw materials are moved **only** between the sanctioned storage location and the
sanctioned test or casting site. There are no other journeys.

### 9.2 Quantity limits

| Material | Maximum per vehicle |
|---|---|
| Finished propellant (grains) | **2 kg**, in one sealed, labelled, rigid container |
| Mixed dry powder | **250 g**, sealed |
| Oxidiser or fuel (raw) | **2 kg each**, in separate sealed labelled containers |
| Ignition powder | **50 g**, sealed, in a separate container |
| **Oxidiser and fuel must never be in the same container** | Always |

### 9.3 Containers

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Type | **Rigid, sealed, non-sparking, labelled** container. A fitted HDPE case or a rigid box with the contents immobilised |
| Contents | **Immobilised** — packed so nothing can shift, rub, or impact. **Grains are the fragile item; they are supported along their length and separated from each other** |
| Padding | Non-combustible, non-shedding padding. **Never loose paper, cloth, or foam that can shift into a gap and abrade the grain** |
| Labelling | **Externally labelled** with contents, mass, batch number, responsible person, and an emergency contact number |
| Position in the vehicle | **Boot or load area**, secured against movement, **away from the occupants**, **away from the battery and any electrical wiring**, **away from heat sources and direct sunlight** |
| ❌ Never | **Glass. Metal cans that can spark. Open bags. Food containers. Unlabelled containers. Propellant loose on a seat** |

### 9.4 Vehicle rules

1. **Never leave propellant unattended in a vehicle.** Not for fuel, not for food, not for a phone
   call, not for five minutes.
2. **Never leave propellant in a parked vehicle in the sun.** Interior temperatures exceed 50 °C.
3. **No smoking in the vehicle at any time, with or without propellant.** No lighters, no matches.
4. **No other hazardous materials in the same vehicle load** — no petrol cans, no LPG cylinders, no
   solvents, no adhesives, no fireworks.
5. **Minimum of two authorised persons travel with the material.** One drives; one is responsible for
   the load and knows exactly what is in the vehicle and what to do if something happens.
6. **The driver is fit for duty.** No alcohol, no fatigue, no impairing medication. This is a
   straightforward road-safety rule that matters more here than usual.
7. **Drive conservatively.** No hard braking, no sharp cornering, no speed. A grain that shifts and
   cracks in transit is a CATO waiting to be fired.
8. **Carry a fire extinguisher in the vehicle** — the same extinguisher that goes to the test site.
9. **Carry the authorisation paperwork** for the material.
10. **Plan the route.** Fewer stops, no unpaved roads where avoidable, no rough terrain at speed.

### 9.5 Notification and paperwork

Before any transport of propellant or oxidiser:

- [ ] **The Safety Officer knows** — what, how much, from where, to where, when, and who is travelling
- [ ] **Written authorisation** for the movement is carried in the vehicle
- [ ] **Purchase and stock registers** are updated
- [ ] **The destination is expecting the material** and someone will be there to receive it, store it
      correctly, and sign the register
- [ ] **Emergency information is to hand**: what the material is, how much, the emergency numbers, and
      the nearest hospital along the route
- [ ] **The legal position is understood.** Potassium nitrate is a **restricted oxidiser** in India under
      the **Explosives Act 1884 / Explosives Rules 2008**, administered by **PESO**, with possible
      state and district-level requirements. **The Safety Officer confirms the current position with the
      faculty advisor before any movement.** See `Safety_Manual_Master.md` §3.2.

### 9.6 Transport prohibitions

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Transport propellant in a way that could be mistaken for concealment** | Containers are labelled and visible; paperwork is carried. See `Safety_Manual_Master.md` §3.2 |
| **Transport above the quantity limits** | Quantity discipline |
| **Transport oxidiser and fuel in the same container** | That container is a propellant |
| **Leave propellant in an unattended vehicle** | Theft, heat, and collision risk |
| **Transport by public transport, bus, train, auto-rickshaw, or ride-hailing service** | No control over the environment, other passengers, or the driver's behaviour. **Never** |
| **Transport propellant together with passengers who are not authorised members** | Uninformed people cannot respond correctly to an emergency |
| **Transport without informing the Safety Officer** | The club's entire oversight chain depends on this |
| **Transport an unlabelled container** | Nobody knows what it is |
| **Transport a damaged or cracked grain "to see if it still works"** | It will not. It will CATO. **Scrap it at the source** |

## 10. Waste, cleaning, and housekeeping

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Residue is propellant** | Scrapings from the vessel, spills, sweepings, and discarded mixed powder are **propellant**. They are collected in a labelled sealed container and disposed of per §8.7 |

## 12. Absolute prohibitions — the complete list

**Any one of these is a stop-work event and a disciplinary matter.**

1. **Never work alone** with propellant, oxidiser, mixed powder, molten propellant, ignition powder, or
   a loaded motor. Two authorised persons minimum, always.
2. **Never grind, mill, or power-blend the mixed propellant.** Oxidiser and fuel are processed
   **separately**, always.
3. **Never grind oxidiser in equipment that has held fuel** without a full clean.
4. **Never work with any propellant other than 65/35 KNSB** without written authorisation from the
   Safety Committee and faculty advisor. **No perchlorates, no chlorates, no AP.**
5. **Never add anything to the melt** other than the authorised **0.1% SLES by mass**.
6. **Never use a heating vessel with an exposed element**, or a gas flame, or a microwave, or an
   uncontrolled heat source. **Thermostatically controlled, no exposed elements, only.**
7. **Never exceed 130 °C** on the melt. **Never add powder to a vessel that has not stabilised at
   temperature.**
8. **Never leave a melt unattended**, not for a second.
9. **Never cast without the three-item rule in place** — dunk bucket full, fire extinguisher in date,
   escape route walked and clear.
10. **Never work without the full required PPE**, including chemical splash goggles **and** face shield
    for casting.
11. **Never wear synthetic clothing** near propellant. Cotton or FR only.
12. **Never work tired, rushed, ill, or within 12 hours of consuming alcohol.**
13. **Never exceed the quantity limits** of §5.4 / §8.1.
14. **Never work outside the sanctioned location.** Never in a hostel, residence, or shared laboratory.
15. **Never store oxidiser and fuel together**, and never store propellant unlabelled or unlocked.
16. **Never transport propellant by public transport**, unattended, above the limit, or without
    informing the Safety Officer.
17. **Never skip the density check or the tap test** on any grain.
18. **Never use a grain that has been dropped, chipped, cracked, discoloured, or that has a soft or
    tacky surface.** Scrap it.
19. **Never fire a grain without the required thin primer** (once an authorised nitrate-based primer
    exists), and **never apply a heavy coat** — a heavy coat causes delayed ignition.
20. **Never bury, dump, or improvise the disposal** of propellant or waste.
21. **Never let schedule pressure, competition deadlines, or a senior member's impatience** justify
    skipping any step of this SOP.
22. **Never ignore a warning sign** — colour change, smoke, bubbling, unusual odour. **Step back, not
    in.**
