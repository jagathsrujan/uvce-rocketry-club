## 1. THE GOVERNING NUMBERS — reference box (memorise these)

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  FORMULATION           65% KNO₃ / 35% sorbitol BY MASS      Off-ratio burns unpredictably │
│  CASTING TEMPERATURE   120–130 °C                          Too cold: lumps and voids     │
│  TEMPERATURE TOLERANCE ± 5 °C                              Check every 2 minutes         │
│  AUTO-IGNITION         > 300 °C                            Ignites with no ignition source│
│  DECOMPOSITION ONSET   ~ 175 °C  (visible colour change)   FIRST WARNING                 │
│  SMOKING / BUBBLING    ~ 250 °C                            SECOND WARNING — EMERGENCY    │
│  IDEAL KNSB DENSITY    1.841 g/cm³                         Lower density = voids = CATO  │
│  SURFACTANT (SLES)     0.1% by mass of molten propellant   More than this = uncharacterised│
│  EXCESS ALLOWANCE      20–25% for small batches            A short batch = a partial mould│
│  CURE BEFORE DEMOULD   1–2 days                            Too soon = distorted grain    │
│  ENERGY CONTENT        ~2.7 kJ/g                           250 g = 675 kJ released in seconds│
│                                                                                          │
│  ⚠  THE 300 °C TOLERANCE IS A MARGIN OF SAFETY, NOT A LICENCE.                            │
│  ⚠  IT APPLIES TO ALREADY-MOLTEN SLURRY BEING GRADUALLY OVERHEATED.                        │
│  ⚠  NEVER USE A HEATING VESSEL WITH AN EXPOSED ELEMENT.                                    │
│  ⚠  NEVER EXCEED 130 °C ON THE MELT.                                                       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 9.1 Overheating warning signs — tick if observed, and act

| Observation | Meaning | Action | Observed? | Action taken |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colour shifting white/ivory → **yellow or amber** | Decomposition beginning (**~175 °C**) | Reduce heat immediately; check temperature; **do not continue without the Safety Officer's assessment** | ☐ | |
| **Greyish-amber** colour | Serious overheating (**~225 °C**) | Switch off; evacuate the immediate area; **batch is scrapped** | ☐ | |
| **Bubbling, puffs of smoke, strong burning-caramel odour** | Advanced decomposition (**~250 °C**) | **Evacuate. Withdraw to at least 10 m. Prepare for ignition. Do not move the vessel** | ☐ | |
| **Visible flame, hissing, bright light** | **IGNITION** | Turn face away; move away **at right angles** to the plume; do not retrieve anything; account for everyone; treat injuries | ☐ | |
| Any smell you do not recognise | Unknown | **Stop. Switch off. Investigate from a distance.** Do not put your face over the vessel | ☐ | |

## 13. Shut down and housekeeping

- [ ] Heating vessel switched off
- [ ] Vessel **not** cleaned while hot
- [ ] No metal tool used to scrape hot propellant residue
- [ ] Vessel cleaned only when cool; residue into a **labelled sealed waste container**
- [ ] Residue stored and disposed of **as propellant** — never washed down a drain, never in general waste
- [ ] Waste mass logged: ______ g
- [ ] Work area swept with a soft brush and damp-wiped (**never** a vacuum cleaner)
- [ ] No powder, residue, or unlabelled container left behind
- [ ] Grinder cleaned with a soft dry brush (**never water**) and stored sealed and labelled
- [ ] All containers labelled with contents, batch, mass, date, and responsible person

**Absolute prohibitions re-stated (Propellant_Handling_SOP.md §12):** never work alone · never grind,
mill, or power-blend mixed propellant · never work with any propellant other than 65/35 KNSB · never
add anything to the melt other than the authorised 0.1% SLES · never use a vessel with an exposed
element · never exceed 130 °C · never leave a melt unattended · never cast without the three-item rule ·
never work without full PPE · never wear synthetics · never work tired, rushed, ill, or within 12 hours
of alcohol · never exceed the quantity limits · never work outside the sanctioned location.
