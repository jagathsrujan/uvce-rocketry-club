## 4. On arrival at site

### 4.1 Site verification

- [ ] Site walked: **dimensions confirmed** — do not estimate, measure
- [ ] Recovery area clear; predicted landing point inside the site
- [ ] Access control confirmed; signs posted in **English and Kannada**
- [ ] Drift direction confirmed **INTO** the clear area (not out of it)
- [ ] Firing line established and **physically marked** (rope, tape, cones)
- [ ] Pad positioned **≥ 10 m** from the line; angle set and **LOCKED**; ground cleared for **3 m**; wetted
- [ ] Range Safety check: exclusion zone and downrange verified clear
- [ ] Fire cover at the pad: **≥ 2 × 20 L water**, extinguisher within **5 m**, fire watch assigned
- [ ] First aid kit and burn dressings at the line; nearest hospital and route known
- [ ] Written location sheet and emergency numbers posted
- [ ] Head count taken, by name
- [ ] Briefing delivered: range rules, countdown, abort calls, roles, emergency actions

### 4.2 Site re-verification — re-checked before every launch day

- [ ] Nobody has started living, working, or grazing on the land
- [ ] No new construction, excavation, or overhead line has appeared
- [ ] No harvesting activity bringing workers and machinery into the field
- [ ] Vegetation condition — still green and mown, or has it dried out since permission was obtained?
- [ ] Access control still works — the club can still stop people entering
- [ ] Recovery area still clear of people, crops, and obstacles
- [ ] Landowner's permission still valid, and the landowner notified of this launch day

### 5.1 Wind limits — quick reference

| Wind speed (sustained, at 2 m) | Action |
|---|---|
| **0–15 km/h** | **GO.** Ideal |
| **15–25 km/h** | **GO with caution.** Re-check the recovery area; expect significant drift |
| **25–32 km/h** | **CAUTION.** Only with a proven design, a large clear site, and Range Safety's explicit go. **No first flights, no new designs, no untested recovery systems** |
| **Above 32 km/h** | **NO-GO.** Cancel or wait |
| **Gusts more than 10 km/h above the sustained speed** | **NO-GO** regardless of the sustained value. **Gusts are what tip a rocket off the rod** |
| **Any wind that will carry the rocket outside the permitted site** | **NO-GO**, regardless of the measured speed |

> **Range Safety owns the weather decision, and it is a hard decision.** If the wind rises above the
> limit mid-session, **launching stops.** Not "one more". Not "this one's already on the pad".
> **Taking a rocket off the pad because the wind rose costs 5 minutes. Launching it costs whatever the
> wind decides.**

## 6. Man-limits and material-limits — recorded BEFORE the first launch

### 6.1 The two limits

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  MAN-LIMIT       No person shall be placed at risk. There is no acceptable number of        │
│                  casualties. A launch is prohibited if ANY person could be harmed —         │
│                  including people who have not consented, who do not know a launch is       │
│                  happening, or who have wandered into the area.                             │
│                                                                                             │
│                  THE MAN-LIMIT IS ABSOLUTE AND IT IS ZERO.                                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  MATERIAL-LIMIT  There is a limit to the property damage that is acceptable, and it is      │
│                  expressed IN ADVANCE. Losing the rocket is acceptable — that is a cost     │
│                  the club has budgeted. Damaging the landowner's fence, crop, vehicle, or   │
│                  building is NOT acceptable, and neither is creating a liability or a fire. │
│                                                                                             │
│                  THE MATERIAL-LIMIT IS DEFINED BEFORE THE LAUNCH, in the site assessment,   │
│                  expressed as: WHAT IS THE WORST THING THIS ROCKET CAN HIT, AND IS THAT     │
│                  ACCEPTABLE?                                                                │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 6.3 The failure modes planned for

| Failure mode | Where it goes | Planned for? |
|---|---|---|
| **CATO on the pad** | Fragments radially, motor hardware in all directions | ☐ Firing line and spectators outside the pad fragment radius |
| **Rod-exit instability / weathercocking** | Rocket turns into the wind, flies low and fast, unpredictably — **the most common serious flight failure** | ☐ Entire low-angle sector treated as at risk |
| **Premature ejection at high speed** | Vehicle breaks up; parts fall from altitude | ☐ Whole cone plus the area under the ascent path |
| **No ejection / lawn dart** | Ballistic, fast, penetrates — **a ballistic impact is lethal** | ☐ **The man-limit is the only protection** |
| **Parachute failure / tangled chute** | High descent rate | ☐ Cone verified empty |
| **Long drift under chute** | Downwind, potentially far outside the planned area | ☐ Wind assessment and downwind check done |
| **Motor failure mid-burn** | Unpredictable, low | ☐ Whole cone |

> **The lawn-dart case is the one that decides the site.** There is no mitigation for it other than a
> site where the impact area is empty. **If the impact area is not empty, do not fly.**

## 8. Countdown protocol — reference, called aloud by the Launch Control Officer

| Time | Call | Expected response / action | Heard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T−5 min** | "FIVE MINUTES TO LAUNCH. RANGE STATUS?" | Range Safety: "RANGE CLEAR" / "RANGE NOT CLEAR" | ☐ |
| **T−3 min** | "THREE MINUTES. PAD IS ARMED?" | Arming officer confirms the igniter is installed, leads shunted | ☐ |
| **T−2 min** | "TWO MINUTES. WEATHER CHECK." | Range Safety reports wind speed, direction, gusts. **Any limit exceeded = HOLD** | ☐ |
| **T−1 min** | "ONE MINUTE. ALL PERSONNEL BEHIND THE LINE. HEADS COUNTED." | Safety Officer confirms the head count by name | ☐ |
| **T−30 s** | "THIRTY SECONDS. CAMERAS RUNNING. SKY CLEAR." | Documentarian confirms recording | ☐ |
| **T−20 s** | "TWENTY SECONDS. ARMING." | LCO removes the shunt and connects the leads. **Announces: "LEADS CONNECTED, SHUNT REMOVED"** | ☐ |
| **T−15 s** | "FIFTEEN SECONDS. SYSTEM ARMED." | Arm switch to ARM. **Second person confirms the ARMED indicator** | ☐ |
| **T−10** | **"TEN"** | Silence from everyone. **Nobody moves** | ☐ |
| **T−9 to T−6** | "NINE… EIGHT… SEVEN… SIX…" | — | ☐ |
| **T−5** | **"FIVE — RANGE CLEAR"** | Range Safety confirms once more | ☐ |
| **T−4, T−3, T−2** | "FOUR… THREE… TWO…" | — | ☐ |
| **T−1** | **"ONE"** | — | ☐ |
| **T−0** | **"LAUNCH"** — or **"IGNITION"** | **Press and hold the launch button** | ☐ |

**Second person who confirmed the ARMED indicator:** ______________________

**Abort calls:** **"ABORT"** or **"HOLD"** — **anyone may call it, at any time, for any reason.** On
hearing it: **stop the count, switch to SAFE, remove the key, disconnect the battery, announce "SYSTEM
SAFE" — and hold.** Nobody approaches the pad until the Safety Officer authorises it and the wait in
Section 10 has elapsed. **There is no penalty for calling an abort. There is a penalty for not calling
one.**

## 9. Range clearance protocol and post-flight recovery

### 9.1 Range clearance — the verbal protocol

> **Only the Safety Officer (or the designated Range Safety) may declare the range clear, and the
> declaration is made out loud, every time, before every arming.**
> **"RANGE IS CLEAR" is never assumed, delegated to silence, or inferred from the fact that nobody has
> said otherwise.**

| Call | Meaning | Who says it | Used today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"RANGE IS CLEAR"** | I have physically verified that nobody and nothing is at the pad, in the exclusion zone, or in the impact cone, and it is safe to arm | Safety Officer / Range Safety | ☐ |
| **"RANGE IS NOT CLEAR"** | Someone or something is in the range, or I cannot verify it is clear. **Arming does not proceed** | **Anyone** | ☐ |
| **"ABORT" / "HOLD"** | Stop the count immediately | **Anyone** | ☐ |
| **"SYSTEM SAFE"** | Arm switch to SAFE, key removed, battery disconnected | Launch Control Officer | ☐ |
| **"ALL CLEAR"** | The flight is complete, the rocket has landed, the system is safe, and the range may be re-entered | Safety Officer | ☐ |

## 10. Misfire and hang-fire — reference box

> ### 🔴 THE CRITICAL RULE
> **DISCONNECT THE IGNITER LEADS FIRST — before touching anything else.**
> A rocket sitting on a pad with an igniter installed is a **loaded device**. It may fire at any moment.

**Definitions:** **Misfire** = the launch command was given and **nothing happened**. **Hang-fire** =
there was **some** response (smoke, a spark, a fizz, a partial ignition) but the rocket did not lift or
lifted weakly. **A hang-fire is more dangerous than a misfire**, because it proves the ignition circuit
works and that something is burning.

**Phase 1 — make safe, without moving:** hold the launch button for 5 seconds past the expected ignition
time; if nothing after **5 seconds**, call **"MISFIRE"** loudly; release the button; **switch to SAFE**;
**remove the key** into your pocket; **disconnect the battery**; announce **"SYSTEM SAFE. HOLDING.
NOBODY MOVES. NOBODY APPROACHES THE PAD."**; everyone stays behind the firing line.

**Phase 2 — wait — minimum times, not targets:**

| Situation | Minimum wait before approach |
|---|---|
| Clean misfire — no smoke, no sound, no heat, no visible response | **60 seconds** model rocket; **5 minutes** experimental or above D class |
| Hang-fire — smoke, spark, fizz, partial burn, or any response at all | **5 minutes** model rocket; **15 minutes** experimental |
| Partial ignition — the motor burned but did not lift | **15 minutes**, and the vehicle is treated as a live energetic device |
| Anything unexplained | **30 minutes**, and the Safety Officer decides. **When in doubt, wait longer — then again** |

**Phase 3 — approach, only after the wait and only with the Safety Officer:** Safety Officer authorises
aloud; two persons minimum, Safety Officer leads; **approach from the side**, never in front of the nose
or behind the nozzle; walk, do not run; **DISCONNECT THE IGNITER LEADS FIRST** (box end first if the run
allows), **then disconnect the igniter from the launch leads**, **then shunt the leads**, **then** touch
the rocket; **never tug on the igniter wires**; feel for heat before touching the motor; remove the
rocket only when the system is safe, the igniter disconnected and shunted, and the motor is cool;
**record everything before disturbing it**.

**Additional hang-fire rules:** treat the vehicle as a live energetic device until the motor is confirmed
cold and the igniter removed · do not force a partially burned motor out of the vehicle · **the motor is
retired after a hang-fire or partial ignition and is not re-flown** · the vehicle is inspected for heat
damage before it flies again · the cause is investigated on the bench, not at the pad.

## 11. NO-GO criteria for flight operations — quick reference

**Any single item below disqualifies the flight. There is no vote and no override.**

1. Any person, animal, or vehicle in the impact cone or the exclusion zone
2. A member of the public who has not been briefed, within the minimum distance
3. The landowner's permission is not on site, has expired, or has been withdrawn
4. The landowner has not been notified for today
5. Any person at the pad after the igniter is installed
6. The head count is not complete — someone is unaccounted for
7. Wind above limits — sustained > 32 km/h, gusts > 10 km/h above sustained, or any wind that carries the rocket outside the permitted site
8. Wind direction carrying the drift outside the clear area
9. Lightning within 10 km, thunder heard in the last 30 minutes, or a thunderstorm approaching
10. Any rain, or wet ground at the pad
11. Cloud ceiling below 2× the expected apogee
12. Less than 90 minutes of daylight remaining
13. Local fire-danger warning in force, or dry vegetation in the impact area in summer without a fire watch and extra water
14. No fire cover at the pad — no water, no extinguisher, no fire watch
15. No first aid kit, or no burn dressings, or no eyewash
16. Aircraft, helicopter, drone, kite, or large bird flock in the sky over the area
17. Within 5 km of an airport, airstrip, helipad, or drone corridor
18. Sight of a defence installation, dam, port, or major power infrastructure
19. Igniter leads not shunted, or the launching circuit not verified
20. Ignition box key not on the Launch Control Officer's person
21. Continuity check not performed, or performed in a way that could deliver firing current
22. Battery voltage low or unverified
23. Rocket unmarked — no club name, ID, or contact number
24. Rocket damaged — cracked fin, deformed body, damaged nose cone, degraded recovery system
25. Recovery system not packed, not verified, or of untested design on a first flight
26. Launch angle not set, not locked, or not verified
27. Pad unstable, or the rod/rail bent or too short for the vehicle
28. Firing line not established, not marked, or people in front of it
29. Any person on site without eye protection
30. Any person on site who has not been briefed on the range rules, countdown, and abort calls
31. Phones in hand during arming, countdown, or flight
32. Any person unfit for duty — fatigued, ill, impaired
33. **Fewer than two people present. Nobody launches alone**
34. A member has raised a safety concern that has not been resolved to their satisfaction
35. Any unexplained anomaly — a component that does not look right, a measurement that does not match, a change nobody can account for
36. **Schedule pressure being used as a reason to proceed**

> **Item 36 causes all the others.** A rushed launch is a launch with no downrange check, a guessed
> wind, an un-shunted igniter, and a rocket nobody counted. **If you are rushing, stop.**

### 12.3 Incident and near-miss reporting

> **Near misses must be reported with the same seriousness as incidents.** A near miss is the most
> valuable data the club has — it is a free lesson. **Hiding one is a serious disciplinary matter.**

| Category | Definition | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Incident** | Any injury, any fire, any unplanned ignition, any motor case failure (CATO), any property damage, any emergency-services call | **Immediately** to the Safety Officer, written report **within 24 hours** |
| **Near miss** | Any event that could have caused injury or damage but did not — a dropped grain, a spill, a stray-current indication, an un-shunted lead discovered, a person found downrange, an extinguisher found empty, an abort called | Written report **within 48 hours**, no matter how trivial it seemed |
| **Anomaly** | A flight that produced unexpected data — off-nominal performance, early/late ejection, recovery failure | Written note in the flight report, flagged at the next club meeting |
| **Equipment defect** | Failed PPE, expired extinguisher, damaged grounding strap, faulty thermometer | Report to the Safety Officer immediately; equipment removed from service and tagged |
