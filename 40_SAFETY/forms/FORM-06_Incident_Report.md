> ### READ THIS FIRST
>
> ## NEAR MISSES MUST BE REPORTED WITH THE SAME SERIOUSNESS AS INCIDENTS.
>
> A near miss is **any event that could have caused injury or damage but did not** — a dropped grain, a
> spill, a stray-current indication, an un-shunted lead discovered, a person found downrange, an
> extinguisher found empty, an abort called. **No matter how trivial it seemed.**
>
> **Near misses are the most valuable data the club has.** They are free lessons — the accident you
> prevent by reporting a near miss is the one that would have injured the next member.
>
> **Hiding one — through embarrassment, or through a belief that it was trivial — is a serious
> disciplinary matter** (`Safety_Manual_Master.md` §9).
>
> **The purpose of this report is to find causes, not to find someone to blame. Blame produces silence;
> silence produces the next accident.**

## 6. Contributing factors

**Tick every factor that applied, and add detail.** Almost every incident has **more than one** cause.
**Look for the second cause.**

### 6.1 Human factors

- [ ] Fatigue or insufficient sleep (less than 6 hours, or more than 10 hours on feet)
- [ ] Time pressure, a hard deadline, or competition pressure
- [ ] Distraction — phone, conversation, another task running in parallel
- [ ] Inexperience with this specific task
- [ ] Over-familiarity / complacency with a routine task ("the boring 40th time")
- [ ] A safety concern was raised and **not** resolved to the raiser's satisfaction
- [ ] Someone was pressured, mocked, or discouraged from calling an abort or a stop-work
- [ ] Impairment — alcohol, recreational drugs, or impairing medication
- [ ] Illness, injury, or a physical limitation affecting the task
- [ ] A step was skipped, or performed from memory instead of from the procedure
- [ ] Lone working, or the second person was not actively watching
- [ ] Communication failure — an instruction was not heard, not understood, or not acknowledged

## 10. Root cause analysis — the 5-Whys method

> **Ask "why" at least five times.** Stop only when you reach something **you can change** — a procedure,
> a training gap, a piece of equipment, or a schedule pressure. If your fifth "why" is still a person's
> name, you have not finished.
>
> **Investigate the procedure, not just the person.** If a competent, trained member made a mistake, the
> procedure is at fault for allowing it.
>
> Worked example from the manual: *"The igniter did not fire" → "the circuit was open" → "the shunt was
> not removed" → "the checklist was not read" → "we were running late and skipped it" → **the real cause
> is the schedule pressure, not the person holding the wires.***

| Level | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **WHY 1** — What happened? (the observed event) | Why did it happen? | |
| **WHY 2** | Why did *that* happen? | |
| **WHY 3** | Why did *that* happen? | |
| **WHY 4** | Why did *that* happen? | |
| **WHY 5** | Why did *that* happen? (**the root cause — something changeable**) | |

## 13. Quick reference — what must be reported, and by when

| Category | Definition | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Incident** | Any injury, any fire, any unplanned ignition, any motor case failure (CATO), any property damage, any emergency-services call | **Immediately** to the Safety Officer; written report **within 24 hours** |
| **Near miss** | Any event that could have caused injury or damage but did not — a dropped grain, a spill, a stray-current indication, an un-shunted lead discovered, a person found downrange, an extinguisher found empty, an abort called | Written report **within 48 hours**, no matter how trivial it seemed |
| **Anomaly** | A test or flight that produced unexpected data — off-nominal pressure curve, early/late ignition, unexpected nozzle erosion, a grain defect found after the fact | Written note in the test report, and flagged at the next club meeting |
| **Equipment defect** | Failed PPE, expired extinguisher, damaged grounding strap, faulty thermometer | Report to the Safety Officer immediately; equipment removed from service and tagged |

**Retention:** incident and near-miss reports are retained **permanently**, with a copy to the faculty
advisor. **These survive member graduation — that is the point.**
