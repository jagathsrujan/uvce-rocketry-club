# UVCE Rocketry Club — Static Test Procedure

**Document:** `40_SAFETY/Static_Test_Procedure.md`
**Parent document:** `40_SAFETY/Safety_Manual_Master.md`
**Applies to:** every static firing of an experimental solid rocket motor, at every scale, including
"quick" tests of a "known-good" design

> **There is no such thing as a quick static test.** A static test is the moment when the largest
> amount of stored energy the club possesses is released inside hardware the club built. The fact that
> it is bolted to a stand rather than flying changes the direction of the fragments, not the energy.

---

## 1. The thirteen things that must be true before you light anything

If any one of these is false, the test does not proceed. Not "proceeds with caution". Does not proceed.

1. A **written test plan** exists, is signed by the Safety Officer and Test Director, and has been
   briefed to every person present.
2. The **motor is the motor that was analysed** — same case, same grain geometry, same nozzle, same
   propellant batch, and documented as such. **No undocumented changes.**
3. The **case has been proof-tested** hydrostatically to **1.2 × MEOP** (see §3.6).
4. The grain has **passed the density check and the tap test** (`Propellant_Handling_SOP.md` §7.3).
5. The **igniter is sized to the motor** and its charge is recorded.
6. **Distance and cover** meet §2. **75–100 m minimum for a proven motor; 150 m or more for a new
   design.**
7. **Range Safety has declared the range clear** — out loud, to the Test Director.
8. The **three-item rule** is satisfied at the pad: dunk bucket, fire extinguisher, escape route.
9. **Fire cover is present**, correct and in date — at the pad and at the firing point.
10. **Every person is accounted for** at the firing point or the observation point. **Nobody downrange.**
11. The **igniter leads are shunted**, and will remain shunted until the moment of connection.
12. **Data capture and video are running and synced**, before arming.
13. **Weather is within limits** — §7.5.

## 2. Site requirements and minimum safe distances

### 2.1 Why distance is the primary safety assurance

> **Nakka positions himself and all others 250–300 ft (75–100 m) away for *proven* motors, and even
> farther for new designs.**

Understand *why* before accepting the number. In a motor case failure:

- The case ruptures and becomes **fragments travelling at hundreds of metres per second**. A steel or
  aluminium fragment at that speed is lethal at tens of metres and dangerous at over a hundred.
- The **nozzle may be ejected** and travels laterally, not along the thrust axis — so standing "behind"
  the motor is not safe.
- The **flame plume** from a ruptured case is several metres long and radiates intense heat.
- The **pressure wave** alone can rupture eardrums and cause lung injury at close range.

**You cannot predict the moment of failure, and you cannot outrun it.** Distance and hard cover are the
only protections that work regardless of what goes wrong. Every other safety measure in this document
is a layer; **distance is the foundation.**

**Distance is therefore the one thing that is never traded for convenience.** If the site does not
allow the minimum distance, the site is wrong — not the distance.

### 2.2 Minimum safe distances

| Motor status | Minimum distance (personnel) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Proven motor** (≥ 3 successful firings of the *identical* design, same batch recipe) | **75 m** absolute minimum, **100 m** club standard | 100 m is the club's working standard even for proven motors. The firing cable is 100 m for this reason |
| **New design, new case, new nozzle geometry, or new propellant batch** | **150 m or more** | There is no heritage to reason from. Assume the worst |
| **Any motor above 200 N·s impulse** | **Scale with propellant mass** — minimum **150 m**, and increase further as mass rises | A larger motor stores more energy and throws fragments further |
| **Observer / visitor viewing point** | **At or beyond the personnel minimum**, behind the firing line, never inside the exclusion zone | See §2.4 |

**These are minimums.** If the site allows more, take more. There is no penalty for being further away
and a great deal of penalty for the opposite.

### 2.3 Site characteristics

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Clear radius around the pad** | **50 m minimum** clear of dry vegetation, combustible material, structures, and parked vehicles |
| **No dry grass** | **In summer, dry grass is a fire-spread path that can outrun you.** The ground within 25 m of the pad is cleared, mown, or wetted. If the surrounding land is dry, the test does not proceed without a fire watch and additional water |
| **Backstop** | An **earth berm or a solid wall** behind the motor, at least as tall as the motor axis, to catch fragments and deflect the plume. **No line of sight** from the firing point or any public area to the motor |
| **No line of sight from public areas** | Roads, footpaths, buildings, farmland with workers, and railways must not have a direct view of the pad. **If a member of the public can wander into the blast fan, the site is wrong** |
| **Overhead** | No overhead power lines, no tree canopy, no structures above the motor |
| **Underground** | No buried services (water, gas, electrical, fibre) within 25 m of the pad. **Verify with the landowner before choosing the pad position** |
| **Firing point** | **Hard cover**: a trench, an earth berm, a concrete wall, or the far side of a vehicle's engine block. **Behind the cover, not beside it.** A person standing in the open at 100 m is less safe than a person behind a berm at 50 m |
| **Access control** | **A single controlled entry point**, or a perimeter that Range Safety can physically walk and verify clear before each arming. Fences, gates, natural boundaries. If people can wander in, a person is posted |
| **Escape routes** | **Two directions of retreat** from the pad, walked and confirmed clear. **Not through the blast fan, not through dry grass, not over a fence** |
| **Water** | A bulk water supply (a bowser or multiple 20 L drums) **at the pad**, not only at the firing point |
| **Communications** | Everyone within earshot of a shout, horn, or radio. **If the site exceeds shouting range, radios or an air horn are mandatory** |
| **Mobile signal** | **Verified at the site, before the test.** If there is no signal, a vehicle must be available to drive to a point where there is |
| **Nearest hospital** | Identified, its distance and route known, and **written on the site sheet** |
| **Written landowner permission** | **On paper, signed, with a validity date.** Carried on site. The IN-SPACe advisory checklist explicitly requires this |

### 2.4 Zones

| Zone | Radius | Who may be there |
|---|---|---|
| **Pad zone** | 5 m around the motor | Nobody, once the motor is mounted. Only the arming team, only before arming, never after |
| **Buffer / exclusion zone** | 25 m around the pad | Nobody, at any time after the igniter is installed. Marked with signs and tape |
| **Control point (firing point)** | At the minimum personnel distance (§2.2) | Test Director, Safety Officer, data operator, documentarian. **Behind hard cover** |
| **Observation point** | At or beyond the personnel minimum, behind the firing line | Everyone else, with PPE, briefed, and **counted** |
| **Muster point** | Fixed, upwind, with a clear route out | Everyone, in an emergency. **Nobody leaves it until the Safety Officer says so, by name** |

**Every person on site is inside one of these zones and is counted by Range Safety.** "I thought he
was with you" is a fatality.

## 3. Test stand setup and motor mounting

### 3.1 Test stand requirements

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Rigidity** | The stand must not flex, resonate, or shift under thrust. **A stand that moves gives you thrust data that is a measurement of the stand, not the motor** |
| **Anchoring** | Staked, bolted, or ballasted. **Ballast with sandbags totalling at least 5× the expected peak thrust**, or ground anchors rated to the same. The stand must not move — a motor that breaks loose becomes a missile |
| **Thrust direction** | **Horizontal firing into a berm or deflector**, or **vertical firing downward into a deflector pit**. Horizontal is easier and standard at our scale |
| **Deflector** | A steel or concrete deflector plate at **45°** in front of the nozzle, to turn the plume upward or sideways away from the stand and the firing point. **The deflector must be anchored** — an unanchored plate becomes a projectile |
| **Clearance** | **Minimum 1 m** of clear space around the motor for plume expansion. The plume must not impinge on the stand structure, the load cell, or the pressure line |
| **Material** | Steel for the frame. **No flammable material, no plastic, no timber closer than 1 m to the motor** |
| **Cable routing** | All cables (igniter, load cell, pressure transducer, thermocouples) routed **away from the plume path**, secured, and protected from radiant heat |
| **Heat shielding** | Load cell and transducer cables shielded with a thermal barrier or routed behind the deflector |

### 3.2 Motor mounting

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Inspect the **case** for damage, corrosion, deformation, or previous bulging. **Any case with a history of an off-nominal firing is retired, not reused** |
| 2 | Inspect the **nozzle** — throat diameter measured and recorded, no cracks, no erosion beyond the design limit |
| 3 | Install the **grain** per the design — freestanding, correctly oriented, with the design inter-segment spacing (10–20% of grain diameter for multi-segment grains) and **O-ring spacers where specified** |
| 4 | Verify the **port-to-throat area ratio ≥ 2** at the start of the burn. **KNSB is prone to negative erosive burning** — this ratio is a safety limit, not a performance preference |
| 5 | Install **O-rings and seals** — new or verified, correctly seated, lubricated with silicone grease only |
| 6 | Fit the **retaining system** (head end and nozzle end). Verify the **safety bolts / retaining ring** are correctly torqued and, where specified, **shear-pin sized** |
| 7 | **Record every measurement**: case, grain mass, grain dimensions, core diameter, throat diameter, port/throat ratio, O-ring part numbers, torque values |
| 8 | Mount the motor in the stand, **aligned with the thrust axis** of the load cell |
| 9 | Verify the motor **cannot rotate** in the mount |
| 10 | **Photograph the mounted motor** from side-on and both ends, before the igniter is installed |

### 3.3 Thrust measurement

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Load cell** | Sized so that **peak expected thrust is 50–80% of full scale**. An oversized load cell loses resolution; an undersized one is destroyed |
| **Mounting** | In line with the motor thrust axis, or on a lever arm with the ratio documented and the calibration accounting for it |
| **Calibration** | **Verified immediately before the test with known masses or a certified proving ring.** Record the before-and-after calibration. A measurement you have not calibrated is not a measurement |
| **Sample rate** | **≥ 100 Hz minimum; 1000 Hz preferred.** A 2-second burn at 100 Hz is only 200 samples — **sample fast, always** |
| **Recording** | To a device that cannot be disturbed by the firing. **Micro-SD logging plus a live laptop display where possible.** Both, not one |
| **Protection** | Cables shielded from radiant heat and routed clear of the plume |

### 3.4 Pressure measurement

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Transducer** | Rated to at least **2× the expected MEOP**. A transducer that saturates tells you nothing |
| **Thermal protection** | **Mandatory.** A grease-filled standpipe, a thermal barrier, or a standoff tube. **A transducer exposed directly to combustion gas is destroyed within milliseconds** |
| **Mounting** | At the head end (forward closure), ported to the chamber, sealed, and mechanically secured against blowout |
| **Calibration** | Verified with a certified pressure gauge or deadweight tester before the test. **Record it** |
| **Sample rate** | Same as thrust — **≥ 100 Hz**, preferably 1000 Hz |
| **Why it matters most** | **The chamber pressure trace is the single most diagnostic measurement in the entire test.** A mountain-shaped curve means delayed end-face ignition. A spike means an oversized igniter. A slow rise means a reluctant start. A rapid rise to failure is the last thing the sensor ever records — and it tells us what to change |

### 3.5 Igniter installation

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Confirm the igniter charge mass** and that it is sized to the motor. **An oversized igniter causes a startup pressure spike that can shear the retaining bolts and burst the case** |
| 2 | Verify the igniter **cannot plug the throat** |
| 3 | Verify the igniter is positioned to ignite the **core and the forward end face** |
| 4 | **Keep the igniter leads SHUNTED** — twisted together — until the moment of connection |
| 5 | Route the igniter leads **out of the plume path** and secure them so they cannot be pulled or vibrate loose |
| 6 | **Do not connect the igniter to the firing cable yet.** Connect it last, after everyone is at distance |
| 7 | Record the igniter type, charge mass, and construction |

### 3.6 Hydrostatic proof test — required for every new motor design

Nakka's rule, and a club requirement:

1. **Fill a completed, sealed motor with water.** Bleed all air — this is the important part, because
   **trapped air stores energy that water does not**.
2. **Pressurise to 1.2 × MEOP** with a grease gun or hydraulic jack fitted with a pressure gauge.
3. **Place a plywood shield between the motor and the operator.** Compressed water stores little
   energy, but trapped air does not forgive.
4. **Hold the pressure for a minimum of 60 seconds**, and inspect for weeping, deformation, or leakage
   from a distance.
5. **Record the pressure, hold time, and observations.**
6. **Any permanent deformation, leak, or failure = the case is rejected.** No repair. No "it's probably
   fine".
7. **Design context:** the case should burst at **no more than 2.5 × MEOP**. **A minimum safety margin
   of 1.5 is appropriate** for motor design. **Avoid "boilerplate" thick-walled cases** — they store an
   incredible amount of energy before bursting and **increase** the severity of a failure.

## 4. Remote ignition system

### 4.1 Why remote

**You are not near the motor when it fires.** Not behind a shield 3 m away, not "just to the side".
You are at the minimum personnel distance of §2.2, behind hard cover. **There is no version of this
where a person stands near the motor with a battery and a switch.**

### 4.2 Cable length

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Firing cable length** | **100 m minimum** (the club's standard club-owned reel). **Longer for new designs and larger motors** |
| **Cable specification** | **2-core copper, 1.5 mm² minimum.** Thin cable over 100 m loses voltage and gives unreliable ignition |
| **Cable condition** | **Continuity tested end-to-end before every session.** A cable with a hidden break means a misfire you will then have to walk up to |
| **Routing** | Away from the plume path, away from foot traffic, **not across the escape route**, secured against being pulled |
| **Spare cable** | Carry a spare. A damaged cable discovered after everyone is in position costs the test |

### 4.3 The ignition box and the truth about the Safe/Arm switch

**The ignition box must have built-in safety checks ensuring that no current can inadvertently reach
the igniter.** Minimum features:

| Feature | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Removable key switch** | Physically breaks the arming circuit. **The key is held on the Test Director's person — never left in the box, never on a lanyard by the box, never "on the table"** |
| **SAFE / ARM selector** | Two-position, detented, clearly labelled, with a **visible and preferably audible indication** of ARMED state |
| **Momentary-contact FIRE button** | Spring-return, so the circuit cannot be left energised |
| **Continuity check with current limited below 50 mA** | Verifies the igniter circuit without delivering enough current to fire the bridgewire. **A continuity check that can fire the igniter is not a safety feature — it is a hazard** |
| **Visible "ARMED" indicator** (LED and/or buzzer) | So that anyone looking at the box knows its state |
| **Series redundancy** | Key switch **and** arm switch **and** fire button in series. **Any one open = no current** |
| **Battery state indication** | A flat battery means a misfire and an approach |
| **Enclosure** | Rigid, closed, with no exposed terminals when in the SAFE state |

> ### ⚠️ A SAFE/ARM SWITCH IS ONLY *PARTIAL* SECURITY
>
> Nakka's wording, and the club's position: **a Safe/Arm switch represents only partial security.**
> It is **one layer**, and it is not sufficient on its own. Treat it as though it might fail, because
> it might — through a wiring fault, a stuck contact, an induced current, a misread switch position, or
> simple human error.
>
> **The complete defence is layered:**
> 1. **Shunted igniter leads**, twisted together, removed only at the moment of connection —
>    *this is the layer that actually protects you, and it is the one people skip*
> 2. **Physical disconnection of the power source** when the system is not in use
> 3. **Key removed and on the Test Director's person**
> 4. **Safe/Arm switch in series with the key and the fire button**
> 5. **Redundant series elements**, so no single failure arms the system
> 6. **Continuity check current-limited below 50 mA**
> 7. **Distance and hard cover** — so that even a total failure of every electrical safeguard, you are
>    not next to the motor
> 8. **The igniter connected last**, after everyone is at distance, as the final step
>
> **A member who relies on the Safe/Arm switch alone is relying on the weakest layer of an eight-layer
> defence.** That is a stop-work finding.

### 4.4 Relay / MOSFET — why it is used

The firing cable carries the **control** signal, not the igniter current. The high-current igniter
circuit is **local to the pad**, switched by a **relay or a MOSFET**.

**Why this matters:**

- **A low-current control line over 100 m loses far less voltage** than a high-current line, so the
  igniter gets the current it needs.
- **A relay gives positive galvanic isolation** when open — the igniter is genuinely disconnected from
  the firing line, not merely switched off by a semiconductor that may leak.
- **A relay that is open cannot be closed by an induced current in the control line** at the levels
  present in a field environment.
- **A relay audibly clicks.** That click is a real, physical confirmation of state that you can hear
  from the firing point.

**Specify the relay or MOSFET to carry at least 2× the igniter current**, and mount it at the pad end,
protected from the plume, dust, and moisture.

### 4.5 Shunting — the rule that actually saves lives

> **Igniter leads are SHUNTED — twisted together — at all times, until the moment of connection.**
> *(Nakka safety rule 6)*

**Shunting = twisting the bared ends of the igniter leads together** so that any stray current —
induced from nearby wiring, static discharge, or a fault in the firing box — flows through the short
circuit instead of through the bridgewire.

**The rules:**

1. **Every igniter is shunted the moment it is built.** Not later. Not after testing. Immediately.
2. **The shunt stays on during storage, transport, mounting, and setup.**
3. **The shunt is removed only at the moment of connection to the firing cable** — and that connection
   is the **last** step before everyone withdraws.
4. **If you stop for any reason — a break, a question, a phone call, a weather check — the leads are
   re-shunted and the firing cable is disconnected.**
5. **If a shunt is found off and nobody knows why, the test stops.** Re-shunt, re-verify the circuit,
   restart the arming sequence.
6. **This applies equally to parachute ejection charges and any other pyrotechnic device.**

**A shunt costs two seconds.** There is no argument against it, and a member who skips it is removed
from energetic work (`Safety_Manual_Master.md` §9.2).

## 6. Misfire procedure

> **The motor did not fire. Your instinct is to go and look. That instinct kills people.**

### 6.1 Immediate actions (do not move from cover)

1. **Do not release the FIRE button early and do not press it again.** Hold for the full expected burn
   plus 5 seconds, then release.
2. If there is no ignition within **5 seconds of the fire command**, call **"MISFIRE"** — loudly, so
   everyone hears.
3. **Switch to SAFE.**
4. **Remove the key** and put it in your pocket.
5. **Disconnect the firing-circuit battery** at the box.
6. **Announce: "SYSTEM SAFE. HOLDING FOR FIVE MINUTES. NOBODY MOVES."**
7. **Everyone stays behind cover.** Nobody stands up to "see better".

### 6.2 The waiting periods

| Situation | Minimum wait before approach |
|---|---|
| Igniter did not initiate; motor clearly unburned, no smoke, no heat | **5 minutes** |
| Motor smoked, sparked, puffed, or partially burned, then stopped | **15 minutes** |
| Motor ignited and burned, and appears to have finished | **10 minutes**, then follow Phase F |
| Anything you do not understand — unusual noise, smell, smoke, or a partial burn | **30 minutes**, and the Safety Officer decides. **If in doubt, wait longer and then wait again** |

**Why the wait is not negotiable:** a slow-burning igniter, a smouldering grain, or a delayed
pyrotechnic reaction can initiate late. Heat-soaked hardware can reignite propellant when air reaches
it. **The wait costs minutes. The alternative costs hands.**

### 6.3 The approach — the order of operations

**Two persons minimum. Safety Officer leads.**

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Safety Officer announces the approach, out loud, so everyone knows |
| 2 | **Approach from the side** — never in front of the nozzle, never directly behind the case head |
| 3 | **Walk. Do not run.** Watch where you step — debris may be hot or sharp |
| 4 | **DISCONNECT THE IGNITER LEADS FIRST — BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE.** Start at the control-point end if the run is long |
| 5 | **Then disconnect the igniter from the motor** |
| 6 | **Immediately SHUNT the igniter leads** (twist the bared ends together) once they are free |
| 7 | **Only then** touch or inspect anything else |
| 8 | **Never tug on the igniter wires.** A tug can move the igniter inside the grain or mechanically disturb a hot grain |
| 9 | Feel for heat before touching the motor. If it is warm, **back off and keep waiting** |
| 10 | Inspect the igniter: did the bridgewire burn through? Is the igniter intact? Is it still in position? |
| 11 | **Record everything** before disturbing the motor: igniter condition, position, motor temperature, time elapsed |

### 6.4 Re-firing after a misfire

- **A re-fire is a new test, not a continuation.** The Test Director must explicitly authorise it, and
  the full arming sequence of §5 Phase B–D is repeated from the beginning.
- **The motor must be verified cold and undamaged** before any re-fire is considered.
- **The igniter is replaced**, never reused, and never "topped up" with more powder.
- **If the cause of the misfire is not understood, there is no re-fire.** Diagnose it on the bench,
  after the session, not at the pad.
- **Two consecutive misfires on the same motor = the motor is retired** pending investigation.

### 6.5 Misfire prohibitions

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Approach before the minimum wait** | A late initiation while your hand is on the motor |
| **Touch the igniter before disconnecting the leads** | The leads are the connection to the power source. **Disconnect first, always** |
| **Send someone to "just have a quick look"** | Same as approaching. The wait applies to everyone |
| **Assume the motor is dead because it looks dead** | Heat-soaked hardware reignites. **Time, not appearance, is the criterion** |
| **Re-fire without a documented cause for the misfire** | You will repeat it, possibly with a partially-burned grain of unknown geometry |
| **Try to pull a partially burned grain out of a case** | It is live propellant. Treat it as such |
| **Skip logging the misfire** | Misfires are data and warnings. Hiding one is a disciplinary matter |

## 7. Data capture, weather limits, and NO-GO criteria

### 7.1 Data capture

| Instrument | Requirement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Load cell** | Calibrated with known masses immediately before the test. **≥ 100 Hz**, 1000 Hz preferred | Total impulse is the **area under the thrust-time curve**. Without it you do not know what your motor did |
| **Pressure transducer** | Thermally protected (grease-filled standpipe or barrier). Calibrated. **≥ 100 Hz** | The pressure trace is the most diagnostic single measurement in the test |
| **High-speed or slow-motion video** | **240 fps minimum; 1000 fps preferred.** Framing verified before arming | The burn is 2 seconds. At 30 fps you get 60 frames and miss everything |
| **Second camera, wider framing** | Full stand, the plume, and the immediate surroundings | Catches debris ejection, stand movement, and secondary fires |
| **Audio** | A plain audio recording is a cheap and surprisingly useful backup for timing | Ignition timing, burn duration, and abnormal sounds |
| **Sync marker** | A clap, a torch flash, or an electrical pulse visible to the cameras **and** recorded in the data log, at the moment of the fire command | **Data you cannot align in time is data you cannot interpret** |
| **Redundancy** | Micro-SD logging **plus** a live laptop display where possible | One failed link loses the entire test |

### 7.2 Why a SIDE-ON camera view matters

**A frontal or rear view of the motor shows you a bright light and a plume. A side-on view shows you
what actually happened.** Specifically:

- **Grain failure and case rupture** — visible as a sudden lateral flash, a split in the case, or a
  spray of fragments. **From the front, this is invisible.**
- **Flame escaping from case joints, the head end, or the nozzle retaining ring** — the classic
  signature of a seal failure or an over-pressure event. Visible only from the side.
- **Nozzle erosion and ejection** — visible as a change in the plume and, in the worst case, the nozzle
  leaving the frame.
- **Plume geometry and direction** — tells you whether the plume is hitting the stand, the deflector, or
  the ground, all of which affect your thrust measurement.
- **The ignition transient** — the moment the grain lights, whether it lights from the core or the end
  faces, and whether ignition is delayed. This is directly relevant to KNSB's **mountain-shaped pressure
  curve** and its reluctant ignition behaviour.
- **Stand movement** — if the stand moves, your thrust data is contaminated, and only the side view
  shows it.
- **Secondary ignition** — burning propellant ejected sideways onto dry vegetation.

**Set the side-on camera at 90° to the motor axis, level with the motor, and slightly further away than
you think is necessary.** Frame the motor, the stand, the deflector, and at least 1 m of ground on
either side. **Cables long enough that nobody is near the camera during firing.**

### 7.3 Data handling

- **Download and back up before anyone leaves the site.** Two copies, two separate devices.
- **Raw data is never overwritten, edited, or "cleaned".** The raw file is the record.
- **Name files systematically**: `YYYY-MM-DD_MotorID_Batch_TestNo_thrust.csv`, and the same for video.
- Store with the test report.

### 7.4 Weather limits

| Parameter | Limit | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Sustained wind at the pad** | **Above 20 km/h** | **NO-GO.** Wind affects plume dispersal and can topple a stand or shift a light motor |
| **Gusts** | **Above 25 km/h** | **NO-GO.** Measured, not estimated — use an anemometer |
| **Lightning** | **Any within 10 km** | **NO-GO**, and evacuate if it approaches during the session |
| **Rain** | **Any** | **NO-GO.** Water and energetic materials, water and electrical firing circuits |
| **Visibility** | **Insufficient to see the pad clearly from the firing point** | **NO-GO.** You must be able to see the motor to know what happened |
| **Remaining daylight** | **Less than 90 minutes** | **NO-GO.** **All firing happens in daylight.** No dusk tests, no "we'll just squeeze it in" |
| **Ambient temperature** | **Above 38 °C** | Reassess. Personnel heat stress is a real cause of error |
| **Dry vegetation within 50 m** | **Uncontrolled** | **NO-GO** unless a fire watch and additional water are in place |

### 7.5 NO-GO criteria — the complete list

**Any single item below disqualifies the test. There is no discussion, no vote, and no override.**

1. Any person or animal **downrange**, in the blast fan, or within the exclusion zone
2. A member of the **public, vehicle, or livestock** within the minimum safe distance
3. **Wind above limits** (sustained > 20 km/h at the pad, gusts > 25 km/h)
4. **Lightning within 10 km**, or thunderstorm activity in the area
5. **Rain or wet ground** at the pad
6. **No fire cover** — no extinguisher at the pad and firing point, no bulk water, no dunk bucket
7. **Dunk bucket not full**, or extinguisher **out of date, unpressurised, or the wrong type**
8. **Escape route blocked**, unclear, or not walked and known by everyone
9. **Fewer than the required number of people**: minimum **two authorised persons** for the test itself,
   plus Range Safety, plus the Safety Officer. **Never a solo test**
10. **No Safety Officer present**, or no Test Director named
11. **Motor not proof-tested** (new design), or **case history unknown or damaged**
12. **Grain has not passed the density check and the tap test**
13. **Igniter leads not shunted**, or shunting discipline not verifiable
14. **Arming circuit never functionally verified**, or the Safe/Arm box not tested this session
15. **Ignition box shows current at the igniter terminals in the SAFE state**
16. **Motor mounting insecure**, or ballast / anchoring less than 5× expected peak thrust
17. **Load cell or pressure transducer uncalibrated**, or data logging not verified running
18. **No side-on camera**, or video not confirmed recording
19. **Data logging not synced** to the fire command
20. **Range Safety has not announced "RANGE IS CLEAR"**
21. **Weather not measured** — wind guested rather than read from an anemometer
22. **Less than 90 minutes of daylight** remaining
23. **Any person on site who has not been briefed** on the sequence, abort signals, escape routes, and
    muster point
24. **Any person on site without the required PPE**
25. **Phones in hand** during an energetic operation
26. **Any person unfit for duty** — fatigued, ill, impaired
27. **A member has expressed a safety concern that has not been resolved to their satisfaction**
28. **Any unexplained anomaly** in setup — a measurement that does not match, a component that does not
    look right, a change nobody can account for
29. **Any item on the checklist unsigned**
30. **Schedule pressure being used as a reason to proceed**

> **Item 30 is the one that causes the others.** When a test is rushed, the checks get skipped, the
> shunts stay off, the range check is a glance, and the wind is guessed. **If you are rushing, stop the
> test.** A delayed test costs a weekend. A rushed test costs a person.

## 8. Post-test inspection and documentation

### 8.1 Motor inspection

| Item | What to look for | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Case exterior** | Bulging, ovality, discolouration, cracking, paint loss | Any permanent deformation = **the case is retired** |
| **Case interior** | Erosion, charring, heat damage, liner condition | Assesses whether the thermal liner is adequate |
| **Head end / forward closure** | Blow-by, seal damage, thread condition, deformation | Seal failures show here first |
| **Nozzle** | Throat diameter (measured!), erosion pattern, cracking, graphite/phenolic wear | **Measure the throat before and after.** Erosion changes chamber pressure |
| **O-rings and seals** | Compression set, cuts, burning, hardening | Failed seals = gas escape = loss of pressure and a burn-through path |
| **Grain remnants** | Sliver shape, unburned propellant, evidence of cracks or channelling, end-face ignition pattern | **Tells you whether ignition was uniform** — directly relevant to KNSB's mountain curve |
| **Inhibitor** | Disbonding, burning behind the inhibitor, charring | Inhibitor failure increases burning area |
| **Igniter** | Position, consumption, residue in the throat | Confirms whether the igniter performed as intended |
| **Thrust stand** | Movement, deformation, fasteners loosened, deflector condition | **A stand that moved invalidates the thrust data** |

**Photograph everything before disturbing it.** Once a component is removed, the evidence of how it
failed is gone.

### 8.2 Data analysis

1. **Plot thrust vs time and pressure vs time** on the same time base.
2. **Compute total impulse** = the area under the thrust-time curve. Compare with the prediction.
3. **Compare the pressure curve shape with the prediction.** Flat, mountain-shaped, spiked, or slow to
   rise? **KNSB's mountain-shaped curve comes from delayed end-face ignition — check the grain remnants
   for confirmation.**
4. **Check burn time** against the prediction from web thickness ÷ burn rate.
5. **Compute delivered Isp** = total impulse ÷ (propellant mass × g).
6. **Note every anomaly** — a spike, a plateau, a tail-off, an unexpected duration.
7. **One test proves nothing.** You need **3–5 identical motors with overlapping thrust curves** before
   you have a repeatable design. Change **one variable at a time** between tests.
8. **Compare with the strand burner data** — the measured burn rate is the foundation of the whole
   design chain.

### 8.3 Test report — within 24 hours

The report contains:

1. **Test identification:** motor ID, batch number, test number, date, location
2. **Objective** — what this test was meant to determine
3. **Configuration:** case, grain geometry and mass, nozzle throat diameter, igniter type and charge,
   propellant batch and density ratio, tap test result
4. **Personnel:** Test Director, Safety Officer, Range Safety, data operator, documentarian, observers
5. **Site and weather:** distance, wind, temperature, humidity, sky
6. **Checklist record** — the signed Phase A–F sheet
7. **Results:** thrust curve, pressure curve, total impulse, burn time, delivered Isp, peak thrust, peak
   pressure
8. **Photographs and video reference**
9. **Post-test inspection findings**, component by component
10. **Anomalies and deviations from prediction**, with a hypothesis for each
11. **Near misses**, if any
12. **Conclusions and the next action** — and explicitly, **what single variable will change next**
13. **Sign-off** by the Test Director and the Safety Officer

**Filing:** the report goes to the club's permanent test record and to the faculty advisor. It is
**dated and versioned**. It is what the next member inherits — and it is why one team's failure becomes
the next team's knowledge instead of the next team's accident.
