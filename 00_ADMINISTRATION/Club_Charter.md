## PREAMBLE

The UVCE Rocketry Club is founded by students of the University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering who wish to design, build, test and fly real rocket hardware — not merely to study it. The Club exists because engineering competence is acquired by building things that can fail, and because a student who has personally watched a vehicle they designed leave the ground carries that experience for life.

The founding members recognise three hard truths at the outset, and this Constitution is written around them:

1. **The greatest risk to this Club is not money, and it is not hardware. It is people.** A rocket can be rebuilt from a parts list and a drawing. A team cannot be rebuilt from nothing in June.
2. **Every founding member will graduate.** A Club whose knowledge lives only in the heads of its current members has a fixed and known date of death. This Constitution therefore treats documentation, succession and recruitment as constitutional obligations, not as administrative preferences.
3. **A rocketry club can injure people.** Composite propellant, pressure vessels, energetic materials, high-speed hardware and range operations are genuinely hazardous. No competition placement, no deadline, no sponsor, and no academic credit is worth a burn, an amputation or a death.

Accordingly, this Constitution is subordinate to safety in all respects, and every member is empowered to enforce that subordination.

## ARTICLE 1 — NAME, AFFILIATION AND JURISDICTION

**1.1 Name.** The Club shall be known as the **UVCE Rocketry Club**, abbreviated **URC**. The name may also be used in the form "UVCE Rocketry Club (URC), University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering, Bengaluru".

**1.2 Affiliation.** The Club shall function as a recognised student technical association of the University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering (UVCE), K.R. Circle, Bengaluru, under the general supervision of the UVCE Student Council and the faculty and administration of the College.

**1.3 Jurisdiction.** The Club's activities shall be conducted in accordance with:

- the rules, regulations and disciplinary code of UVCE and its parent university;
- the laws of India, including such rules and guidelines as apply to rocketry, explosives, hazardous materials, aviation and public safety;
- the rules of any competition in which the Club participates, including the **IN-SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition**, whose rulebook takes precedence over the Club's own preferences for the duration of that competition.

**1.4 Non-affiliation disclaimer.** The Club is a student body. It does not speak for, represent, or bind UVCE, IN-SPACe, the Department of Space, or any government authority. No Club document, social media post or public statement shall imply institutional or governmental endorsement of the Club's technical work.

**1.5 Non-commercial character.** The Club is a non-profit, non-commercial student body. No member shall draw a salary, commission or personal profit from Club funds or Club work.

**1.6 Location.** The Club's registered place of activity is the UVCE campus, K.R. Circle, Bengaluru — 560 001. Test and launch activities shall be conducted only at sites for which written permission has been obtained (see Article 8 and the companion document *Institutional Approvals and Compliance*).

## ARTICLE 2 — VISION AND MISSION

**2.1 Vision.** To establish UVCE as a recognised centre of student-built rocket engineering in Karnataka — a Club whose alumni enter the aerospace and defence sectors already having designed, built, tested and flown hardware, and whose documentation and safety record are good enough to be shown to any authority without embarrassment.

**2.2 Mission.** The Club shall:

- design, build, ground-test and fly increasingly capable rocket vehicles, beginning with low-power model rockets and progressing deliberately toward higher-power vehicles within the limits of student capability, budget and legal permission;
- instil a safety culture in which hazard identification is routine, checklists are obeyed, and any member may stop work without fear of social penalty;
- prepare and field a competitive team for the IN-SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition;
- sustain itself across graduating batches through documented knowledge, structured recruitment and named successors for every role;
- make rocket engineering accessible to UVCE students of all branches, and to school and pre-university students in Bengaluru through outreach.

**2.3 Motto (adopted).** *"Document it, or it never happened."*

## ARTICLE 3 — AIMS AND OBJECTIVES

**3.1 Technical capability building.** To develop, in each member, practical competence in:

(a) rocket vehicle design and flight performance prediction;
(b) solid propulsion formulation, grain geometry and static testing, strictly within permitted limits;
(c) structural design, materials selection, fabrication and structural testing;
(d) avionics, onboard data logging, telemetry, recovery electronics and flight-computer integration;
(e) recovery system design — parachute sizing, deployment, and dual-deployment practice;
(f) range operations, launch procedures, and the disciplined execution of checklists.

The expected learning progression for a new member is set out in the companion document *Recruitment and Onboarding*, and shall be reviewed annually.

**3.2 Safety culture.** To build a culture in which safety is a design input rather than a compliance afterthought; in which every test and launch is preceded by a written hazard assessment, a written procedure and a go/no-go checklist; and in which near-misses are reported and studied as readily as failures.

**3.3 Competition participation.** To prepare, register and field a team for the IN-SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition, and to treat the competition's technical report, safety documentation and bill-of-materials requirements as productive discipline rather than paperwork.

**3.4 Outreach.** To conduct demonstrations and workshops for UVCE students, neighbouring institutions, schools and pre-university colleges, in order to strengthen the pipeline of future members and to discharge the Club's obligation to the wider student community.

**3.5 Institutional memory.** To maintain a complete, current and accessible archive of the Club's designs, decisions, test data, procedures, finances and lessons learned, such that a new team can pick up the work of a departed team without repeating its mistakes.

**3.6 Research and publication.** To encourage members to document their work at a standard suitable for a technical report, a student paper, a competition submission or a project exhibition, and to credit contributors accurately.

## ARTICLE 4 — SAFETY-FIRST CLAUSE (PARAMOUNT)

> **4.1 Supremacy of safety.** The safety of members, the College community and the public is the Club's first and overriding consideration. Where any provision of this Constitution, any competition deadline, any sponsor expectation, any academic requirement or any instruction from any person conflicts with safety, **safety prevails**. This clause overrides every other clause of this Constitution and every other Club document.

**4.2 Authority to halt.** **Any member of the Club, of any category and any seniority — including a first-semester member on their first day — has the unconditional authority to say "STOP" and to halt any operation that they believe, in good faith, to be unsafe.** Upon such a halt:

(a) the operation stops immediately and remains stopped;
(b) the halting member is not required to justify the decision at the moment of halting, and shall not be penalised in any way — not socially, not academically within the Club's influence, and not in the allocation of roles or opportunities;
(c) the Safety Officer shall be informed promptly and shall record the halt and the reason in the Club's safety log;
(d) work may resume only after the Safety Officer, in consultation with the Team Lead, has reviewed the concern and recorded a written clearance. The Safety Officer may not override a halt unilaterally in a case involving energetic materials, pressure systems or personnel at risk; such a case requires the Faculty Advisor to be informed before resumption.

**4.3 Absolute prohibitions.** The Club shall not, under any circumstances:

(a) fly or test a vehicle or device whose design, propellant or hardware has not been reviewed and cleared through the Club's safety process;
(b) conduct a launch without the applicable permissions and site approvals in place (see *Institutional Approvals and Compliance*);
(c) permit a member to work alone on energetic materials, pressurised systems, or any operation designated as a two-person operation in the applicable procedure;
(d) use restricted, controlled, explosive-licensed, or otherwise legally restricted chemicals, or any hazardous chemical not explicitly permitted by the applicable rules and approved by the Faculty Advisor;
(e) modify a commercial motor, or manufacture a motor, in any manner contrary to the rules of the relevant competition and applicable Indian law;
(f) conduct any activity while impaired by alcohol, cannabis, or any substance affecting judgement;
(g) conceal an incident, a near-miss or a safety violation.

**4.4 Safety documentation.** No test or launch shall proceed without: a written hazard assessment, a written procedure with defined hold points, a go/no-go checklist with named signatories, a defined exclusion zone and range safety officer, and a written emergency response plan naming who calls whom and where the nearest hospital is.

**4.5 Personal protective equipment.** PPE requirements shall be written into each procedure. The minimum for propellant handling and static testing shall be defined by the Safety Officer with the Faculty Advisor's acknowledgement, and shall be enforced without exception.

**4.6 Right to resign from an unsafe task.** No member shall be required to participate in any operation they consider unsafe. A member who declines shall be reassigned without prejudice.

**4.7 Reporting.** The Safety Officer shall report every incident and near-miss to the Faculty Advisor and the Team Lead in writing within 24 hours, and to the Club at the next general meeting. Incident reports are blameless in intent: the purpose is to find the cause, not the culprit. Concealment, however, is a disciplinary matter under Article 9.

## ARTICLE 5 — MEMBERSHIP

### 5.1 Categories

The Club shall have the following categories of membership:

| # | Category | Who | Voting rights | Term |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Core Team Member** | Students holding an office or an active subsystem responsibility | Yes | One academic year, renewable |
| 2 | **General Member** | Any enrolled UVCE student in good standing who has completed induction | Yes (on constitutional matters) | While enrolled and active |
| 3 | **Probationary Member** | New members within their trial period | No | Up to one semester |
| 4 | **Alumni Mentor** | Graduated former members and invited industry mentors | No | Continuing |
| 5 | **Faculty Advisor** | A member of UVCE faculty appointed by the College | No | As appointed |

### 5.2 Eligibility

(a) **General and Probationary Members:** any student currently enrolled at UVCE, in any branch and any year, who has completed the safety induction, signed the code of conduct and the safety undertaking, and paid any Club subscription (if levied).
(b) **Core Team Members:** drawn from General Members; must have completed at least one full semester of active participation, or satisfy the Founders' Exception in Clause 5.6 for the inaugural year; must be selected or elected under Article 7.
(c) **Alumni Mentors:** former members who have graduated, or external professionals invited by the Core Team with the Faculty Advisor's concurrence. Alumni Mentors have no vote and no design authority (see Clause 6.4 and the companion *Roles and Responsibilities*).
(d) **Faculty Advisor:** a faculty member of UVCE appointed by the College. The Faculty Advisor has no vote in Club elections but holds the oversight and veto powers described in Article 6.4.

### 5.3 Expectations of all members

Every member shall:

(a) read, sign and abide by the Code of Conduct (Article 9) and the safety undertaking;
(b) complete the safety induction before entering the workshop, handling any hardware, or attending a test or launch;
(c) attend general meetings or send prior apologies; three consecutive unexplained absences from meetings without apology shall trigger a welfare check by the Operations Lead, and continued absence may be treated as resignation under Clause 5.5;
(d) contribute to documentation (Article 12) — no member is exempt, and documentation is treated as engineering work, not as an optional extra;
(e) behave with respect toward all members regardless of branch, year, gender, language, religion, caste, disability or background;
(f) accept that the Club's safety rules bind them personally, on and off the range.

### 5.4 Rights of members

Every member, including Probationary Members, has the right to:

(a) attend and speak at general meetings (voting is as per Clause 5.1);
(b) inspect the Club's records, ledgers, designs and documentation, subject only to the confidentiality provision in Clause 14.6;
(c) receive safety training appropriate to the work they are asked to do;
(d) halt an unsafe operation under Clause 4.2, without penalty;
(e) receive credit for their contributions in reports, competition submissions and public communication;
(f) raise a grievance with the Core Team, and, if unsatisfied, with the Faculty Advisor.

### 5.5 Cessation of membership

Membership ends on: graduation or transfer from UVCE; written resignation addressed to the Team Lead; removal for misconduct under Article 10; or, for General Members, twelve months of continuous non-participation confirmed in writing by the Team Lead after a welfare check. Alumni retain the option of continuing as Alumni Mentors.

### 5.6 Founders' Exception (transitional, first year only). In the founding academic year 2026–27, the offices of the Club may be filled by the founding members without the one-semester participation requirement, in order to constitute the Club at all. This exception expires at the end of the 2026–27 academic year, after which Article 7 governs all appointments without exception. **All founders shall nonetheless designate a successor under Article 8 within 30 days of the Club's ratification, as a condition of holding office.**

## ARTICLE 6 — ORGANISATIONAL STRUCTURE

### 6.1 Principle

The Club is led by a Core Team of office-bearers. Responsibility for the Club's safety, technical integrity and continuity is distributed deliberately and never concentrated in one person. **For this reason there is both a Team Lead and a Vice President, and a Vice President's principal duty is to be capable of replacing the Team Lead at a day's notice.**

### 6.2 Core Team positions

| # | Position | Primary mandate | Minimum headcount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **President / Team Lead** | Direction, external representation, final accountability | 1 |
| 2 | **Vice President** | Deputy leadership, internal coordination, designated successor to the Team Lead | 1 |
| 3 | **Safety Officer** | Safety authority, hazard review, incident reporting, holds a veto | 1 (+ 1 deputy) |
| 4 | **Propulsion Lead** | Propellant, motor design and static testing within permitted limits | 1 |
| 5 | **Structures Lead** | Airframe, fins, materials, fabrication and structural testing | 1 |
| 6 | **Avionics Lead** | Flight computer, sensors, telemetry, recovery electronics, software | 1 |
| 7 | **Operations Lead** | Range operations, logistics, scheduling, launch-day execution | 1 |
| 8 | **Treasurer** | Funds, budget, ledger, procurement, audit | 1 |
| 9 | **Documentation Lead** | Archive, version control, design records, competition documentation | 1 |

Each position's detailed duties, deliverables, handover obligations and successor requirements are specified in the companion document **`Roles_and_Responsibilities.md`**, which is incorporated into this Constitution by reference and is read as part of it.

### 6.3 Steering Committee

The Core Team, together with the Faculty Advisor and up to two Alumni Mentors, shall form the Steering Committee. It shall meet at least monthly and shall: review progress against the Club's plan; approve budgets above the Operations limit (Article 13); review every incident report; and conduct the annual review of this Constitution in March.

### 6.4 Faculty Advisor — appointment and boundaries

(a) The Faculty Advisor shall be a member of the UVCE faculty appointed by the College, and shall be recorded by name in the Club's records and in every competition submission where required.

(b) **The Faculty Advisor acts in an oversight capacity only.** Consistent with the IN-SPACe competition framework and with the College's own governance expectations, the Faculty Advisor:
 - **shall** ensure compliance with College, legal and competition requirements; attend and observe key design reviews and tests where practicable; verify that documentation and safety processes are actually followed; act as escalation point for member grievances; and certify submissions requiring faculty attestation;
 - **shall NOT** make design decisions, direct technical recommendations, prescribe design solutions, perform the team's engineering, or act as de facto Team Lead. If the Faculty Advisor offers a technical opinion, it is recorded as advice only; the team decides, and the decision and its rationale are documented.

(c) Where the Faculty Advisor is the Head of Institution or a Head of Department, the roles shall be kept distinct in the Club's records, and Club decisions shall be made by the Core Team.

(d) If the Faculty Advisor is unavailable for a period exceeding 30 days, the Team Lead shall request the College to nominate an interim advisor, and shall record the request.

### 6.5 Alumni Mentors — boundaries

Alumni Mentors are a source of continuity, review and encouragement. They may review documents, ask probing questions, recount what went wrong in their own time, and connect the Club to industry and funding.

**Alumni Mentors shall not provide design recommendations or dictate technical solutions** — whether in configuration choice, propellant formulation, structural sizing, avionics selection or recovery design. Their role is Socratic review and experience transfer, not engineering direction. Advice given shall be recorded in the design record as advice and attributed as such; the technical decision, and the reason for it, always belongs to the current team.

### 6.6 Sub-teams and project owners

Each Lead may constitute a sub-team. Each Lead shall name a **deputy** and, for every active project, a **project owner** and an **understudy**. This implements the two-deep rule of Article 8.3(a).

### 6.7 Vacancies

A vacancy in any office shall be filled within 21 days by the procedure in Article 7.3. Until it is filled, the Vice President shall assume the vacant role's coordination duties, and if the Vice President's own office is vacant, the Team Lead shall, but only on an interim basis and only with the Faculty Advisor informed in writing.

## ARTICLE 7 — ELECTIONS, SELECTION AND TERMS

### 7.1 Term of office

The term of office for all Core Team positions is **one academic year**, from the first general meeting after the end of the even-semester examinations (normally late May or early June) to the corresponding date in the following year. Terms may be renewed once for a second year with the Steering Committee's endorsement. No member shall hold the same office for more than two consecutive terms, other than the Safety Officer, whose term may be extended if no trained successor exists — and in that case the two-deep requirement of Article 8 shall be treated as unmet, flagged in writing to the Faculty Advisor, and remedied within one semester.

### 7.2 Election procedure

(a) **Nominations.** The Documentation Lead shall open nominations at least 21 days before the election meeting. Any General Member in good standing may be nominated for any office, with their written consent, and must state what they intend to do in the role.

(b) **Eligibility.** Nominees for a Core Team office shall have completed at least one full semester of active Club participation. Nominees for Safety Officer and for technical Leads shall additionally have completed the relevant technical and safety training, or satisfy the Steering Committee of equivalent competence recorded in writing.

(c) **Voting.** Elections shall be by secret ballot of the General and Core Members present, and by simple majority. Quorum for the election meeting is one-half of the Club's enrolled members or 15 members, whichever is fewer.

(d) **Timing.** Elections shall be held **before the graduating batch departs**, and in any case no later than 30 June each year. **The Club shall at all times maintain an elected Team Lead for the forthcoming year before the outgoing Team Lead's graduation.** Where no eligible candidate stands for an office, the outgoing Core Team, with the Faculty Advisor, shall appoint a member and record the reason for the appointment in the minutes — this is a failure of the pipeline and shall be reviewed at the next Steering Committee meeting.

(e) **Notice.** The election notice, list of nomination papers and the results shall be recorded in the minutes and filed in the Club archive.

### 7.3 Casual vacancies and mid-term removals

(a) A casual vacancy shall be filled by a by-election of the General Members, or, if the vacancy arises within 60 days of the end of term, by Core Team appointment with the Faculty Advisor informed.

(b) An office-bearer may be removed mid-term for: dereliction of duty (defined as failing, for more than 45 consecutive days, to perform the duties recorded in `Roles_and_Responsibilities.md`); a safety violation; misconduct under Article 9; or misuse of funds. Removal requires a two-thirds majority of a quorate general meeting, with 14 days' written notice of the motion to all members and to the affected office-bearer, who shall be given an opportunity to be heard. The Faculty Advisor shall be informed of any such motion before the vote.

(c) The Safety Officer's removal shall additionally require the Faculty Advisor's written acknowledgement, and the Faculty Advisor may veto the removal of a Safety Officer who has, in the Advisor's assessment, acted in good faith on a safety concern.

### 7.4 Office-bearer declaration

Every office-bearer shall, within 14 days of taking office, file with the Documentation Lead: an acceptance note, a successor designation (Article 8), and their term's intended deliverables. Failing to file within 30 days constitutes grounds for a casual vacancy.

## ARTICLE 8 — SUCCESSION AND KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER

> This Article is the heart of the Constitution. A club that dies every June is not a club; it is a project with an expiry date. Compliance with this Article is a condition of holding office, not an aspiration.

**8.1 Successor designation (two-deep rule at the leadership level).** Every office-bearer and every Lead shall, within 30 days of taking office:

(a) designate **one named successor** — a member in a junior year — and record the name in the Club's Succession Register;
(b) record what the successor must be taught and how competence will be demonstrated;
(c) ensure the successor actually participates in the role's core work during the term: attending the meetings, being copied on the correspondence, learning the procedures, and taking the lead at least once on a task of real consequence.

A successor who has never done the work is not a successor. They are a name on a list.

**8.2 Mandatory documentation handover.** No office-bearer or Lead may relinquish a role — including on graduation — without completing the handover process of Article 8.4 and obtaining a written handover acknowledgement from the successor and from the Documentation Lead. The Faculty Advisor and the Team Lead shall be informed of any handover not completed 30 days before the outgoing member's graduation. **A member who leaves without a completed handover shall not be issued a Club service certificate, a letter of recommendation, or a competition-credit letter**, and this consequence shall be stated up front at the time of their appointment.

**8.3 No single point of failure (the general continuity rule).**

(a) **No critical subsystem may depend on a single person.** For every critical subsystem — propulsion, structures, avionics, recovery, range operations, finances, documentation — there shall be at least two members who can operate it, diagnose it and explain it.

(b) The Documentation Lead shall maintain and publish, at the start of each semester, a **Critical Knowledge Register** listing each critical subsystem or process, the members competent in it, and the members in training. Any item with fewer than two competent members shall be flagged **RED** in the Register and reported to the Steering Committee, which shall direct a remedy (training, recruitment, or a documented simplification of scope).

(c) If a RED item remains RED for one full semester, the Club shall not proceed with any activity that depends on it. The Club's scope is limited by its redundancy, not by its ambition.

**8.4 Handover process — mandatory for every role.** A handover shall comprise, at minimum:

1. a **written handover note** (three to ten pages) covering: the state of the work; what is decided and what is still open; known problems and half-finished tasks; contacts and their roles; access credentials transferred through the proper channel (never by plain-text password in a document — see Article 12.7); and an honest list of what the outgoing holder never got around to doing;
2. a **file and archive audit**: all drawings, data, code, photographs, test logs, purchase records and correspondence in the Club's archive, correctly named and versioned, with nothing left on a personal device;
3. a **live walkthrough**: a two-hour session in which the outgoing holder briefs the successor and the successor demonstrates access to every system;
4. a **joint handover meeting** with the Team Lead and Documentation Lead, minuted;
5. a **sign-off**: the successor confirms in writing that they can find the files, operate the systems, and name the open problems. If they cannot, the handover is incomplete.

**8.5 Annual March Review.** In March every year, before the graduating batch leaves, the Steering Committee shall conduct a **Continuity Review** covering: the Succession Register, the Critical Knowledge Register, the state of the archive, and the readiness of the incoming team. The outcome shall be minuted and filed. This review is mandatory and may not be skipped for lack of time.

**8.6 Exit interview.** Every departing member, whatever their category, shall be offered an exit interview and invited to write a short "what I wish I had known" note for the archive. These notes are among the Club's most valuable documents and shall be indexed and retained permanently.

**8.7 Protection of the archive on dissolution.** If the Club ceases to function or is dissolved, its archive shall be handed to the Faculty Advisor and the Department for safekeeping, and shall be offered to any successor student body seeking to revive the Club. The archive shall not be deleted, sold or taken by any individual.

## ARTICLE 9 — CODE OF CONDUCT

### 9.1 General conduct

Members shall:

(a) treat every other member with courtesy and respect, in person and online;
(b) maintain the College's discipline and anti-ragging rules in full; harassment, ragging, discrimination and intimidation are grounds for immediate removal under Article 10;
(c) represent the Club honestly, and not claim institutional or governmental endorsement the Club does not have;
(d) respect College property, workshop tools, lab equipment and the property of others;
(e) use Club funds, tools and materials only for Club purposes, with records;
(f) keep the Club's shared channels professional; no member shall be excluded from a technical discussion by language, clique or inside joke.

### 9.2 Amateur and student rocketry ethics

The Club adopts and enforces the following ethical rules. Violation is a disciplinary matter, and, in a competition context, may also render the Club's entry liable to disqualification.

**(a) No copying of designs or code.** The Club shall not copy, reproduce or adapt other teams' designs, drawings, CAD models, code, reports or documentation without written permission and proper attribution. Open-source material used shall be attributed in the design record and in any competition submission, with its licence respected. Inspiration drawn from public literature is legitimate; uncredited appropriation is not.

**(b) No outsourcing of development.** The Club's design, analysis, fabrication and software shall be the work of its own members. The Club shall not pay, persuade or allow a third party — including a vendor, a senior, an alumnus, an external engineer or a service provider — to perform the intellectual work of the project. Commercial supply of standard materials, components, machined parts and off-the-shelf hardware is permitted and expected; **the engineering decisions and the integration are not.** Where a vendor performs fabrication to the Club's drawings, the drawings, specifications and inspection are the Club's, and this shall be documented. Where an external person materially contributes, they are recorded as a mentor or advisor and their contribution is described honestly in any submission.

**(c) No restricted or hazardous chemicals.** The Club shall not procure, store, handle or use restricted, controlled, explosive-licensed or otherwise prohibited chemicals, energetic materials, oxidisers or additives. Any propellant or material the Club does use shall be within the limits explicitly permitted by the applicable competition rules and by law, shall be approved in writing by the Safety Officer and acknowledged by the Faculty Advisor, and shall be handled under a written procedure. **If a member is unsure whether a substance is permitted, the substance is not used until the question is answered in writing by the Safety Officer with the Faculty Advisor informed.** No member shall attempt to synthesise, improvise or "improve" a propellant outside an approved and reviewed procedure.

**(d) No falsification.** Test data, flight data, photographs, invoices, membership records and competition submissions shall be truthful. Data shall not be invented, smoothed, selected or discarded to make a result look better. Where a test failed, the record says so.

**(e) Safety of self and others first.** No member shall pressure another member to proceed with an operation they consider unsafe.

**(f) Respect for the law and for authorities.** Members shall cooperate honestly with College authorities, district administration, police, and any regulatory body, and shall not conduct unpermitted launches or tests.

### 9.3 Undertaking

Every member shall sign a safety and conduct undertaking in the form prescribed in Annexure A, and renewed annually.

## ARTICLE 10 — DISCIPLINE

**10.1 Grounds.** Misconduct includes: a safety violation; concealment of an incident; harassment, ragging or discrimination; falsification of data or records; misuse or misappropriation of funds or property; breach of the ethics rules in Article 9.2; and conduct bringing the Club or the College into disrepute.

**10.2 Procedure.** The Core Team shall (a) inform the member in writing of the allegation; (b) give the member a reasonable opportunity to respond, ordinarily not less than seven days; (c) hear the matter with the Faculty Advisor informed; (d) decide by simple majority of the Core Team, with the Faculty Advisor's view recorded; and (e) communicate the decision and reasons in writing, minuted and filed.

**10.3 Sanctions.** Warning; suspension of range and workshop privileges; suspension from the Club for a stated period; removal from office; termination of membership. **In a case involving a deliberate safety violation, the Safety Officer may immediately suspend the member's workshop and range privileges pending the full procedure** — this is a protective measure, not a punishment, and the full procedure shall follow within 14 days.

**10.4 Appeal.** A member may appeal in writing to the Faculty Advisor within 14 days. The Faculty Advisor's decision, taken after consulting the College as appropriate, is final within the Club.

## ARTICLE 11 — MEETINGS AND QUORUM

**11.1 General Body Meeting.** Held **fortnightly** during the semester, at a fixed day and time published at the start of each semester. Agenda circulated at least 24 hours in advance. The Team Lead chairs, or the Vice President in their absence.

**11.2 Core Team Meeting.** Held **weekly**. Standing agenda: safety review (always first); progress against plan; blockers; finance; documentation; people and pipeline. Minutes recorded by the Documentation Lead or a designated alternate.

**11.3 Steering Committee Meeting.** Held **monthly**, with the Faculty Advisor and Alumni Mentors invited.

**11.4 Design Reviews.** Held at project milestones. See the companion document *Project Management and Documentation* for the Preliminary Design Review and Critical Design Review requirements.

**11.5 Quorum.**

| Meeting | Quorum |
|---|---|
| General Body Meeting | One-third of enrolled members, or 12 members, whichever is fewer, including at least 3 Core Team members |
| Core Team Meeting | One-half of filled Core Team positions, including the Team Lead or Vice President |
| Steering Committee | Team Lead or Vice President, plus two other Core Team members, plus the Faculty Advisor or their nominated substitute |
| Election meeting | As Clause 7.2(c) |
| Constitutional amendment | As Clause 15.3 |
| Dissolution | As Article 16 |

**11.6 Decisions.** Ordinary decisions by simple majority of those present and voting; the chair has a casting vote only in the case of a tie in a Core Team meeting. No decision may be taken at a meeting without quorum; anything decided without quorum is void and shall be re-tabled.

**11.7 Minutes.** Every meeting shall be minuted: date, time, present, absent with apologies, agenda, decisions with the reasoning, action items with named owners and due dates, and the next meeting date. Minutes shall be circulated within 72 hours and filed in the archive. **An action item without a named owner and a date is not an action item.**

**11.8 Emergency meetings.** The Team Lead, or any two Core Team members, or the Safety Officer alone, may convene an emergency meeting with not less than 12 hours' notice. Safety matters take precedence over all other business at any meeting.

## ARTICLE 12 — DOCUMENTATION, RECORDS AND THE ARCHIVE

**12.1 Principle.** Documentation is not overhead. It is the Club's principal asset and its only defence against the graduation cliff. Every member is a documenter.

**12.2 Minimum documentation set.** The Club shall maintain, at all times:

1. this Constitution and the companion administration documents;
3. the **Design Record** — every significant design decision with date, options considered, rationale, decision-maker and status;
4. drawings and CAD models, correctly named and versioned;
5. the **Test Log** — every test, with setup, data, outcome and conclusions;
6. the **Incident Register** — incidents and near-misses, blameless in intent;
7. the **Safety Log** — halts, hazard assessments, clearances;
8. the **Financial Ledger** — every rupee in and out, with receipts;
9. the **Bill of Materials** with estimate-versus-actual costs;
10. the **Succession Register** and **Critical Knowledge Register** (Article 8);
11. minutes of all meetings;
12. the **Member Register** and the **Skills Matrix**;
13. the **Approvals File** — every permission, letter and acknowledgement.

**12.3 Decision records.** Every design decision shall be recorded with: date; decision; alternatives considered; rationale; who decided; who was consulted; and the reversibility of the decision. A decision that is not recorded did not happen, and will be re-litigated by next year's team at full cost.

**12.4 Standards and naming.** File naming, revision numbering, drawing conventions and log formats are specified in the companion document *Project Management and Documentation*, and are binding on all members.

**12.5 Version control.** Documents and code shall be version-controlled. **Git is the Club's required tool**, with a remote repository hosted on the College's or the Club's own account (see *Project Management and Documentation* for the recommended structure). A design file that exists only as `final_final_v2.docx` on one laptop is a liability.

**12.6 Archive custodian.** The Documentation Lead is the custodian of the archive, and shall publish its structure at the start of each semester. Access shall be granted to all members; write access to the archive's master branches is limited, but read access is universal.

**12.7 Credentials.** Account credentials, repository access and lab permissions shall be held in a shared, access-controlled store that the Team Lead, Documentation Lead and Vice President can all reach, so that a single member's departure cannot lock the Club out of its own systems. Passwords shall not be written into ordinary design documents, reports or chat messages.

**12.8 Retention.** The Design Record, Test Log, Incident Register, Financial Ledger, Approvals File and minutes are permanent records. Nothing in these categories shall be deleted, ever — superseded documents are marked superseded, not removed.

**12.9 Backup.** The archive shall be backed up in at least two independent places (a cloud repository and a physical drive held by the Documentation Lead and one other Core Team member), with a documented check of restore capability once per semester. Anything important shall not exist in only one location, and shall never exist on only one person's laptop.

## ARTICLE 13 — FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

**13.1 Bank account and funds.** The Club's funds shall be held in a **bank account in the name of the Club** (or, where the College requires it, in an account operated through the College/department with the Club as the designated beneficiary), opened only after the Club's recognition and with the Faculty Advisor's knowledge. **No Club money shall be held in a personal bank account, UPI wallet or cash box of any office-bearer**, other than a small imprest advance under Clause 13.4.

**13.2 Signatories.** The account shall have at least two signatories: the Treasurer and the Team Lead, or the Treasurer and the Faculty Advisor where the College's rules require a faculty signatory. **Every payment above INR 2,000 requires two signatures/approvals.** No office-bearer shall be a sole signatory.

**13.3 Approval thresholds.** Unless the Steering Committee or the College's rules set a stricter limit:

| Amount | Approval required |
|---|---|
| Up to INR 2,000 | Operations Lead or the relevant Lead, within their approved budget line; recorded in the ledger |
| INR 2,001 – INR 10,000 | Treasurer **and** Team Lead, in writing (email/message is acceptable if filed) |
| INR 10,001 – INR 50,000 | Core Team resolution at a quorate meeting, minuted, with the Faculty Advisor informed |
| Above INR 50,000 | Steering Committee resolution with the Faculty Advisor's concurrence, and compliance with any College procurement rules |

**13.4 Imprest and reimbursement.** A standing imprest of not more than INR 5,000 may be held by the Operations Lead for field and range expenses, reconciled within 7 days of each event. Reimbursement requires a receipt; expenses above INR 500 without a receipt require the Treasurer's and Team Lead's joint written approval, and the reason shall be recorded.

**13.5 Receipts and record-keeping.** Every rupee received and spent shall be recorded in the **Financial Ledger** with date, amount, counterparty, purpose, budget line, approver and the receipt's file reference. Receipts and invoices shall be scanned and filed in the archive within 7 days. Cash transactions shall be avoided wherever possible, and any cash movement shall be countersigned on the day.

**13.6 Budget.** The Treasurer shall present an annual budget for Core Team approval within 30 days of taking office, and a revised budget at the start of each semester. Each Lead shall own their budget line and shall not exceed it without Clause 13.3 approval.

**13.7 Bill of materials and estimate-versus-actual.** The Club shall maintain a current **Bill of Materials (BOM)** for each vehicle, listing every item, quantity, unit cost, supplier, estimated cost and actual cost, with the variance recorded. This is required both for good management and because the IN-SPACe competition requires participating teams to document a complete bill of materials with estimate-versus-actual costs. The Treasurer and the Documentation Lead are jointly responsible for its accuracy.

**13.8 Sponsorship and donations.** All sponsorship money, CSR contributions, donations and prize money shall be received into the Club account, acknowledged in writing to the donor, and recorded with the purpose and any conditions attached. Sponsors' conditions shall be honoured only insofar as they do not conflict with Article 4 or with the College's rules. **No sponsor may influence a design or safety decision.**

**13.9 Audit.** The accounts shall be audited **every semester** by two persons who are not signatories — ordinarily the Documentation Lead and one General Member nominated by the general meeting, or the College's accounts office where it offers this. The audit report shall be presented to the General Body within 30 days of the semester's end and filed in the archive.

**13.10 Transparency.** The Treasurer shall report income, expenditure and balance at every General Body Meeting. Any member may inspect the ledger and receipts on request.

**13.11 Handover of finances.** The Treasurer's handover shall include bank mandates updated, signatories changed, ledger closed and reconciled, all receipts filed, and a joint reconciliation signed by the outgoing and incoming Treasurer and the Team Lead.

## ARTICLE 14 — RELATIONSHIP WITH THE COLLEGE AND EXTERNAL PARTIES

**14.1 Recognition.** The Club shall obtain and maintain official recognition from the College through the Student Council, and shall comply with the conditions of that recognition (see *Institutional Approvals and Compliance*).

**14.2 Institutional approval letter.** For the IN-SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition, the Club shall obtain an **approval letter on institutional letterhead signed by the Head of the Institution**, listing every team member's name, institute ID, course, branch, year of graduation and role. The Documentation Lead shall maintain the current version in the Approvals File and shall refresh it whenever the team roster changes.

**14.3 Use of College name.** The College's name and logo shall be used only in the forms the College permits, and never in a way that suggests institutional endorsement of an unapproved activity.

**14.4 External collaboration.** Collaboration with other institutions, industry or mentors requires the Core Team's approval and the Faculty Advisor's knowledge. Any collaboration shall respect Article 9.2 (no copying, no outsourcing).

**14.5 Media and public communication.** Only the Team Lead, or a member designated by the Team Lead, shall speak to the media or post on the Club's official accounts. Safety incidents shall not be discussed publicly before the Faculty Advisor and the College have been informed.

**14.6 Confidentiality.** Member contact details, medical information and internal disciplinary records shall be kept confidential to the Core Team and the Faculty Advisor, and shall not be published.

## ARTICLE 15 — AMENDMENTS

**15.1 Proposal.** Any Core Team member, or any five General Members, may propose an amendment in writing to the Documentation Lead, stating the text and the reason.

**15.2 Notice.** The proposed amendment shall be circulated to all members at least **14 days** before the meeting at which it is to be considered.

**15.3 Approval.** An amendment requires a **two-thirds majority of a quorate General Body Meeting**, with at least one-half of enrolled members or 15 members present (whichever is fewer), and the Faculty Advisor informed in writing of the outcome. Amendments touching Article 4 (Safety) require, in addition, the Faculty Advisor's written acknowledgement, and **no amendment may dilute Clause 4.1 or Clause 4.2** — the supremacy of safety and the authority of any member to halt an unsafe operation are unamendable in substance.

**15.4 Record.** Every amendment shall be recorded in the Amendment Register with version number, date, text changed, proposer, and approving meeting. Superseded versions shall be retained.

**15.5 Annual review.** The Steering Committee shall review this Constitution every March, and shall correct defects found during the year. The review is minuted.

## ARTICLE 16 — DISSOLUTION

**16.1 Grounds.** The Club may be dissolved only if: (a) the College withdraws recognition; or (b) the General Body resolves to dissolve by a **three-fourths majority of enrolled members** at a meeting convened with 30 days' written notice stating the dissolution motion; or (c) membership falls below the minimum of eight members for two consecutive semesters and no revival is achieved within one further semester.

**16.2 Procedure.** Before dissolution takes effect, the Core Team shall prepare a **Dissolution Report** containing: the reasons; a complete statement of assets, liabilities and bank balance; the disposition of all physical assets (to be offered first to the College/department for use by other student bodies, and not to be distributed to individuals); and the disposition of the archive under Article 8.7.

**16.3 Assets.** No Club asset, and no part of any remaining balance, shall be distributed to any member, office-bearer or former member. Remaining funds shall be transferred to the College, the department, or a student welfare or technical fund designated by the College.

**16.4 The archive survives.** The archive shall be handed to the Faculty Advisor and the Department, indexed, and offered to any future student group seeking to revive the Club. **The Club's records outlive the Club.**

**16.5 Final audit.** The accounts shall be audited and the audit report filed with the College before dissolution is complete.

## ARTICLE 17 — INTERPRETATION AND MISCELLANEOUS

**17.1 Interpretation.** Where this Constitution is silent or ambiguous, the Core Team shall interpret it, and the Faculty Advisor may be consulted. Interpretations shall be minuted so that next year's team does not have to guess.

**17.2 Precedence.** In case of conflict: (1) applicable law and government authority directions; (2) College rules; (3) competition rules, for competition activities; (4) this Constitution; (5) other Club documents. **Article 4 (Safety) qualifies all five.**

**17.3 Severability.** If any clause is held invalid, the remainder continues in force.

**17.4 Companion documents.** The following are incorporated by reference and are binding:
- `Roles_and_Responsibilities.md`
- `Recruitment_and_Onboarding.md`
- `Institutional_Approvals_and_Compliance.md`
- `Funding_and_Sponsorship.md`
- `Project_Management_and_Documentation.md`

**17.5 Language.** The Constitution is written in English, which is the working language of the Club. Members may participate in discussion in Kannada or any other language they are comfortable in; minutes and records shall be in English.
