## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

1. **At appointment.** Every office-bearer reads their own role description, signs it, and files the signed copy with the Documentation Lead within 14 days of taking office. The signature means "I have read this, I accept it, and I know what I hand over."
2. **At every handover.** The "Handover Pack" and "What a successor needs to know" sections are the handover checklist. Nothing is complete until the successor can find every item unaided.
3. **At every review.** Ambiguity about who does what is the most common cause of a club stalling. When two people are unsure who owns a task, come back to this document; if it is still unclear, it is a defect — amend it via the Documentation Lead.
4. **Two-deep rule.** Every role has a named successor and a deputy. See `Club_Charter.md` Article 8. **No role may be held by one person with no understudy.** A role with a vacant successor line is a RED item on the Critical Knowledge Register.

**Common shorthand used below:** Team Lead = President. "Semester week" means a normal teaching week, excluding examination and vacation periods.

## 1. PRESIDENT / TEAM LEAD

**Purpose.** To hold the Club's direction, to represent it externally, and to carry final accountability for whether the Club does what it said it would do — safely, and on time, and in a way the next team can inherit.

**Specific duties.**
- Chair General Body and Core Team meetings; set the agenda with the Documentation Lead; ensure the standing items (safety first, then progress) are always covered.
- Own the Club's annual plan and semester goals, and be answerable for progress against them.
- Represent the Club to the College administration, the Student Council, the department, the Faculty Advisor, sponsors and competition organisers. Be the principal signatory on external communication, with the Faculty Advisor copied on anything institutional.
- Convene the Steering Committee with the Faculty Advisor; ensure it meets monthly.
- Make the call on scope: what the Club will attempt this year and — more importantly — what it will explicitly not attempt. Publish that decision in writing.
- Own the competition relationship for the IN-SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition: registration, deadlines, submission calendar, coordination with the team.
- Chair PDR and CDR reviews (or delegate the chair, but attend).
- Escalate early and honestly: on slipping schedules, on missing permissions, on RED continuity items, on anything safety-related.
- Run the annual March Continuity Review and Elections process to schedule (Charter Articles 7 and 8).
- Ensure every office-bearer files their successor designation and handover pack.
- Be the person the College calls first, and be reachable.

**Required skills.** Technical literacy sufficient to understand the team's designs well enough to ask hard questions (not to make every decision — the Leads decide); project management basics; clear written and spoken English; the ability to say no to a plan that is unsafe or unaffordable; willingness to delegate; composure in an incident.

**Time commitment.** 10–15 hours/week in-semester; 20+ hours/week in the four weeks before a competition submission, a major test or a launch campaign; available on call during test and launch days.

**Key deliverables.**
- Semester goals document and the year plan, published within 30 days of taking office.
- A current competition calendar with named owners per milestone.
- Monthly Steering Committee minutes (with the Documentation Lead).
- Signed successor designation within 30 days.
- The annual Continuity Review report.
- Complete handover pack before graduation.

**Handover pack.**
- The year plan with actual progress marked against it, and an honest note on what did not happen.
- The external relationship map: names, designations, phone numbers and email addresses of every College officer, the Student Council, the Faculty Advisor, sponsors, competition contacts.
- The current status of every approval and permission (see *Institutional Approvals and Compliance*), including expiry dates.
- The competition calendar for the coming year with registration deadlines.
- Access credentials to the Club's official email, social accounts and repository organisation.
- Open issues and the decisions that were deferred.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- Which promises have been made to the College or to a sponsor, and what fulfilling them requires.
- Whose support the Club actually depends on, and how to keep it.
- Which past plan was over-ambitious and why — the specific failure mode, not a general warning.
- The exact date by which the next competition registration and the institutional approval letter must be started (approval letters take time and cannot be produced the day before a deadline).
- That the Team Lead's job is not to be the best engineer in the room. It is to make sure there is a next team.

## 2. VICE PRESIDENT

**Purpose.** To be the second-in-command and internal coordinator, and — the defining purpose — to be capable of taking over the Team Lead's duties at a day's notice.

**Specific duties.**
- Act as Team Lead in the Team Lead's absence, with full authority, and record anything material decided in that capacity.
- Own internal coordination: the semester calendar, meeting schedule, room bookings, workshop and lab slot bookings, internal deadlines.
- Track every action item from every meeting to closure; the Club's action register is the Vice President's responsibility.
- Chair the weekly Core Team meeting on a fixed cadence (e.g. alternate weeks) so that the Team Lead's absence does not stop the Club.
- Handle member welfare: check in on members who disappear, mediate interpersonal friction early, ensure no member is isolated or excluded from technical work.
- Own the induction and onboarding schedule with the Documentation Lead and the relevant Leads (see *Recruitment and Onboarding*).
- Manage the shared workspace: repository hygiene, folder structure compliance, meeting-minutes filing.
- Deputise for the Team Lead at reviews when required, and co-sign internal approvals where the Charter requires two.
- Own the member register and skills matrix, jointly with the Documentation Lead.

**Required skills.** Organisation and follow-through; comfort with calendars, trackers and documents; good interpersonal judgement; ability to lead a meeting; enough technical breadth to know who owns what.

**Time commitment.** 8–12 hours/week in-semester; more during onboarding season and before major deadlines.

**Key deliverables.**
- Semester master calendar published in week 1.
- Action item register, current, with overdue items escalated to the Team Lead weekly.
- Onboarding schedule and completion record for each new cohort.
- Current member register and skills matrix.
- Signed successor designation within 30 days.

**Handover pack.**
- Master calendar template with the previous year's fixed dates (semester start, exam windows, competition deadlines, College events).
- Action register with the disposition of each item.
- Member register, attendance record, and a candid note on which members were at risk of leaving and why.
- Room booking and lab access procedures, with the names of the people who approve them.
- The onboarding schedule that existed, and what was wrong with it.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- Exactly which recurring College deadlines exist (they are the same every year, and they are knowable in advance).
- Who to ask for a room, a lab slot, a projector, and how far in advance.
- Which members are becoming experts and in what — a Vice President should be able to name the Club's five most important continuity risks.
- That being the deputy means actually doing the job occasionally, not merely holding the title.

## 3. SAFETY OFFICER

**Purpose.** To be the Club's independent safety authority: the person who reviews hazards before work happens, who can stop work, and who makes sure that the Club's safety claims are true rather than aspirational. **This role holds a veto (subject to Clause 4.2(d) of the Charter) and reports directly to the Faculty Advisor on safety matters, independently of the Team Lead.**

**Specific duties.**
- Maintain the Club's safety management system: written procedures, hazard assessments, go/no-go checklists, exclusion zones, emergency response plans.
- Review and sign off every procedure involving energetic materials, pressure systems, fabrication hazards, electrical hazards, or flight operations **before** work begins. No sign-off, no work.
- Conduct the mandatory safety induction for every new member before they enter the workshop or attend a test/launch. Keep the attendance record.
- Define PPE requirements per activity, and enforce them — including the authority to remove a member from the range for non-compliance.
- Act as Range Safety Officer or appoint and train a competent RSO for each test and launch; brief all participants; control access to the pad area; hold the countdown authority.
- Maintain the **Safety Log** (halts, clearances, hazard assessments) and the **Incident Register** (incidents and near-misses), both blameless in intent.
- Report every incident and near-miss to the Faculty Advisor and Team Lead in writing within 24 hours, and present all reports at the next general meeting.
- Lead the investigation of any incident to root cause, with the output being a corrected procedure, a corrected design, or a corrected training requirement — and follow up to confirm the correction was actually implemented.
- Verify that propellants, chemicals and materials in use are permitted under applicable rules and law; keep the approved-materials list; refuse anything not on it.
- Maintain the inventory of hazardous materials, with quantities, storage conditions and disposal records.
- Verify that the permissions and site approvals required for a launch are in place before the launch is authorised — and refuse to authorise if they are not.
- Keep the first-aid kit, fire extinguishers and eye-wash arrangements stocked, inspected and known to all; ensure the emergency plan names the nearest hospital and the transport arrangement.
- Run a safety review as the first standing item at every Core Team meeting.
- Train a deputy Safety Officer to the same standard. **The Safety Officer's role is the one role where a single point of failure is least acceptable**, because a Club with no Safety Officer may not test or fly.

**Required skills.** Careful, methodical, unembarrassed about stopping things; ability to read a procedure and find the step that will hurt someone; working knowledge of propellant handling, pressure systems, electrical safety and range operations; calm in an emergency; respected enough that people listen, and fair enough that people are not afraid to report.

**Time commitment.** 8–12 hours/week in-semester; full-day presence at every test and launch; on call during any hazardous activity.

**Key deliverables.**
- Written safety procedures for each hazardous activity, current and version-controlled.
- Hazard assessment and go/no-go checklist for every test and launch, filed before the event.
- Induction records for 100% of members who work in the workshop.
- Incident and near-miss reports within 24 hours of every event, and a semester safety summary.
- Approved-materials list, current.
- Semiannual audit of PPE, first aid, extinguishers, storage and disposal.
- Signed successor designation within 30 days; a trained deputy before the first hazardous test of the term.

**Handover pack.**
- Every procedure, checklist and hazard assessment, in the archive.
- The Safety Log and Incident Register, complete and unedited.
- The approved-materials list, with the basis for each entry and the open questions.
- Storage inventory, disposal records, and the location and condition of all safety equipment.
- The emergency plan with the nearest hospital's name, distance and phone number, and the College's emergency contacts.
- The list of members currently inducted, and those who still need induction.
- A candid note on which past procedures were weak, which were written after an incident instead of before it, and what the Club got lucky about.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- **That their authority to halt work does not depend on the Team Lead's agreement.**
- Which activities this Club has never actually done safely, and what would have to change before it attempted them.
- That a stop called in good faith is never a career penalty, and that a club which punishes stops will eventually have a serious incident.
- The exact legal and competition limits the Club works within, and that these must be re-verified each year — rules change.
- Where the paperwork is, because at an incident the last thing anyone should be doing is looking for the hazard assessment.

## 4. PROPULSION LEAD

**Purpose.** To own the design, analysis, fabrication and testing of the Club's propulsion system, strictly within the limits permitted by the applicable rules and by law, and to be the Club's most careful engineer.

**Specific duties.**
- Design and analyse the motor/grain configuration (geometry, burn profile, throat and nozzle, casing and insulation) using established tools and hand calculations, with the analysis recorded in the Design Record.
- Predict performance (thrust curve, total impulse, burn time, chamber pressure, propellant mass, delivered specific impulse estimates) and supply those predictions to the flight performance analysis. Be explicit about uncertainty.
- Prepare and maintain the **static test programme**: test stand requirements, instrumentation (load cell, pressure transducer, sampling rate, calibration), test matrix, procedures, and post-test data analysis.
- Work with the Structures Lead on casing and nozzle structural margins; with the Avionics Lead on instrumentation; with the Safety Officer on every procedure before execution.
- Maintain the propulsion Bill of Materials and the propellant/materials inventory, with quantities, storage, and disposal records.
- Verify that the total impulse class of each vehicle is known and recorded, and that it sits within the Club's permitted scope and any competition class limits.
- Maintain the motor history record: every grain, every pour, every test, with batch data, cure conditions and outcomes.
- Train at least one understudy to be able to run a static test end-to-end before the mid-point of the term.

**Required skills.** Thermodynamics and compressible flow basics; solid propellant fundamentals; instrumentation and data analysis (Python/Excel); machining and composite/thermal fabrication; strict procedural discipline; numeracy sufficient to catch an order-of-magnitude error before it becomes a burst casing.

**Time commitment.** 10–15 hours/week in-semester; substantially more during a test campaign; must attend every test of their hardware.

**Key deliverables.**
- Propulsion design report with performance predictions and margins.
- Static test procedure, instrumentation plan, calibrated data set and post-test analysis for each test.
- Propellant/grain batch records and motor history record.
- Propulsion section of the PDR and CDR packages, and of any competition technical report.
- Propulsion BOM with estimate-versus-actual costs.

**Handover pack.**
- All design files, analysis spreadsheets/scripts, and the reasoning behind each design choice, including rejected options and why they were rejected.
- All static test data in raw form, plus analysis, plus the calibration records of every sensor.
- Test stand drawings, the stand's own limitations, and its known failure points.
- Grain and motor history records.
- The supplier list with part numbers, prices and lead times, and an honest note on which suppliers were reliable.
- The list of things the Lead would do differently, in priority order.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- Which analyses were done properly and which were estimates that got written up as if they were analyses.
- Where the design margins actually are, and which ones are thin.
- The permits, limits and competition class rules the Club operates under, and the fact that these must be re-checked each year.
- That no test is run on hardware whose procedure the Safety Officer has not signed, and no propellant is mixed that is not on the approved list.
- The single most important lesson: a propulsion test that "sort of worked" is a failed test, and the anomaly is the data.

## 5. STRUCTURES LEAD

**Purpose.** To own the airframe, fins, internal structure, recovery-system mounting, materials selection, fabrication quality and structural validation of the vehicle.

**Specific duties.**
- Design the airframe, nose cone, fin can, centring rings, motor mount, bulkheads and internal load paths, with CAD models and drawings under the Club's naming conventions.
- Perform structural analysis: fin flutter, fin attachment loads, body-tube buckling, recovery deployment loads, shock-cord sizing, and a mass and centre-of-gravity budget.
- Own the **mass and CG budget** as a living document, and provide the input to the stability analysis (calibers of stability, CP/CG margin) before every flight.
- Select materials and processes; prepare fabrication drawings; run the fabrication with the members; enforce tolerance and workmanship standards.
- Plan and run structural tests: fin attachment pull tests, tube crush/buckling tests, recovery deployment and shock-load tests, and drop tests as required.
- Own the surface finish, alignment (fin cant, straightness) and dimensional inspection of the flight vehicle, with a written inspection record before flight.
- Maintain the structures BOM, tooling inventory and workshop consumables list.
- Own workshop safety compliance for hand and power tools, jointly with the Safety Officer, and ensure members are trained before using any machine.

**Required skills.** Mechanics of materials; CAD (FreeCAD/Onshape/SolidWorks/Fusion); composite layup and adhesives; hand and power tool competence; measurement and inspection discipline; ability to build to a drawing rather than to a feeling.

**Time commitment.** 10–15 hours/week in-semester; heavy during fabrication weeks and before launches.

**Key deliverables.**
- CAD models, fabrication drawings and an assembly drawing, version-controlled.
- Structural analysis report with margins and assumptions.
- Mass and CG budget, current within a week of any change.
- Inspection record for the flight vehicle before every flight.
- Structural test reports.
- Structures BOM with estimate-versus-actual costs.

**Handover pack.**
- CAD files in the repository (native format plus a neutral export such as STEP/PDF), with the final revision clearly marked.
- Fabrication drawings, jigs and fixture designs, and the tooling itself with a note on where it is stored.
- Analysis files with assumptions documented.
- Mass and CG budget with the final measured masses, and a note on how measured mass compared with estimates (this is the single most useful number for next year's team).
- The inspection record and the list of known non-conformances that were accepted, with the justification.
- Supplier and workshop contacts, and which external machinist or vendor did what.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- Which joints and interfaces failed or nearly failed in testing, and how they were fixed.
- Which measurements were actually measured and which were assumed.
- That the mass budget is the design's most fragile number and that it must be updated the moment a part changes.
- How to get workshop and machine access, who supervises it, and what the College requires before a student may use a machine.
- Which fabrication step was the hardest to get right on the first attempt — usually the answer is fins, alignment, or the fin-to-tube joint.

## 6. AVIONICS LEAD

**Purpose.** To own the vehicle's electronics, software, sensors, data logging, telemetry and recovery electronics — the subsystem on which both the flight data and the safe recovery of the vehicle depend.

**Specific duties.**
- Specify and integrate the flight computer, IMU/barometer/GPS sensors, power system, wiring, connectors and switches, with attention to vibration, thermal environment, and continuity under acceleration.
- Write and maintain the Club's flight software and any ground-station software; version-control it under Git with tagged releases for each flown configuration. **The software revision that flew must be recorded against the flight.**
- Design and validate the recovery electronics: apogee detection, dual deployment logic, timers, backup charges, and continuity checks — with the Safety Officer reviewing all energetics-related logic.
- Design the telemetry/recovery-tracking chain (radio, antenna, ground station, range testing), and run a range test of every tracking system before every launch.
- Build and maintain the ground support equipment: test boxes, bench power, data cables, spare parts, and a labelled inventory.
- Own pre-flight electronics checks: continuity, battery voltage under load, switch states, arming sequence, and a written pre-flight checklist with dual verification.
- Conduct bench tests and hardware-in-the-loop tests of every flight-critical function, with logged results.
- Analyse flight data after each flight and produce a flight data report: what was commanded, what happened, what was anomalous.
- Maintain the avionics BOM, spares inventory and firmware/software release log.

**Required skills.** Embedded electronics (microcontrollers, sensor buses, power budgeting); C/C++ or MicroPython and Python; soldering and wiring to a reliable standard; RF basics; data analysis and plotting; methodical debugging; a habit of documenting pinouts and configurations.

**Time commitment.** 10–15 hours/week in-semester; significant blocks before each launch for bench and range testing.

**Key deliverables.**
- Avionics architecture document, wiring diagram/pinout table, and mass and power budget.
- Version-controlled flight software with a release log; the flown revision identified per flight.
- Bench test and range test reports for every flight-critical function.
- Pre-flight checklist for the avionics and recovery chain.
- Flight data report for every flight, including anomalies.
- Avionics BOM with estimate-versus-actual costs.

**Handover pack.**
- Repository access, the full software repository with tagged releases and a working build/upload procedure that a new member can follow unaided.
- Pinout tables, wiring diagrams, connector conventions, and the calibration values for every sensor.
- Ground station equipment, labelled, with a working end-to-end demonstration before handover.
- Spares inventory with quantities and part numbers; a list of what is consumable and must be re-ordered.
- Logs from every flight, in raw form.
- A note on every bug that was worked around rather than fixed.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- The exact arming and disarming procedure, and why the order matters.
- Which failure modes have actually occurred in flight (they will recur), and the countermeasures.
- That a tracker which has not been range-tested on the day is not a tracker.
- That the flown source revision must be recorded — otherwise the flight data cannot be interpreted and next year's team will repeat the bug.
- Where spares are, because the part that fails is always the one you have no spare for.

## 7. OPERATIONS LEAD

**Purpose.** To make things happen physically: range operations, logistics, scheduling, transport, materials, and the disciplined execution of launch days.

**Specific duties.**
- Own the **launch campaign plan**: site, dates, permissions status, transport, personnel list, roles on the day, equipment manifest, catering/water, and contingency for weather and failure.
- Act as Launch Director on launch days (or appoint one), run the pre-launch briefing, and enforce the range schedule with the Range Safety Officer. **Operational authority on the range rests with the Launch Director; safety authority rests with the RSO. Where they disagree, the RSO prevails.**
- Verify before every campaign that all permissions, landowner consents and intimations are in place and physically present at the site (see *Institutional Approvals and Compliance*). No paperwork, no launch.
- Own logistics: procurement of standard materials and consumables, transport of hardware, storage, tool control, and the packing list for every trip.
- Maintain the equipment register (tools, stands, ground support equipment, PPE, pads, cases) with condition and location, and run a semiannual inventory.
- Maintain the consumables and spares stock so that a test is never cancelled for want of a bolt, a battery or a length of shock cord.
- Own the Club's calendar at the operational level with the Vice President: workshop slots, lab slots, test windows, review dates.
- Run the post-campaign debrief within 72 hours, with a written record of what went wrong operationally.

**Required skills.** Logistics and planning; comfortable with checklists and manifests; calm and decisive under time pressure; good with people on the day; clear verbal communication; willingness to do unglamorous work.

**Time commitment.** 8–12 hours/week in-semester; 12–16 hour days on launch and test days; heavy in the week before each campaign.

**Key deliverables.**
- Written launch campaign plan for every campaign.
- Permissions status sheet, verified and signed off before each campaign.
- Equipment register, current, with a semiannual physical inventory.
- Packing list and post-campaign debrief for every trip.
- Operations BOM/consumables record with estimate-versus-actual costs.

**Handover pack.**
- The campaign plan template and the previous campaigns' plans with the debriefs attached.
- Site dossiers: every site used, with maps, GPS coordinates, access roads, landowner contact, mobile coverage, nearest hospital, distance and travel time, water and shade availability, and known hazards.
- Permission letters and their validity dates, with the renewal lead times.
- Equipment register, with a candid note on what is broken, worn or missing.
- Vendor and transporter contacts with rates.
- The range-day role list and briefing notes.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- That permission paperwork takes weeks, not days, and must be started before the design is finished.
- Which site actually worked, and which one looked good on a map but was unusable.
- The nearest hospital to each site, and the route in both directions.
- That the launch director's job is to keep to the schedule and to be the person who says "we are not flying today".
- How much a campaign actually costs in transport and consumables — the number nobody budgets for.

## 8. TREASURER

**Purpose.** To keep the Club solvent, honest and audit-ready: every rupee recorded, every purchase approved, every sponsor's money accounted for, and a complete bill of materials with estimate-versus-actual costs.

**Specific duties.**
- Maintain the **Financial Ledger**: date, amount, counterparty, purpose, budget line, approver, and receipt reference for every transaction.
- Open and operate the Club bank account with the Team Lead (or Faculty Advisor) as co-signatory; maintain mandates; ensure no single-signatory payments.
- Prepare the annual budget within 30 days of taking office and a revised budget each semester; review the budget against actuals monthly and report variance.
- Process approvals per the Charter's thresholds; refuse payments that lack approval, and escalate refusals to the Team Lead in writing.
- Collect, scan and file every receipt and invoice within 7 days; keep the physical originals in the Club's financial file.
- Maintain the **Bill of Materials with estimate-versus-actual costs** for each vehicle, jointly with the Documentation Lead. This is both sound management and a competition requirement.
- Manage the imprest advance held by the Operations Lead, and reconcile it within 7 days of each event.
- Invoice and acknowledge sponsors and donors promptly and in writing, with the agreed deliverables tracked to completion.
- Prepare for and support the semester audit; present the audit report to the General Body within 30 days of the semester's end.
- Report income, expenditure and balance at every General Body Meeting.
- Know and apply the College's financial and procurement rules, and the rules for handling sponsorship received through the College.
- Track the competition's requirement that teams document a complete bill of materials, and ensure the format matches what the organisers ask for.

**Required skills.** Numeracy and bookkeeping discipline; spreadsheet competence; knowledge of the College's financial processes; integrity under pressure; the willingness to say "there is no budget for that" to a friend.

**Time commitment.** 6–10 hours/week in-semester; heavy at semester boundaries, during procurement pushes and at audit.

**Key deliverables.**
- Annual and semester budgets.
- Financial Ledger, current, with receipts filed.
- BOM with estimate-versus-actual and variance, per vehicle.
- Semester audit report, presented and filed.
- Sponsor acknowledgement letters and a sponsorship register (who, how much, what was promised, what was delivered).
- Monthly financial summary to the Core Team.

**Handover pack.**
- Ledger closed and reconciled to the bank statement, signed by outgoing and incoming Treasurer and the Team Lead.
- Bank mandates updated and signatory changes completed — this is a concrete administrative task with the bank, not a note in a document.
- All receipts, invoices and originals in the financial file, indexed.
- The budget with actuals and an explanation of every material variance.
- The sponsorship register with outstanding deliverables flagged.
- Vendor list with payment terms, GST details where applicable, and reliability notes.
- A note on any pending dues, advances or disputed invoices.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- The exact approval thresholds, and that exceeding them without approval is a disciplinary matter.
- That the Club's money must never sit in a personal account, and why.
- Which budget lines are chronically underestimated (transport, consumables and spares are the usual three).
- How long money takes to actually arrive from the College or a sponsor — cash-flow timing kills campaigns far more often than total budget size.
- That the IN-SPACe competition provides **no funding support** to participating teams, so every rupee must be raised locally.

## 9. DOCUMENTATION LEAD

**Purpose.** To be the guardian of the Club's memory: the archive, the design record, the version control system, the competition documentation, and the handover process. **This role exists because the Club's greatest risk is the graduation cliff.**

**Specific duties.**
- Own the archive: folder structure, naming conventions, access permissions, and the rule that nothing lives on one person's laptop.
- Maintain the **Design Record** — every significant design decision with date, options, rationale, decision-maker and status.
- Own version control: administer the Git organisation/repositories; set the branching and tagging convention; verify that code and documents are actually committed; run a repository health check monthly.
- Maintain the **Succession Register** and the **Critical Knowledge Register** (Charter Article 8), publish them at the start of each semester, and flag RED items to the Steering Committee.
- Administer the handover process: schedule handovers, collect handover packs, verify that each pack meets the Article 8.4 standard, and obtain written acknowledgements.
- Take and circulate minutes of General Body, Core Team and Steering Committee meetings within 72 hours.
- Own the Club's competition documentation: technical report, safety documentation, bill-of-materials formatting, submission packaging, and the institutional approval letter's currency.
- Maintain the approvals file: scan every permission, letter, acknowledgement and receipt of permission, with validity dates and renewal reminders.
- Maintain the member register and skills matrix with the Vice President.
- Own backup: verify the two-location backup and run a documented restore test once per semester.
- Run the annual March Continuity Review with the Team Lead, and the exit interviews with departing members.
- Publish the "what I wish I had known" notes and keep them indexed.

**Required skills.** Documentation discipline and writing clarity; file and information organisation; Git fundamentals; attention to detail; patience with people who "will document it later"; the assertiveness to refuse to close a handover that is incomplete.

**Time commitment.** 8–12 hours/week in-semester; heavy at semester boundaries, at handover season (March–June) and before competition submissions.

**Key deliverables.**
- Archive structure published at the start of each semester, with access granted.
- Design Record, current.
- Succession Register and Critical Knowledge Register, published each semester.
- Minutes of every meeting, circulated within 72 hours and filed.
- Approvals file, current, with a renewal calendar.
- Backup verification report each semester.
- Annual Continuity Review report.
- Complete competition documentation packages, submitted on time.

**Handover pack.**
- Repository administrator access transferred and verified; the naming and versioning convention documented; a repository map explaining where everything lives.
- The archive structure with a short guide: "if you are looking for X, it is in Y".
- The Succession Register and Critical Knowledge Register with the current RED items.
- The approvals file with every validity date and the next renewal due.
- Templates: minutes, test log, incident report, decision record, handover note, BOM, skills matrix.
- A note on which documents are stale, which are known to be wrong, and which were never finished.

**What a successor needs to know.**
- That the archive is the Club's most valuable asset and its only defence against the graduation cliff.
- That a handover without a written note is not a handover, and that they have the authority to refuse to sign one off.
- How to run a restore test, and why the Club does one.
- Where the competition submission templates and past submissions are, since next year's report should start from last year's structure.
- That most documentation failures happen in the last two weeks before a deadline, so the record must be kept continuously, not reconstructed afterwards.

## 10. FACULTY ADVISOR

**Purpose.** To provide institutional oversight, ensure the Club complies with College, legal and competition requirements, and act as the College's point of contact and escalation route — without directing the Club's engineering.

**Specific duties.**
- Verify that the Club's activities comply with College rules and applicable law, and that required permissions are in place before hazardous activities. **The Faculty Advisor may prohibit an activity that is non-compliant or unsafe.**
- Attend and observe key design reviews (at minimum the CDR), static tests and launch campaigns where practicable, or nominate a substitute faculty member and record the nomination.
- Certify documents requiring faculty attestation, including the competition's institutional approval letter, on institutional letterhead signed by the Head of the Institution where required.
- Act as escalation point for member grievances, safety concerns and disciplinary appeals, and receive all incident reports.
- Attest to the Club's compliance record in the annual March Continuity Review.
- Support the Club's access to College resources — labs, workshop, funds, room bookings — by endorsing reasonable requests to the administration.
- Provide continuity across the Club's office-bearer turnover: the Faculty Advisor is often the only person who spans two or three generations of the Club, and should hold the archive copy accordingly (Charter Article 8.7).

**Required skills / qualifications.** A member of UVCE faculty, appointed by the College, with the ability to represent the Club to the administration. Technical familiarity is helpful for judgement but is not the role's purpose.

**Time commitment.** 2–4 hours/month, plus attendance at reviews, tests and launch days where possible, plus availability for escalations.

**Explicit boundaries — what the Faculty Advisor SHALL NOT do.**
- **Shall not make design decisions.** No configuration choices, no sizing, no propellant selection, no component selection.
- **Shall not direct technical recommendations.** If the Advisor offers a technical opinion, it is recorded in the design record as advice, clearly attributed, and the team decides. The Advisor does not instruct.
- **Shall not perform the team's engineering work** or take over a Lead's functions, even to meet a deadline. Rescuing a team by doing its work destroys the learning that justifies the Club's existence.
- **Shall not act as de facto Team Lead** or chair the Club's internal decision-making, except where the College's own processes require it.
- **Shall not use the Advisor position to impose a research agenda**, a project preference, or a student's inclusion for reasons unrelated to the Club's work.

**Key deliverables.**
- Written appointment on record, with name and designation, in the Club's Approvals File and in every competition submission where required.
- Attestation on required documents, in time for deadlines.
- Incident reports acknowledged in writing.
- A short annual note to the College on the Club's compliance and safety record, as part of the March Continuity Review.
- A named interim advisor if unavailable for more than 30 days.

**Handover pack (on change of advisor).** The Club's Constitution and companion documents; the archive copy; the Approvals File; the Incident Register; the current rosters and Succession Register; and a note on the Club's open risks and pending approvals.

**What a successor advisor needs to know.**
- The oversight-only boundary, and that respecting it is what keeps the Club a student-run enterprise.
- The contact list of College officers, and which approvals the Club depends on.
- The distinction between the Advisor's role and that of the Head of Institution, where the two are different people.
- The fact that the Club's most likely failure mode is not an accident but extinction in June.

## 11. ALUMNI MENTOR

**Purpose.** To transfer experience across graduating batches, provide external perspective, open doors to industry and funding, and review the Club's work critically — without doing the Club's engineering for it.

**Specific duties.**
- Review documents and design records on request and ask probing questions — the questions a reviewer will ask, not the answers they should reach.
- Share lessons from their own time, including what failed, what was over-ambitious, and what was learned too late.
- Participate in design reviews as a reviewer, with questions and risk observations recorded in the review minutes.
- Connect the Club to industry, suppliers, sponsors, competition alumni, internships and higher-study opportunities.
- Contribute to the Alumni Fund or to fundraising efforts where able (see *Funding and Sponsorship*).
- Attend the annual March Continuity Review and the AGM where possible, and act as a continuity anchor across generations.
- Offer career and academic guidance to individual members on request.

**Required skills / background.** Former Club member, or an external professional invited by the Core Team with the Faculty Advisor's concurrence, with relevant engineering, industry or entrepreneurial experience.

**Time commitment.** 1–3 hours/month, flexible; higher during design review season and competition preparation.

**Explicit boundaries — what Alumni Mentors SHALL NOT do.**
- **Shall not provide design recommendations.** No prescription of configuration, formulation, sizing, material, component or recovery design. The role is Socratic: ask what was considered, what the margins are, what happens if it fails, what the evidence is.
- **Shall not dictate solutions or do the design work**, even where they could do it faster and better. A mentor who designs the rocket teaches the team nothing and creates a dependency that breaks the moment the mentor is busy.
- **Shall not hold a vote**, hold an office, or sign off on safety or financial matters.
- **Shall not bypass the current team** by instructing members directly or resolving disputes with the Team Lead's authority.
- **Shall not use the relationship for commercial gain**, or steer the Club to a supplier for personal benefit.
- Where advice is given, it is recorded in the design record as advice, attributed, and the decision remains the team's.

**Key deliverables.**
- Review comments at PDR/CDR and on submitted documents, recorded in the review minutes.
- At least one "lessons from my time" note for the archive per year, if willing — these become part of the Club's institutional memory.
- Introductions to at least one new contact (industry, sponsor, mentor, institution) per year where able.
- Availability for the March Continuity Review.

**Handover pack.** Not applicable in the usual sense; instead, Alumni Mentors contribute a standing written note to the archive: what they did, what broke, what they would do differently, and who else the Club should talk to.

**What a new Alumni Mentor needs to know.**
- The boundaries above, and that the Club's competence depends on the current members making — and owning — the decisions.
- The current state of the Club's designs and documentation, so that their questions are relevant.
- The safety and permissions framework in force today, which may be stricter than when they were a member.
- The Club's non-negotiables: no copying, no outsourcing, no restricted chemicals, honesty in data.

## 13. TIME COMMITMENT SUMMARY

| Role | Routine (hrs/week) | Peak (hrs/week) | Peak periods |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team Lead | 10–15 | 20–25 | Competition submission, launch campaigns, elections |
| Vice President | 8–12 | 16–20 | Onboarding, deadline weeks |
| Safety Officer | 8–12 | 16+ (full days on range) | Every test and launch |
| Propulsion Lead | 10–15 | 20–25 | Static test campaigns |
| Structures Lead | 10–15 | 20–25 | Fabrication and pre-launch |
| Avionics Lead | 10–15 | 20–25 | Bench/range testing, pre-launch |
| Operations Lead | 8–12 | 16–20 (12–16 hr days) | Campaign weeks |
| Treasurer | 6–10 | 15 | Budget, procurement push, audit |
| Documentation Lead | 8–12 | 18–22 | Semester ends, handover season, submissions |
| Faculty Advisor | ~1 (2–4/month) | Event days | Reviews, tests, launches |
| Alumni Mentor | ~0.5 (1–3/month) | Review weeks | PDR, CDR, competition |

**Guidance on overload.** A member holding a technical Lead role while also carrying a heavy academic load is a retention risk and a safety risk. The Core Team shall monitor workload at the weekly meeting, and the Vice President shall act when a member exceeds their stated commitment for more than three consecutive weeks — the remedy is redistribution of work, not exhortation to work harder. **No member should be asked to choose between the Club and their degree; the Club loses that contest every time, and should not create it.**
