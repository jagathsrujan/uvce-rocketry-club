## ⚠️ READ THIS FIRST — STATUS OF THIS DOCUMENT

> **This document is awareness guidance written for a student club. It is not legal advice, and it does not create or confirm any legal permission.**
>
> 1. **Rules change.** Indian space-sector regulation, IN-SPACe advisory guidance, PESO rules, police and district administration procedures, and competition rulebooks are all subject to revision. **The Club must verify current requirements directly with the relevant authority before every activity**, and must not rely on this document as a substitute for that verification.
> 2. **The Club's scope is model rocketry and student sub-orbital work only**, and only within the limits summarised in Section 5. **For any activity beyond that — higher impulse classes, custom or high-mass propellants, larger vehicles, any launch exceeding the thresholds described, or anything the Club is unsure about — the Club must seek proper guidance from IN-SPACe, the district administration and qualified advisers before proceeding.** This is not optional and it is not a formality.
> 3. **Where this document states a number or process, treat it as a prompt to verify, not as an authority.** Where the Club cannot get a clear answer, the answer is "do not proceed yet", and the Club writes down the question, who it asked, and when.
> 4. **Nothing the Club does may imply government or institutional endorsement.** The Club is a student body operating with the College's recognition and, where required, with permissions from the competent authorities.
> 5. **When in doubt, ask.** Approaching the district administration early and honestly has repeatedly proven to be the difference between a club that flies and a club that is shut down. Section 7 explains why proactive notification is in the Club's interest.

## 1. OVERVIEW — THE APPROVAL LANDSCAPE

A student rocketry club in Karnataka may need approvals, permissions, intimations or acknowledgements at five levels. Not all apply to every activity, and the applicable set depends on what is being done, where, and with what.

| Level | Authority | What it covers | Typical instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Institutional** | UVCE — Student Council, Head of Department, Principal / Head of Institution | Club recognition; faculty advisor; lab and workshop access; use of College name; institutional approval letter for competitions | Recognition letter; appointment letter; access permissions; letter on institutional letterhead |
| **2. Site / land** | Landowner (private, institutional, or government) | Permission to enter, occupy, set up equipment, and conduct tests or launches on a site | Written landowner permission letter |
| **3. District administration** | District Collector / Deputy Commissioner; Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM / Assistant Commissioner); jurisdictional police | Permission or intimation for conducting launches in the district; public safety; law-and-order clearance | Written permission, acknowledgement, or receipt of intimation |
| **4. National space framework** | IN-SPACe (Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre), Department of Space | Authorisation or advisory for sub-orbital launches and model rocketry above defined thresholds | Authorisation, advisory, or intimation acknowledgement |
| **5. Explosives / hazardous materials** | PESO (Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation) and, where applicable, State licensing authorities | Use, storage and transport of explosives and certain hazardous materials; where custom or high-mass propellants are involved, a clearance or no-objection may be sought | Licence, clearance, or no-objection |

**Practical sequencing rule:** start the institutional and site processes **at least eight weeks** before a planned activity, and the district and national processes **as early as the activity is even tentatively scheduled**. Approvals are the longest-lead item in any campaign, longer than fabrication and longer than fundraising. **A permission that arrives after the launch date is not a permission.**

## 2. COLLEGE-LEVEL APPROVALS

### 2.1 Official club recognition

**Why it matters.** Recognition is what turns a group of students into a body that can hold a bank account (or use the College's), book rooms and labs, receive funds, use the College's name in a competition entry, and survive a change of office-bearers. An unrecognised club is a personal project with an expiry date.

**Process (verify each step against current UVCE practice — procedures vary by institution and change over time):**

1. **Draft and adopt the Constitution** (`Club_Charter.md`) at an inaugural meeting, with founding members signing Annexure B.
2. **Identify a Faculty Advisor** willing to serve, and obtain their informal concurrence before approaching the Student Council. An application without a willing advisor generally stalls.
3. **Prepare the recognition application pack:**
 - Cover letter to the Student Council / Dean (Student Welfare) / Principal, as the College's process requires;
 - the Club Constitution, with the founding members' names, institute IDs, branches and semesters;
 - the list of proposed office-bearers for the coming year;
 - the Faculty Advisor's consent and a short CV/designation note;
 - a one-page statement of purpose, planned activities for the year, and how the Club differs from existing bodies;
 - a statement of safety policy, referencing Article 4 of the Constitution and the hazardous-materials policy;
 - a statement of resource requirements: a meeting room, workshop/lab access, storage space, and any funding request;
 - an undertaking to comply with College rules, anti-ragging rules, and applicable law.
4. **Submit to the Student Council** through the prescribed channel and maintain a dated acknowledgement copy of everything submitted. Keep a scanned copy the same day.
5. **Attend the Student Council meeting** if called, with the Team Lead presenting and the Faculty Advisor present or available.
6. **Obtain written recognition** naming the Club, the Faculty Advisor and the office-bearers, with any conditions stated.

**Approximate timeline:** 3–8 weeks, sometimes a full semester if the academic calendar intervenes. **Start in the first month of the academic year.**

**Common reasons recognition is delayed or refused:**
- No willing faculty advisor.
- Constitution missing a safety clause, a financial control clause, or a dissolution clause (all three are in `Club_Charter.md` deliberately).
- No clarity on what the Club will actually do, or an unrealistically ambitious plan presented as fact.
- Overlap or friction with an existing student body.
- A request for resources without justification, or with no plan for how they will be used safely.
- Activities proposed that the College considers beyond a student body's competence or risk appetite. **Present model rocketry as the starting scope, with progression explicitly subject to approvals — this is both honest and far more likely to be approved than a proposal to build large rockets in year one.**

### 2.2 Student Council process — practical notes

- Know the calendar: most student councils have an annual window for new-club applications and a review cycle. Missing the window costs a year. **Find out the window before writing anything.**
- Identify a sympathetic senior faculty member or an existing club office-bearer who can explain how the council actually works in practice, not just on paper.
- Present with a demonstration, not just a document. If the Club has flown even a small model rocket, show the video and the flight data. A concrete artefact moves a council more than a proposal does.
- Ask for less than you want in the first application, and ask precisely. "One room, one evening a fortnight, and workshop access under supervision for four members" is grantable; "a dedicated lab and ₹5 lakh" is not, in year one.
- **Get the recognition conditions in writing**, and diarise any reporting obligations (annual reports, activity reports, account statements). Failing to file a routine report is a common and entirely avoidable cause of losing recognition.

### 2.3 Faculty Advisor appointment letter

**The Club should obtain a written appointment** — on College letterhead or as a formal office order — that records:

- the Faculty Advisor's name, designation and department;
- the Club's name and the academic year;
- that the appointment is in an **oversight and compliance capacity**, consistent with Clause 6.4 of the Club Constitution and with the competition framework, and that the Advisor does not make design decisions or direct technical recommendations;
- the Advisor's role in certifying documents, receiving incident reports, and acting as the escalation point;
- the date of appointment and its validity.

**Why written matters:** competition submissions and any institutional letter may require the advisor to be named and identifiable. A verbal arrangement evaporates when the faculty member goes on sabbatical, and it cannot be produced at a desk when an official asks who supervises this club.

**Also obtain, or at least record:** the name of a **nominated substitute** if the Advisor is unavailable for more than 30 days (Constitution Clause 6.4(d)).

### 2.4 Request for lab and workshop access

**What to ask for, specifically:**

| Resource | Requested form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting room | Fortnightly slot, fixed day and time, for the academic year | A fixed slot is worth more than a large room |
| Workshop access | Supervised access for named, safety-inducted members, during stated hours | Never request unsupervised access for a new club; it will be refused and it should be |
| Machine use | Named machines (drill press, lathe, saw, sander) with the department's supervision or after a training sign-off | Colleges have liability rules about student machine use; comply with them exactly |
| Storage | A locked cupboard or shelf for tools, materials and PPE | Without storage, tools walk and the Club re-buys them every semester |
| Lab space | Bench space for avionics work, with power and network | A corner of an existing lab is usually sufficient and far easier to obtain |
| Testing space | A safe outdoor area on or near campus for very low-power static/bench work, if permitted | Many colleges prohibit this entirely; do not assume, and do not do it quietly |
| Electrical and charging | Permission to charge LiPo and other batteries in a designated, fire-safe location | Battery charging is a genuine fire risk; ask for a proper place and use a LiPo-safe bag |

**How to frame the request:** a one-page request, signed by the Faculty Advisor, stating (a) exactly what is needed, (b) for how many named members, (c) what safety training those members have completed, (d) what the Club will do to protect the equipment and the premises, and (e) what the College gets in return — competition participation, project exhibitions, outreach, and documentation the department can cite.

**Conditions the Club should expect and accept:** an inventory signed by a responsible member; supervision requirements; restricted hours; a prohibition on storing hazardous materials on campus; and a prohibition on any energetic-material work inside College premises. **The Club's Constitution should be consistent with these constraints: propellant work, if any, is done at a permitted external site under a written procedure, not in a College lab.**

## 3. THE INSTITUTIONAL APPROVAL LETTER — WHY IT MATTERS

### 3.1 What it is

For the **IN-SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition**, the Club is required to submit an **approval letter on institutional letterhead, signed by the Head of the Institution**, listing **every team member's name, institute ID, course, branch, year of graduation and role**. Treat this as one of the Club's most important documents of the year: **without it, the team cannot compete, however good the rocket is.**

**Verify the current year's exact requirements in the competition rulebook and with the organisers.** The content and format may change year to year, and the Club should confirm:
- the signatory required (Head of Institution / Principal / Registrar / Dean);
- the exact fields required per member;
- whether the letter must be dated within a stated window before submission;
- whether a scanned copy, a hard copy, or both are required;
- whether the Faculty Advisor must also attest;
- whether the letter must be on letterhead with seal, and whether an email submission is acceptable.

### 3.2 Why it matters beyond the competition

- It is institutional acknowledgement that the team exists and is acting with the College's knowledge — the single most useful document the Club can hold if anyone ever questions what these students are doing.
- It establishes the College's awareness of the activity, which is essential to the Club's standing on campus.
- It provides the paper trail connecting the team to the institution, which supports access to funds, labs and equipment.
- It forces the Club to maintain an **accurate, current roster** — which is itself good practice, and which the Succession Register and member register in `Recruitment_and_Onboarding.md` should already provide.

### 3.3 How to obtain it without a crisis

1. **Build the roster early and keep it current.** Name, institute ID, course, branch, year of graduation and role for every member. Keep it in one controlled document; do not reconstruct it from WhatsApp history the week before the deadline.
2. **Draft the letter yourself** and hand the draft to the office with the roster attached as a table. Offices are far more likely to sign a well-prepared draft than to compose one.
3. **Approach the office at least four weeks before the deadline**, in person, with the Faculty Advisor's support and a covering note explaining what the competition is and what the letter is for.
4. **Expect revision requests** — on the roster, on the wording, on who signs. Leave time for two rounds.
5. **Collect it in person where possible**, check every member's name, ID, branch and year of graduation against the roster before leaving the office, and obtain the sealed/stamped version.
6. **Scan it immediately**, file it in the Approvals File, and record the issue date and the deadline it serves.
7. **Re-issue if the roster changes** after the letter is issued. A letter listing a member who has left, or omitting someone who joined, is a discrepancy that can be questioned.

**Failure mode to avoid:** discovering in the final week that the Head of Institution is travelling, or that the office requires a week's notice for letterhead correspondence. **Start a month early, every year, without exception.**

## 4. LANDOWNER PERMISSION FOR A TEST SITE

### 4.1 Why written permission is essential

Nearly every launch or static test needs a piece of land that the Club does not own. Verbal permission from a caretaker, a friendly farmer or a security guard is **not** adequate: it can be withdrawn mid-campaign, it will not survive a change of guard, and it provides no defence if something lands on property, injures a person, or attracts the attention of the police. **Written permission, on paper, signed, dated and carried to the site, is the standard the Club must hold.**

### 4.2 What a written permission letter must contain

The Club should prepare a draft letter and ask the landowner (or their authorised representative) to sign it. Minimum contents:

1. **Land ownership details**
 - Name of the owner or lawful occupier;
 - designation if the land belongs to an institution, trust, company or government body, and the name and designation of the person authorised to grant permission;
 - survey number(s) / khasra / plot number(s), village, hobli, taluk, district;
 - approximate extent of the area permitted (in acres/guntas or square metres), with a sketch or map if possible;
 - contact details (phone, address) of the owner or their representative on site.
2. **Purpose**
 - a plain-language statement that permission is granted for **student educational rocketry activity**, including (as applicable) static testing of solid rocket motors, launch of model rockets, recovery of flown hardware, and setup of ground equipment;
 - a statement of the **expected maximum motor impulse class or total impulse**, expressed in Newton-seconds, so that the landowner knowingly permits the actual activity and not something vaguer or larger;
 - a statement that the activity is non-commercial, educational and conducted by students of UVCE.
3. **Validity dates**
 - a **start date and an end date** (not "until further notice");
 - the specific permitted dates or date ranges, or a stated window within which the Club may notify specific days;
 - a stated advance-notice requirement (e.g. "the Club shall intimate the owner at least 7 days before each use").
4. **Specific activities permitted**
 - what may be done: entering the land, clearing a small area, positioning launch rails/pads and test stands, using generators, erecting a safety perimeter, recovering hardware, and removing the Club's own equipment and debris;
 - what is **not** permitted: digging, burning, cutting vegetation, leaving materials behind, use of the land by anyone other than the named team, sub-letting to third parties;
 - the **specific hours** permitted (e.g. 06:00–11:00) and a prohibition on activity outside them;
 - the **maximum number of persons** permitted on site, and a requirement that the Club provide a list of participants.
5. **Safety and liability acknowledgements**
 - the Club's undertaking to conduct the activity under a written safety procedure, with a designated Range Safety Officer, an exclusion zone, firefighting equipment and first-aid provision;
 - the Club's undertaking to restore the site and remove all debris, and to compensate for any damage caused by the Club's activity, as agreed;
 - confirmation that the landowner's permission does not imply any endorsement of the activity's safety, and that the Club bears responsibility for conducting it safely;
 - a statement that the Club holds the applicable institutional approvals and, where required, has notified the district administration (attach copies or reference numbers).
6. **Signatures**
 - signature of the owner or authorised signatory, with name, designation, date and (if available) seal;
 - signature of the Team Lead on behalf of the Club, and countersignature or acknowledgement by the **Faculty Advisor** where the College's process requires it;
 - two copies signed, one retained by each party.

### 4.3 Additional site rules the Club should apply to itself

- **Never test or launch on a site the Club has not physically visited and walked in daylight**, with the recovery area and downrange assessed.
- **Always carry the original permission letter to the site**, along with copies of any district or police intimation and the institutional approvals.
- **Inform the local police station** of the activity, even where permission rests with the landowner, if the activity involves anything that could be mistaken for a hazard or a weapon, or if the site is near a village, road or habitation. A five-minute conversation prevents a much longer one.
- **Never launch over a road, railway, habitation, water body used by people, livestock, or standing crops.** Never launch into airspace near an airport, military area, or restricted zone. Check for nearby aerodromes and restricted airspace before selecting a site.
- **Respect the landowner's other interests**: livestock, crops, hunting seasons, rituals, and the local community's calendar all matter. Ask.
- **Leave the site cleaner than you found it.** The Club will want to come back next year.

### 4.4 If the site is government land

Government land, forest land, revenue land, or land under a public authority requires a different process, typically involving the district administration or the relevant department, and can take considerably longer. **Do not assume a local official's verbal nod is sufficient.** Route the request formally, through the Faculty Advisor and the College, and verify the current procedure with the district office. If the process cannot be completed in time, **select a different site rather than proceed.**

## 5. NATIONAL FRAMEWORK — IN-SPACe GUIDELINES ON SUB-ORBITAL LAUNCHES AND MODEL ROCKETRY

### 5.1 What this section is

This section summarises, in the Club's own words and for awareness only, the advisory framework published by IN-SPACe for sub-orbital launches and model rocketry, as understood at the time of writing. **It is a summary for planning purposes and is not a legal document. The Club must read the current published guidelines in full, on the IN-SPACe website, before relying on them, and must confirm the current position with IN-SPACe and the district administration for the specific activity proposed.**

### 5.2 The impulse thresholds — the key planning numbers

The framework is organised around the **combined total impulse** of the activity, expressed in **Newton-seconds (N·s)**.

| Combined total impulse | What the framework indicates | Club action (verify current requirements) |
|---|---|---|
| **Below 10,000 N·s** | **No authorisation is required** under the advisory framework for model rocketry in this band. | Inform the **local administration** about the activity. Follow all institutional, site and safety requirements. Maintain records. |
| **Between 10,000 and 40,960 N·s** | **At least 30 days' prior intimation** should be given, and an **advisory** should be obtained. | Submit intimation at least 30 days in advance; obtain the advisory; keep the acknowledgement in the Approvals File; carry it to the site. |
| **Below 40,960 N·s combined total impulse** | **Does not require IN-SPACe authorisation.** | This is the ceiling of the Club's permitted scope under this framework. **The Club shall not plan any activity whose combined total impulse approaches or exceeds this limit.** |
| **At or above 40,960 N·s** | **Outside the model-rocketry band described here; authorisation would be required.** | **Out of scope for the Club.** Do not proceed without proper guidance from IN-SPACe and qualified advisers, and without the College's explicit approval. |

> **Planning rule for the Club:** state the combined total impulse of every activity in writing before it is approved, keep it well inside the applicable band, and treat the 40,960 N·s figure as a hard ceiling that the Club plans nowhere near. **If the Club is uncertain which band an activity falls into, it is in the higher band until an authority says otherwise.**

### 5.3 Local administration approval — always required

**Independent of the impulse thresholds, launches require approval from the local administration.** Depending on the district and the nature of the activity, this means the **District Collector / Deputy Commissioner**, the **Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) / Assistant Commissioner**, or another **designated authority**, and in practice often involves the **jurisdictional police station** as well.

The Club should:
1. **Identify the correct authority** for the proposed site. This is a district-specific question — ask the local Taluk office or the district office directly, and record who said what.
2. **Apply in writing, well in advance**, with the Club's identity, the Faculty Advisor's and College's details, the site with survey numbers, the dates, the nature of the activity, the impulse class, the safety arrangements, the number of participants, and the institutional approvals already obtained.
3. **Attach the institutional approval and the landowner's permission** to the application.
4. **Follow up in person** at the office; a polite visit to the concerned desk resolves most delays.
5. **Obtain the permission or acknowledgement in writing.** Where the authority's practice is to acknowledge rather than permit, obtain the acknowledgement with a reference number and date.
6. **Carry the original to the site**, together with the landowner's letter and the institutional approvals, and be ready to produce them.

**Note:** procedures, nomenclature (Collector / Deputy Commissioner / DC), and practice vary between districts and states, and change from time to time. **Verify with the specific district office.** Do not assume that a process that worked in one district will work in another.

### 5.4 PESO clearance or no-objection, where applicable

Where the activity involves **custom propellants, high-mass propellants, or any explosive or hazardous material within the meaning of the applicable explosives rules**, a **clearance or no-objection from the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO)** may be required, in addition to the space-sector and district approvals. Storage, transport and handling of such materials carry their own requirements, including licensed premises and qualified personnel.

**The Club's position:**
- **The Club's baseline is to avoid this category entirely** — that is, to work only with commercially manufactured model rocket motors and materials, within the permitted impulse bands, at approved sites, under written procedures. This keeps the Club within a scope that students can responsibly manage.
- **The Club shall not procure, store, handle or use restricted, controlled or explosive-licensed materials** (Constitution Clause 9.2(c)).
- **If the Club ever contemplates anything in this category, it shall first seek proper guidance from PESO, IN-SPACe and qualified advisers, and shall not proceed without the College's explicit written approval and the required licences.** This is a decision for the institution, not for students to take on their own.
- **Where a no-objection is sought**, keep the correspondence and the response in the Approvals File, whether the answer is yes, no, or "not applicable".

### 5.5 Other national-framework points worth knowing

- **Airspace.** Model rocketry activities should be kept clear of airports, aerodromes, helipads, military areas, restricted and prohibited airspace, and notified zones. Verify the position of the nearest airfield before selecting a site. Any activity that could interfere with aviation is a serious matter.
- **Recovery.** The Club is responsible for recovering its hardware and for anything it lands on. Plan recovery before launch, brief the recovery team, and obtain permission for access to adjacent land where needed.
- **Maritime and border areas.** Launching near the coast, in coastal regulation zones, or near international borders involves additional authorities. Avoid these areas as a student club.
- **Reporting.** Where an activity is intimated or authorised, comply with any reporting or post-activity intimation requirement, and file the report in the Approvals File.
- **Public communication.** Do not describe the Club's work in terms that suggest a commercial launch service, a weapons application, or a capability the Club does not have. Be accurate, modest and factual in all public communication (Constitution Clause 14.5).

## 6. DISTRICT ADMINISTRATION — WHEN AND HOW TO NOTIFY

### 6.1 When

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Any launch, of any impulse class, at any site | Notify / seek permission from the local administration as described in Section 5.3. **Always.** |
| An activity in the 10,000–40,960 N·s band | 30 days' prior intimation **and** an advisory, **plus** the local administration process. Both, not either. |
| Static tests that do not involve flight | Local administration permission is not normally the governing requirement, but the site permission and College approvals are. **Notify the landowner, inform the local police station if the activity could be mistaken for a hazard, and check whether the district requires intimation for static testing with energetic materials.** If unsure, ask, and record the answer. |
| A demonstration flight for recruitment or outreach, including on campus or at a school | Same as any launch. A demo flight is a launch. It is also a public event, which raises the stakes rather than lowering them. |
| A change of site, date or impulse class after permission is granted | **Re-apply or re-intimate.** A permission is specific to what was described in it. |
| Anything the Club is unsure about | Ask the district office in writing and record the answer. **Asking early has never harmed a student club; launching without permission has ended several.** |

### 6.2 How — practical steps

1. **Find the right desk.** Ask at the Taluk office or the district office which officer handles permissions for such activities. Get the officer's designation and the correct submission channel (physical application, e-office, or both).
2. **Write a formal application** on the Club's letterhead (or through the College), addressed to the officer by designation. Contents: who the Club is; the College and Faculty Advisor; the site with survey numbers and a location map; the dates and times; the nature of the activity; the impulse class; the expected number of participants and spectators; the safety arrangements (RSO, exclusion zone, firefighting, first aid, emergency plan); the institutional and landowner approvals obtained; and the request.
3. **Attach everything**: institutional approval, Faculty Advisor's note, landowner permission, safety plan, participant list, and a copy of the Club's Constitution's safety clause if it helps establish credibility.
4. **Follow up in person, politely, more than once.** Keep a log: date, officer met, what was said, what was asked for.
5. **Obtain the permission or acknowledgement in writing**, with a reference number if one is issued.
6. **Inform the jurisdictional police station** separately, in person, with a copy of the permission and a contact number for the day. Give them the date, time, location and duration, and the name of the person in charge on site.
7. **Carry the originals to the site** and be ready to produce them.
8. **After the activity, send a short courtesy report** to the same officer: what was done, when, the outcome, and thanks. **This single habit is the most effective thing the Club can do to make next year's permission easier.**

### 6.3 Why proactive notification is worth the effort

- **It converts a potential incident into a routine activity.** An unannounced rocket launch near a village produces a police complaint, a frightened public and a College inquiry. An intimated launch produces, at worst, a phone call asking what time it finishes.
- **It builds an official record of the Club as a responsible body.** Officers change postings, but files remain. A Club with three years of clean correspondence is treated very differently from one appearing for the first time.
- **It protects individual members.** If something goes wrong, the first question asked is "who permitted this?" A permission letter answers it.
- **It protects the College**, which matters enormously for the Club's continued existence. An institution that is embarrassed by a student club will withdraw its support.
- **It is the standard the Club should want to meet.** The Club is asking to be trusted with energetic hardware. Being visibly, boringly correct about paperwork is exactly how that trust is earned.
- **It makes the competition possible.** Competition organisers and the College both look for evidence that the team operates within a framework. Documentation of past permissions is that evidence.

## 7. THE CLUB'S OWN SCOPE POLICY (SELF-IMPOSED)

To keep the Club safely inside the framework, and to make decisions quickly without ambiguity, the Club adopts the following scope policy. **It is deliberately more conservative than the framework's ceiling.**

1. **The Club's baseline activity is model rocketry using commercially manufactured motors**, within the lowest impulse band, at approved sites, under written procedures and with local administration informed.
2. **The Club shall state the combined total impulse of every activity in writing** before approval, and shall keep it well within the applicable band.
3. **The Club shall treat 40,960 N·s as a hard ceiling and shall plan nowhere near it.** Reaching the intimation band requires the Steering Committee's resolution, the Faculty Advisor's acknowledgement, 30 days' intimation and an advisory, and the district permission, all in the Approvals File before the activity.
4. **The Club shall not undertake custom propellant development** in its early years. Any move toward it requires the Faculty Advisor's acknowledgement, the College's written approval, a written safety case, verification of the applicable PESO position, and a decision taken by the institution — not by students alone.
5. **The Club shall not conduct any hazardous activity on College premises.** Energetic materials are handled only at an external permitted site.
6. **The Club shall not launch without every applicable permission in hand.** "The permission is coming" is not a permission.
7. **The Club shall not conceal an activity from the College or the authorities**, ever, for any reason, including to meet a competition deadline. **A missed deadline is recoverable. A concealed incident is not.**
8. **The Safety Officer may refuse to authorise any activity on compliance grounds**, and that refusal stands until the deficiency is remedied in writing.

### 8.5 Renewal calendar

The Documentation Lead shall maintain a **single-page renewal calendar** showing, for each approval, the expiry date, the required renewal lead time, and the date by which the application must be made. It shall be reviewed at the first Core Team meeting of every month, and any item within 45 days of expiry shall be escalated to the Team Lead in writing. **Most approval failures are not refusals; they are expiries that nobody was watching.**

### 8.6 Handover of compliance records

The compliance file is a mandatory element of the Team Lead's, Safety Officer's, Operations Lead's and Documentation Lead's handover packs (Constitution Article 8.4). Where an approval will still be valid after a handover, the incoming holder must be able to name it, find it, and state its expiry date before the handover is signed off. **A handover in which the successor cannot locate the current district permission is an incomplete handover.**

## 9. QUICK-REFERENCE CHECKLIST — BEFORE ANY TEST OR LAUNCH

- [ ] Combined total impulse calculated in writing, and the applicable band identified (Section 5.2)
- [ ] Club recognition in force, and Faculty Advisor appointed and named
- [ ] Institutional approval letter current, with an accurate roster
- [ ] Landowner written permission in hand, valid for the date, covering the actual activity and impulse class
- [ ] District administration permission or acknowledgement in hand (Section 5.3)
- [ ] IN-SPACe intimation submitted at least 30 days prior **and** advisory obtained, if in the 10,000–40,960 N·s band
- [ ] PESO position verified, if any custom or high-mass propellant is contemplated
- [ ] Police station informed, with the officer's name and the day's contact number
- [ ] Hazard assessment, written procedure, and go/no-go checklist signed by the Safety Officer
- [ ] Range Safety Officer and Launch Director named; roles briefed
- [ ] Exclusion zone defined; spectators briefed; access controlled
- [ ] Firefighting equipment, first-aid kit, and the nearest hospital's name, route and number known to all
- [ ] Weather and wind limits stated, with a named person empowered to cancel
- [ ] Recovery plan and recovery team briefed; access to adjacent land arranged if needed
- [ ] Original permission documents physically on site, and scanned copies in the archive
- [ ] Participants list recorded, with attendance
- [ ] Post-activity report and courtesy note to the authorities scheduled
- [ ] Any deviation from the approved plan identified and, if material, the activity halted and re-approved

**If any box is unticked, the activity does not proceed.** There is no version of this where a competition deadline justifies an unticked box.

## 10. TEN RULES TO REMEMBER

1. **Verify everything, every year.** This document is awareness guidance, not authority.
2. **Approvals are the longest-lead item in any campaign.** Start eight weeks early; start the district and national processes as soon as an activity is even tentatively planned.
3. **The institutional approval letter is mandatory for the competition** and must list every member's name, ID, course, branch, year of graduation and role, signed by the Head of Institution on letterhead.
4. **40,960 N·s is the ceiling, not a target.** Below 10,000 N·s, no authorisation is required but the local administration must be informed; between 10,000 and 40,960 N·s, give 30 days' prior intimation and obtain an advisory.
5. **Launches always require local administration approval**, whatever the impulse class — Collector, SDM, or the designated authority, and in practice the police as well.
6. **Where custom or high-mass propellants are involved, a PESO clearance or no-objection may be required.** The Club's baseline is to avoid this category entirely.
7. **Landowner permission must be written, specific, dated and carried to the site.**
8. **Notify the district proactively.** It converts a potential incident into a routine activity, and it is the cheapest insurance the Club can buy.
9. **For anything beyond model rocketry, seek proper guidance from IN-SPACe and qualified advisers, and obtain the College's explicit approval before proceeding.**
10. **File and scan everything the day it is received.** An approval nobody can find is an approval the Club does not have.
