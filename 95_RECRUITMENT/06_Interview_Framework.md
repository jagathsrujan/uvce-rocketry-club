# Stage 6 — Interview Framework

**15 minutes. Structured. Looking for commitment, not knowledge.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **When** | Week 4 |
| **Duration** | 15 minutes strict |
| **Panel** | **Two people minimum.** Never interview alone. |
| **Marked out of** | 20 (combined with the pre-interview score to rank) |

---

## What the interview is actually for

By this stage you already know:

- ✅ They complete tasks without reminders (Stages 2–5)
- ✅ They show up in person (Stage 3)
- ✅ They read carefully (test)
- ✅ They can articulate a view (essay)

**What you do not know yet:**

| Unknown | What answers it |
|---|---|
| Will they actually stay past month two? | Evidence of past follow-through |
| Do they handle not knowing gracefully? | How they respond to a question they cannot answer |
| Do they take safety seriously, or is it boilerplate? | How they talk about the safety content |
| Will they work well with the others? | Their behaviour in the doubt session, and here |
| Do they want *this*, or just *a club*? | Why they picked this over alternatives |

> ⚠️ **Do not test technical knowledge.** They have none, and testing for it selects for confidence
> rather than capability. You will miss the quiet person who would have been your best propulsion
> lead.

---

## Structure (15 minutes)

| Time | Section | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **0–2 min** | Introduction | Put them at ease. Explain the format. |
| **2–5 min** | Follow-through | Have they finished things before? |
| **5–8 min** | Handling difficulty | What do they do when stuck? |
| **8–11 min** | Safety disposition | Do they take it seriously? |
| **11–13 min** | Their essay | One specific question from what they wrote |
| **13–15 min** | Their questions | What do *they* want to know? |

---

## Questions by section

### 1. Follow-through

**Pick one or two.**

- *"Tell me about something you started and finished that took more than a few weeks. What kept you
  going?"*
- *"Have you ever joined something and then stopped going? What happened?"*
  → **This is the better question.** Everyone has quit something. Someone who can explain *why*
  honestly is more self-aware than someone who claims they never quit.
- *"What else are you involved in right now?"*
  → Listen for the total load. Someone in five clubs will not have time for this one.

### 2. Handling difficulty

- *"Tell me about a time you got properly stuck on something. What did you do?"*
- *"If you spent three Saturdays building something and it failed every time, what would you do on the
  fourth?"*
- **Then deliberately ask something they cannot answer:**
  - *"Do you know what a de Laval nozzle is?"*
  - **Watch what they do.** The good response is *"No — what is it?"* or *"I think it's something to
    do with the exhaust, but I'm not sure."* The bad response is guessing confidently, or being
    visibly embarrassed and shutting down.

> 🔴 **This is the single most informative moment in the interview.** In rocketry, you will not know
> things constantly. Someone who can say "I don't know" calmly is worth more than someone who cannot.

### 3. Safety disposition

- *"The reader said a CATO is almost never one failure — it's several barriers failing at once. Does
  that match how you think about risk?"*
- *"Suppose you're at a test and you notice someone skipped a checklist step. Nobody was hurt and the
  test worked. What do you do?"*
  → **The right answer interrupts the process, or at least reports it.** *"It worked, so it's fine"*
  is a serious flag.
- *"Have you ever been in a situation where you had to follow a safety rule you thought was
  unnecessary?"*

> ⚠️ **Weight this heavily.** A candidate who is strong everywhere else but treats safety as
> bureaucracy is a liability to the club, not an asset.

### 4. Their essay

Pull **one specific line** and ask about it. For example:

> *"You wrote that you're worried you'll lose interest when things fail. Our first motor test will
> almost certainly fail. How do you think you'll handle that?"*

**Why:** it proves you read it, and it turns the interview into a conversation.

### 5. Their questions

- *"What do you want to know about the club?"*
- **A candidate with no questions is a mild flag.** Not fatal — some people are just nervous. But
  genuine curiosity shows here.

## Scoring (20 marks)

| Criterion | Marks | Full marks looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence of follow-through** | 5 | A concrete example of finishing something difficult |
| **Handles not knowing well** | 5 | Comfortable saying "I don't know", curious rather than defensive |
| **Safety disposition** | 5 | Treats safety as fundamental, not as paperwork |
| **Communication** | 3 | Clear, honest, listens as well as talks |
| **Genuine interest in *this*** | 2 | Can say why rocketry specifically, not just "a technical club" |

### Total ranking

| Component | Marks |
|---|---|
| Open-book test | 50 |
| Essay | 20 |
| Doubt-session engagement | 10 |
| **Interview** | **20** |
| **Total** | **100** |

---

## 🚩 Red flags

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **"It worked, so it's fine"** about a skipped safety step | The single most serious flag. Do not admit to hands-on work. |
| **Confidently guesses** at a technical question rather than admitting ignorance | In rocketry, confident guessing bursts motors |
| **Cannot name anything they finished** | Weak predictor of staying |
| **Blames others** for a past failure, with no self-reflection | Will do the same when a test fails |
| **No questions at all**, and no curiosity about the work | May want *a club*, not *this club* |
| **Only talks about prestige, competitions, or resume** | Will leave when it stops being impressive |
| **Cannot discuss their own essay** | Likely generated. Ask them to expand on a specific sentence. |
| **Very high load** — five other clubs, heavy placement prep | Will not have the hours. Be honest about the commitment. |

## ✅ Green flags

| Green flag | Why |
|---|---|
| **Admits a weakness unprompted** | Self-awareness is rarer and more useful than confidence |
| **Talks about a failure with interest rather than shame** | Exactly the disposition rocketry requires |
| **Asks a specific technical question** | Genuine curiosity |
| **Says "I don't know" cleanly** | Will learn faster and be safer |
| **Has fixed or built something, anything** | Evidence of the instinct |
| **Pushes back on something you said, with reasoning** | Independent thinking. Welcome it. |
| **Asks what happens if they don't make core** | Cares about being involved, not just selected |

---

## Practical guidance

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| **Two interviewers, always** | One person's impression is unreliable and unfair |
| Score independently, then compare | Discussing during the interview anchors you both |
| Keep to 15 minutes | Longer is not better and it exhausts you |
| Take notes during | You will not remember 15 candidates |
| Ask the same core questions of everyone | Otherwise you cannot compare |
| Tell them the timeline for the decision | Uncertainty breeds resentment |
| **Thank them regardless of outcome** | They are your classmates |

### If you are interviewing many people

- **Batch them.** Four or five per session, with a break between.
- **Reconcile scores the same day.** Memory decays fast.
- **Watch for your own bias** toward people who talk like you, or who share your branch. The quiet
  mechanical student and the loud CSE student may both be excellent — for different subsystems.

---

## After the interview

**Within 48 hours**, send everyone an outcome. Both kinds.

| Outcome | What to say |
|---|---|
| **Core team** | Welcome, plus the first task and the next meeting date |
| **General member** | Welcome, plus what they get (workshops, learning paths, launches) and the next open door |
| **Not this time** | Thank them genuinely, tell them the next intake exists, and invite them to the next workshop |

> 🔴 **Do not ghost anyone.** Twenty people gave you four weeks of their time. A two-line message costs
> you nothing and protects your reputation in a college you will be in for years.

---

## The decision meeting

After all interviews, the panel meets once:

1. **Rank by total score** — but do not treat it as a cut-off
2. **Discuss every candidate with a red flag**, regardless of score
3. **Discuss every candidate with a strong essay but a weak test** — these are often the best people
4. **Balance the team by subsystem**, not just by score. You need avionics as much as you need
   propulsion.
5. **Check the year distribution.** Weight toward 1st and 2nd years — they are the club's future.
6. **Confirm the general membership list.** Everyone who reached Stage 4 should be on it.
