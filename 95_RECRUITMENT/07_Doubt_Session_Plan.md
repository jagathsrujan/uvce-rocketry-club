# Stage 3 — Doubt Session Plan

**The first in-person commitment. The highest-value stage in the funnel.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **When** | Week 2 |
| **Duration** | 90 minutes |
| **Where** | A classroom or lab with a projector |
| **Who runs it** | You + 2–3 helpers |
| **Output** | Questions answered, and your first real read on who is serious |

---

## Why this stage matters more than the others

Stages 1, 2, 4 and 5 are all **asynchronous**. Anyone can watch a video or read a document in bed.
**This is the first time they have to leave their room and show up.**

That single fact makes this the most informative stage:

- **Turning up is the filter.** Not knowledge, not eloquence — just showing up.
- **It is also the conversion point.** Passive interest becomes a relationship here, or it does not.
- **And it is where most of your eventual core team will first reveal themselves** — not by knowing
  things, but by asking good questions and staying afterwards.

> 🔴 **Do not skip this because it is inconvenient to organise.** A club that only communicates
> through documents does not become a club.

---

## Format (90 minutes)

| Time | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **0–5 min** | Welcome and context | Who you are, why you are doing this |
| **5–20 min** | "What we're actually building" | Concrete, specific, with our real numbers |
| **20–35 min** | Live demonstration | 🔴 **The most important 15 minutes** |
| **35–65 min** | Open Q&A | Their questions, not yours |
| **65–80 min** | "What happens next" | The funnel, honestly explained |
| **80–90 min** | Informal hang-around | 🔴 **Where the real signal is** |

---

## Segment 2 — What we're actually building (15 min)

**Do not give a lecture about rocketry.** They have watched the videos.

Instead, answer the question they actually have: **"What would I be doing?"**

| Show | Why |
|---|---|
| **Our real numbers** — 40 mm, ~900 mm, 250 g, 24 N·s, 200 m | Makes it concrete |
| **The workspace** — open the README, scroll the folders | Proves it is real, not a WhatsApp group |
| **A spreadsheet, live** — open the motor calculator, change a number, let the throat diameter recompute | 🔴 **This lands harder than any slide.** They see that this is engineering, not enthusiasm. |
| **An honest weakness** — e.g. "we don't know our burn rate yet, and that blocks the motor design" | Credibility. Anyone can claim a plan. |

> **Do not oversell.** A club that promises a rocket in three months and delivers nothing loses
> everyone. A club that says "this will take a year and we will fail repeatedly" keeps the people
> worth keeping.

---

## Segment 3 — Live demonstration (15 min) 🔴

**If you do only one thing well, do this.** A demonstration converts people in a way that talking
cannot.

### Options, in order of impact

| # | Demo | Cost | Setup |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **A water rocket launch** | ₹300 | 10 min |
| **2** | **OpenRocket live** — build a rocket, run a sim, show the trajectory | ₹0 | 5 min |
| **3** | **A fin alignment jig** — pass it round, let them hold it | ₹200 | — |
| **4** | **A test-stand load cell** — press it, show the reading on the laptop | ₹800 | 20 min |
| **5** | **A cast inert grain** — salt instead of potassium nitrate, safe to handle | ₹100 | — |

**Best combination if you have 15 minutes:** OpenRocket live (5 min) + water rocket or inert grain
passed around (10 min).

> ⚠️ **Never bring live propellant to an indoor session.** Use inert grains (table salt replaces the
> potassium nitrate, same 65/35 ratio) or nothing energetic at all. This is non-negotiable, and it is
> also a demonstration of the culture you are asking them to join.

---

## Segment 4 — Open Q&A (30 min)

### Facilitation rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Answer honestly, including "I don't know"** | They will model your behaviour. If you bluff, they will bluff. |
| **Redirect to the source** — "that's in the reader, page 5" | Teaches them the documentation habit |
| **Welcome basic questions** | "What is a grain?" is exactly what you want them asking |
| **Do not let one person dominate** | "Let's take a question from someone who hasn't asked yet" |
| **Write questions down** | Unanswered questions become the next reader revision |
| **If a question is about propellant, be careful** | Give the principle, not a procedure. Point to the SOPs. |

### Questions you will get

Prepare short answers for:

| Question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| *"Do I need to be good at maths?"* | No. You need algebra and care with units. |
| *"Is it dangerous?"* | Yes, genuinely — and that is exactly why we have the procedures we do. Here is how we manage it. |
| *"Can I break things?"* | Yes. Things will break. That is the process. |
| *"How much time?"* | Honestly: 6–8 hours a week for core members. Less for general. |
| *"What if I don't make the core team?"* | You get workshops, the learning material, and access to launches. And you can apply again. 🔴 **Answer this well — it is the question that decides whether people commit.** |
| *"Can girls join?"* | Obviously yes — and say so plainly. If the room is male-dominated, address it. |
| *"When do we launch something?"* | Give a real, modest timeline. Do not promise a date you cannot hold. |

---

## Segment 5 — What happens next (15 min)

**Be completely transparent about the funnel.** Surprises feel like rejection; a stated process feels
like a fair game.

| Stage | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Reader + open-book test** | 10 pages, open book. We are testing whether you read. | Week 3 |
| **Essay** | 500 words, one prompt | Week 3 |
| **Interview** | 15 minutes, informal | Week 4 |
| **Decision** | Within 48 hours of the last interview | Week 4 |
| **General membership** | Everyone who gets to Stage 4 | Week 4 |

**Say explicitly:** *"You are not competing against each other for a fixed number of seats. We are
looking for people who will show up."* This changes the room's atmosphere from competitive to
collaborative — and collaboration is what you are actually selecting for.

---

## Segment 6 — The hang-around (10+ min) 🔴

**Do not pack up and leave.** Stay. Talk to people individually.

**This is where you will actually identify your core team.** Not from the questions they asked in the
room, but from who stays to ask one more thing when nobody is watching.

**Watch for:**

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ Stays to ask a specific technical question | Genuine curiosity |
| ✅ Asks "what can I do *now*?" | Wants to start |
| ✅ Asks about a subsystem specifically | Has already thought about where they fit |
| ✅ Talks to other attendees, not just you | Will work in a team |
| ⚠️ Leaves immediately | Not necessarily bad, but tells you nothing |

**Take a note afterwards** of who stayed and what they asked. This feeds the doubt-session engagement
score (10 marks).

---

## Logistics checklist

- [ ] **Room booked** — with a projector and power
- [ ] **Time chosen** to avoid clashes with lectures (late afternoon works well)
- [ ] **Reminder sent** 24 hours before, to everyone who signed up
- [ ] **Demo materials ready** — water rocket built, OpenRocket file loaded, inert grain cast
- [ ] **Laptop and cables** — test the projector before people arrive
- [ ] **Attendance sheet** — names and numbers, to confirm who actually came
- [ ] **Printed copies of the reader** — for anyone who wants one (most will prefer digital)
- [ ] **2–3 helpers** to answer questions and note who engages
- [ ] **Photos taken** — for the club's social media and records
- [ ] **Room left clean**

---

## After the session (same evening)

1. **Send the reader** to everyone who attended — [`03_Foundation_Reader.md`](<03_Foundation_Reader.md>)
2. **Send the test and essay prompt** with a clear deadline
3. **Note who attended**, and who stayed afterwards
4. **Write down every unanswered question** — these become improvements to the reader
5. **Follow up individually** with anyone who seemed particularly engaged. A personal message costs
   you two minutes and is disproportionately effective.

---

## The honest measure of success

**If 35 people said they were coming and 12 turn up, that is a normal and good result.**

Do not be discouraged. The 12 who came are worth more than the 35 who did not, and the funnel is
working exactly as designed.
