# 00 — Building the Founding Team

**The 2–4 people you need before the recruitment funnel can run at all.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **When** | 3 weeks before the recruitment drive begins |
| **How many** | **3 minimum** (including you). 4 is better. More than 5 is a committee. |
| **Selection basis** | Disposition and follow-through — **not** knowledge. Nobody at UVCE has any. |
| **Output** | Named subsystem leads who can each teach their own Foundation tier |

---

## 1. Why you need founders before you need members

You cannot run the funnel alone. Concretely:

| Stage | Why one person cannot do it |
|---|---|
| **Doubt session** | You will get propulsion, structures, avionics and regulatory questions in the same hour. One person cannot answer all of them well. |
| **Interviews** | 🔴 **Two panellists minimum.** One person's impression is unreliable and unfair to the candidate. |
| **Test marking** | Two people mark independently, then reconcile. One marker anchors on their own bias. |
| **Demo preparation** | Building the water rocket, loading OpenRocket, casting an inert grain — that is a morning's work for two people and a whole day for one. |
| **Continuity** | If you are the only founder and you get sick, have exams, or lose motivation, the club stops existing. |

> 🔴 **The single most important structural decision you will make:** nobody owns a subsystem alone.
> Not even you. Every role needs a named second person, or the club dies the semester that person
> has exams.

---

## 2. The minimum founding team

| Role | Essential? | Profile | Why this role exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Team Lead / Systems** | ✅ **You** | Organised, persistent, comfortable being the one who keeps things moving | Owns the roadmap, the design numbers, and the institutional relationships |
| **Propulsion Lead** | ✅ **Essential** | Meticulous, patient, takes safety seriously without being told | The highest-consequence subsystem. This person sets the club's safety culture. |
| **Avionics Lead** | ✅ **Essential** | Comfortable with code **or** electronics (both is rare, one is enough) | The largest single workload, and the hardest role to fill |
| **Structures Lead** | 🟠 Strongly wanted | Hands-on, enjoys fabrication, patient with fiddly work | The most tractable subsystem — but someone must own it |
| **Operations Lead** | 🟠 Strongly wanted | Organised, writes well, follows up without reminders | Documented everything, runs logistics, chases approvals |

### If you can only find two people

| Combination | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **You + Avionics** | ✅ Workable. You take propulsion *and* systems temporarily. Recruit structures at the funnel. |
| **You + Propulsion** | 🟡 Workable, but you must then own avionics — and you are mechanical. Expect to struggle. |
| **You + Structures** | ❌ Weakest combination. Avionics is the larger workload and the harder hire. |

> **If you can only get one person, get the avionics lead.** Structures and propulsion can be learned
> from documents by a motivated mechanical student. Avionics needs a different skill set entirely,
> and you cannot fake your way through a flight computer.

---

## 3. Where to find them

**This is a different search from general recruitment.** You are not advertising a club; you are
asking specific people to take on specific responsibility.

### The founder pitch (different from the member pitch)

> **Do not say:** *"I'm starting a rocketry club, want to join?"*
>
> That is easy to say yes to and easy to abandon.

> **Do say:** *"I'm building a rocketry club, and I need someone to own the propulsion side. I've
> already done the research — there are 60+ documents in a workspace. What I don't have is a second
> person who will actually show up. Are you in?"*

**Why this works:** it names a specific responsibility, it proves the work is already underway, and it
makes the ask personal rather than generic.

**Your 386,000-word workspace is the strongest asset you have here.** Open it. Let them scroll it.
Most student club pitches are a WhatsApp group and an intention. Yours is 64 documents and four
working spreadsheets.

### Candidate sources, in order

| # | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **People you already know who finish things** | 🔴 Best source by far. Think of anyone you have seen complete a project, fix something, or keep a commitment. |
| 2 | **Classmates who ask good questions** | The person who stays after a lecture to ask a follow-up |
| 3 | **Other branches' students** | You need ECE/EEE for avionics and CSE for software. Actively cross branches. |
| 4 | **Marvel Student Track participants** | They are already self-selected for hands-on work |
| 5 | **Anyone who has built or fixed anything** | A repaired bike, a hacked calculator, a rebuilt PC — the subject is irrelevant, the instinct is not |
| 6 | ❌ **Open call for founders** | Produces title-seekers. Founders are recruited personally, one at a time. |

### What to actually look for

In rough order of importance:

| # | Quality | How you detect it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Finishes things** | Ask for an example. Vague answers mean no. |
| 2 | **Shows up** | Give them a small task. See if they do it without a reminder. |
| 3 | **Handles not knowing** | Ask them something they cannot answer. Watch the response. |
| 4 | **Safety disposition** | Read them the CATO energy argument. Do they take it seriously or shrug? |
| 5 | **Works with others** | Will they share credit? |
| 6 | **Available** | 🔴 **Ask directly how many hours a week they have.** Do not assume. |

> ⚠️ **What you are NOT looking for: existing rocketry knowledge.** Nobody at UVCE has any, including
> you. If you filter for expertise you will select for confidence — and you will end up with someone
> who talks well and builds nothing.

## 5. Checkpoints — what each founder must be able to explain

**If a founder cannot explain these without notes, they are not ready to sit in the doubt session.**

### 5.1 Propulsion Lead

- [ ] What KNSB is (65/35), and why sorbitol rather than sucrose
- [ ] What **total impulse** is, and what class our motor is (~24 N·s, E-class)
- [ ] Why **specific impulse** matters — and why KNSB means a *bigger* motor than APCP
- [ ] What **Kn** is and what it controls
- [ ] Why you **cannot independently choose** impulse, thrust, burn time and grain size
- [ ] Progressive / neutral / regressive, and what shapes the thrust curve
- [ ] Why our grains are **freestanding**, referring to the material properties
- [ ] What **"mesa propellant"** means and why it complicates design
- [ ] The main **CATO causes** (voids, cracks, low density, oversized igniter)
- [ ] Why **ignition primer is mandatory** for KNSB, and how thick the coat should be

### 5.2 Structures Lead

- [ ] The **four systems** and how they interact (mass, drag, load tradeoffs)
- [ ] **CG vs CP** and the rule
- [ ] **Static margin**, the target range, and how to calculate it
- [ ] Why **too much stability** is a problem (weathercocking)
- [ ] Why **PVC is dangerous** and what paper/phenolic do instead
- [ ] What the **15 g / 30 g** requirements mean
- [ ] The **2.5 × MEOP** rule — including why there is an *upper* limit
- [ ] **Fin flutter** and why thickness matters so much
- [ ] **Buckling** — why thin tubes fail below their material strength
- [ ] **Descent rate** and how to size a parachute

### 5.3 Avionics Lead

- [ ] The **five functions** of the flight computer
- [ ] The **state machine** and why state must survive a processor reset
- [ ] **Apogee detection** — barometer as primary, and the four failure modes
- [ ] Why **LiPo batteries are prohibited**, and why spring contacts are too
- [ ] **Redundant deployment chains** — and why they must share nothing
- [ ] 🔴 **Why a stalled servo can reset the flight computer**, and how to prevent it
- [ ] The **telemetry requirements** (≥1 Hz, field order, file naming)
- [ ] Why the barometer must be **zeroed at the pad**
- [ ] Why **test stand instrumentation comes before** the flight computer
- [ ] How a **load cell** is calibrated

### 5.4 Operations Lead

- [ ] The **regulatory thresholds** (10,000 / 40,960 N·s) and where our motors sit
- [ ] What **approvals** are needed, and from whom
- [ ] The **documentation standard** — "if it is not written down, it did not happen"
- [ ] The **funnel stages** and the timeline
- [ ] The **budget** and where the money comes from
- [ ] The **Marvel tracks** and what to apply to
- [ ] The difference between a **PDR and a CDR**

### 5.5 Team Lead (you)

- [ ] Everything above, at least in summary
- [ ] The **current blocking issue** (unknown burn-rate coefficients) and why it matters
- [ ] The **roadmap** and what the next three milestones are
- [ ] Each founder's subsystem, well enough to know when they are stuck

## 6. The practical — each founder builds something

**Reading does not make you credible. Having made something does.**

This does not need to be ambitious. It needs to be *done*.

| Role | Minimum practical | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Propulsion** | Cast an **inert grain** (table salt replaces potassium nitrate, same 65/35 ratio) | Half a day | ₹150 |
| **Structures** | Build and fly a **water rocket**, or cut and square an airframe tube | Half a day | ₹300 |
| **Avionics** | Read a **barometer or IMU** and log readings to a CSV | Half a day | ₹800 |
| **Operations** | Set up the **shared folder, form, and broadcast group** for the funnel | 2 hours | ₹0 |
| **Team Lead** | Run **OpenRocket** and produce a trajectory for our 40 mm rocket | 2 hours | ₹0 |

> 🔴 **Do the practical before the doubt session.** When someone asks *"have you actually built
> anything?"*, the answer needs to be yes. It is also the strongest possible answer to *"is this
> real?"* — and you can bring the object with you.

## 7. Splitting the funnel

Assign these before the drive starts.

| Stage | Owner | Support |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Announcement** | Team Lead | Operations |
| **2. Video compilation** | Team Lead | — |
| **3. Doubt session** | Team Lead facilitates | 🔴 **Each founder answers their own subsystem's questions** |
| **3. Live demo** | Structures (water rocket) + Team Lead (OpenRocket) | — |
| **4. Test marking** | Team Lead **+ one other**, marking independently | — |
| **5. Essay marking** | Operations Lead + Team Lead | — |
| **6. Interviews** | 🔴 **Two panellists per interview, rotating** — pair the Team Lead with each subsystem lead in turn | — |
| **Decision meeting** | All founders | — |
| **Onboarding** | Team Lead | Operations |

### Interview panel rotation

Pair up so every candidate meets two founders, and no founder interviews everyone alone:

| Session | Panellists |
|---|---|
| Interviews 1–4 | Team Lead + Propulsion |
| Interviews 5–8 | Team Lead + Structures |
| Interviews 9–12 | Team Lead + Avionics |
| Interviews 13–15 | Propulsion + Avionics |

**Why rotate:** you get multiple independent reads on each candidate, and each founder gets a say in
who they will be working alongside. It also prevents any one founder's bias from deciding the intake.

## 8. What to tell a prospective founder

Be completely honest. The pitch is stronger for it.

> **What this is:** a rocketry club that builds real hardware. We will design and cast our own solid
> motors, build flight computers, and test on a private site outside Bengaluru.
>
> **What exists already:** the research. 64 documents, four working engineering spreadsheets, a
> validated propellant reference, safety procedures, a recruitment plan. What does not exist is a
> second person who will actually show up.
>
> **What I need from you:** own one subsystem. Roughly 6–8 hours a week. Read the material in the
> first three weeks, then help run recruitment.
>
> **What I am not asking for:** any prior knowledge. Neither of us has any. I am asking whether you
> will read the documents and turn up on Saturday.
>
> **The honest risk:** this will probably take a year before we fly anything meaningful, and most
> tests will fail. If that sounds tedious rather than interesting, this is not for you — and that is
> a completely reasonable position.

## 9. The founder's covenant

Agree these on day one, in writing. They are the club's constitution in miniature.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Nobody owns a subsystem alone** | Every role has a named second. This is the rule that keeps the club alive. |
| **If you cannot make a commitment, say so** | Silence is what kills projects |
| **Document what you do** | Undocumented work is invisible to everyone who comes after |
| **Teach what you learn** | The final test of understanding |
| **Safety overrides everything, including your own plan** | Including the Team Lead's plan |
| **Anyone can call a stop** | Including a member in their first week. Especially then. |
| **If you leave, hand over properly** | A written handover is the price of departure |

## 10. Red flags in a prospective founder

| Red flag | Why it is disqualifying |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **"I'll help whenever you need me"** | Means they will help never. Founders need a named subsystem, not availability. |
| 🔴 **Wants a title, asks no questions about the work** | Title-seeker. Will vanish when it stops being prestigious. |
| 🔴 **Does not do the Week 1 reading** | This is your test, before you have committed anything. If they fail it now, they will fail it in month three. |
| **Immediately proposes changes to the plan in week 1** | Enthusiasm is fine; rewriting the roadmap before reading it is not |
| **Dismisses the safety material** | Direct liability. See the CATO video. |
| **Cannot name anything they have finished** | Strong predictor of not finishing this |
| **Says yes to everything** | Will overcommit and then disappear |

## Green flags

| Flag | Why |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Does the Week 1 reading without being reminded** | 🔴 The single best predictor you have |
| ✅ **Asks a specific technical question** | Genuine curiosity |
| ✅ **Says "I don't know" cleanly** | Will learn faster and be safer |
| ✅ **Names something they built or fixed** | Evidence of the instinct |
| ✅ **Asks what happens if they fail** | Thinking about risk honestly |
| ✅ **Pushes back on part of the plan, with reasoning** | Independent thinking. Welcome it. |

> ⚠️ **Do not skip the teach-back.** It is tempting, because you all "know" the material by then. But
> explaining a thing out loud is where you find out you did not actually understand it — and the
> doubt session is not the place to discover that.

## 12. The honest position

**You are asking people to join something with no history, no funding, no members, and no hardware.**

What you have instead is **evidence of work already done**. That is genuinely rare in a student club
pitch, and it is your entire advantage. Use it.

**Expect to ask five or six people before two say yes.** That is normal. The ones who say yes after
seeing the workspace are the ones who will still be there in a year.

**And if you cannot find a third person within three weeks:** run the funnel anyway, with two. It will
be harder and the doubt session will be shakier, but a club of two that recruits five is a club. A
club of one waiting for the perfect co-founder is nothing.
