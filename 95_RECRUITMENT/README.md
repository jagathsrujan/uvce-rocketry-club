# UVCE Rocketry Club — Recruitment & Induction Programme

**A six-stage funnel from "interested" to "core member."**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 4–5 weeks end to end |
| **Entry** | Anyone who responds to the announcement |
| **Exit** | 5–8 core members + a wider general membership |
| **Companion** | [`../90_LEARNING/`](<../90_LEARNING/README.md>) — the ongoing training paths after induction |

---

## The funnel

```
STAGE 1 ──► STAGE 2 ──► STAGE 3 ──► STAGE 4 ──► STAGE 5 ──► STAGE 6
Announce   Videos     Doubt       Reader +    Essay +     Interview
+ collect  (async)    session     test        submission  → CORE TEAM
                                                             ↓
                                                    Everyone else → general
                                                    membership + workshops
   ~100        ~50        ~35         ~25           ~15           5–8 core
```

**Expected attrition is the design, not a failure.** Each stage filters for a different thing:

| Stage | Filters for |
|---|---|
| 1 → 2 | Curiosity |
| 2 → 3 | Follow-through on a low-effort task |
| 3 → 4 | Willingness to show up in person |
| 4 → 5 | Willingness to read and think |
| 5 → 6 | Ability to articulate their own view |
| 6 | **Commitment and fit** — the only thing that actually matters |

> 🔴 **Do not skip stages to save time.** The early stages are cheap and they remove the people who
> would have wasted your time later. A member who cannot watch four videos will not build a motor.

## Stage-by-stage summary

### Stage 1 — Announcement (Week 1)
Broadcast to college groups, WhatsApp, branch notice boards, classroom announcements.
**Goal:** collect names and contact details. Nothing more.

### Stage 2 — Video compilation (Week 1–2, async)
A curated 2-hour watch list: what rocketry is, what we're building, who does this well, and why it's
worth your time. **Deliberately low effort** — anyone can watch videos.

**Output:** a one-line message saying which video interested them most. Nothing more.

### Stage 3 — Doubt session (Week 2, in person, 90 min)
The first in-person commitment. Free-form Q&A plus a live demonstration.

**This is the highest-value stage.** It converts passive interest into a relationship.

### Stage 4 — Foundation Reader + open-book test (Week 3)
A 10-page reader covering the fundamentals, then an **open-book** test on it.

**Why open book:** you are testing whether they will *read and find information* — which is the actual
engineering skill — not whether they can memorise. Open book also removes exam anxiety as a barrier.

### Stage 5 — Essay (Week 3–4)
500 words on a set prompt. Reveals genuine thinking versus parroting.

### Stage 6 — Interview (Week 4)
15 minutes, structured. Focused on **commitment and fit**, not knowledge.

## 🔴 The thing most clubs get wrong

**Everyone who reaches Stage 4 must be given somewhere to belong, whether or not they make core.**

If you run a six-stage funnel and then ghost the 20 people who didn't make the core team, you have
burned 20 people's goodwill in your own college. You will not get a second chance at that
reputation.

**So decide the parallel path now:**

| Outcome | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Core team (5–8)** | Subsystem ownership, the funded work, competition travel |
| **General member** | Workshop access, the learning paths in [`90_LEARNING/`](<../90_LEARNING/README.md>), attendance at tests and launches |
| **Everyone who applied** | A thank-you message, and an open invitation to the next workshop |

**Tell them this at Stage 1.** People commit harder when they know that trying does not mean being
discarded.

## What you are actually selecting for

In rough order of importance:

| # | Quality | How to detect it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Follows through** | Did they complete each stage on time, without reminders? |
| 2 | **Shows up** | Did they come to the doubt session in person? |
| 3 | **Reads and thinks** | The open-book test and the essay |
| 4 | **Handles not knowing** | How do they respond to a question they cannot answer? |
| 5 | **Safety disposition** | Do they take the safety content seriously or treat it as boilerplate? |
| 6 | **Works with others** | How they behave in the doubt session |

> ⚠️ **What you are NOT selecting for: prior knowledge.** Nobody in your college knows rocketry. If you
> filter for existing expertise you will select for confidence, not capability — and you will miss the
> quiet person who would have become your best propulsion lead.

## Honest expectations

| Stage | Who drops out | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 → 2 | Most | Curiosity is cheap |
| 2 → 3 | Many | Watching videos is easy; showing up is not |
| 3 → 4 | Some | Reading 10 pages is real work |
| 4 → 5 | Few | If they read, they will write |
| 5 → 6 | Few | |
| **Overall** | **~90%** | **This is normal and healthy** |

**If 100 people respond and you end with 6 core members and 15 general members, that is a
successful drive.** Do not be discouraged by the numbers — be suspicious if the numbers are too
good, because it usually means the stages were too easy to be meaningful.

## Before you start — prerequisites

- [ ] **Faculty advisor confirmed** (even informally) — you need a staff contact for room bookings
- [ ] **A room booked** for the doubt session and the test
- [ ] **The 10-page reader finalised** — [`03_Foundation_Reader.md`](<03_Foundation_Reader.md>)
- [ ] **The test and answer key finalised** — [`04_Open_Book_Test.md`](<04_Open_Book_Test.md>)
- [ ] **Two other people to help assess** — you cannot interview alone and you should not try
- [ ] **A WhatsApp broadcast group created** (announcements only, Core Team as admins)
- [ ] **A shared folder** for distributing the reader and collecting submissions
- [ ] **Decided what general members get** — see the warning above
