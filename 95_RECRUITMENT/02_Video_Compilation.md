---

## Why this stage exists

Two reasons, and only two:

1. **To give you a picture of what we actually do** — so you can decide whether this is for you
   before committing real time.
2. **To filter for follow-through.** Watching two hours of video is not hard. But it *is* a task, and
   the people who do it are the people who will show up later.

> **You do not need to understand everything.** Nobody does after two hours. If a video goes over your
> head, that is expected and fine — the point is exposure, not mastery.

---

## PLAYLIST 1 — The hook (20 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@BPSspace](https://www.youtube.com/@BPSspace)

**Find:** the channel's **most popular videos** (sort by "Popular" on the Videos tab).

**Watch:** any **two** of the shorter videos.

**Why:** Joe Barnard builds real flight hardware in his garage — thrust-vectored landing, custom flight
computers, actual failures on camera. This is the closest thing to what we do, made by someone who is
honest about how hard it is.

**What to notice:** how often things fail, and how he responds to failure.

---

## PLAYLIST 2 — The basics (30 min)

### 2a. How rockets work (15 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@NASA](https://www.youtube.com/@NASA)

**Search within the channel:** `how rockets work`, `thrust`, `Newton's third law rocket`

For deeper fundamentals, the written version is excellent and free:
[NASA Beginner's Guide to Rockets](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/rocket/)

**Why:** NASA explains thrust, impulse and staging more clearly than almost anyone. This is the
foundation everything else sits on.

### 2b. Real launches, with explanation (15 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@EverydayAstronaut](https://www.youtube.com/@EverydayAstronaut)

**Find:** any launch breakdown or vehicle explainer.

**Why:** Tim Dodd explains *why* real rockets are built the way they are — staging, propellant choice,
why they're so big. It makes the engineering constraints feel real.

---

## PLAYLIST 3 — The builders (50 min) ⭐ MOST IMPORTANT

This is the section that matters most. These are people doing what we are trying to do, at varying
scales.

### 3a. The sugar motor pioneer (20 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@richardnakka7434](https://www.youtube.com/@richardnakka7434)

**Why:** Richard Nakka has been designing and testing **potassium nitrate/sorbitol motors since the
1970s**. **Our propellant is his propellant.** Every measured number in our reference documents traces
back to his testing.

**What to notice:** he tests on a stand, records pressure and thrust, and publishes the data — including
the failures. That is the methodology we are copying.

**This is the single most relevant channel on this list.**

### 3b. Model rocketry, done properly (15 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@apogeerockets](https://www.youtube.com/@apogeerockets)

**Find:** build videos, "how to" content, or their newsletter discussions.

**Why:** the practical craft — building airframes, packing parachutes, stability, finishing. This is
the stuff you will do with your hands in month one.

### 3c. Something that goes wrong (15 min)

**Search YouTube for:** `solid rocket motor CATO`, `rocket motor static test failure`

**Why:** ⚠️ **This one is not optional.** Watch at least one motor fail.

**What to notice:** how *violent* it is, and how far debris travels. This is why we fire from 75–100
metres behind hard cover, and why the safety rules are not negotiable. A CATO on video teaches in
thirty seconds what a document cannot.

## PLAYLIST 4 — What's possible (25 min)

### 4a. Scale (15 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@SpaceX](https://www.youtube.com/@SpaceX) **or**
[youtube.com/@EverydayAstronaut](https://www.youtube.com/@EverydayAstronaut)

**Find:** a full launch or a vehicle overview.

**Why:** perspective. What we build is tiny, and that is fine — but it is built on exactly the same
physics. The difference is engineering resources, not principles.

### 4b. India's programme (10 min)

**Go to:** [youtube.com/@isroofficial5866](https://www.youtube.com/@isroofficial5866)

**Why:** this is the national context we are working in. IN-SPACe — the body that runs the competition
we are aiming at — exists to build exactly this kind of student and startup ecosystem.

## PLAYLIST 5 — Our project (10 min)

**Read:** [`../README.md`](<../README.md>) — the workspace index — and
[`../UVCE_Rocketry_Roadmap.md`](<../UVCE_Rocketry_Roadmap.md>).

**Why:** this is what *we* are building, with our actual numbers (40 mm, 200 m, 24 N·s), our actual

## After watching — your output

Send **one message** containing:

1. **Which video interested you most, and why.** One or two lines. There is no correct answer.
2. **One thing you did not understand.** Genuine confusion is welcome and useful — it tells us what
   to cover in the doubt session.
3. **Which subsystem sounds most interesting to you** — propulsion, structures, avionics, simulation,
   or operations. *You are allowed to say "I don't know yet", and that is a perfectly good answer.*

> **Why we ask for this.** It is not a test. It is the first small piece of follow-through, and it
> gives us something to talk about at the doubt session instead of starting from nothing.

---

## A note on what you will and will not get from this

**You will get:** a sense of whether this excites you, what the work looks like, and how failure-prone
it is.

**You will not get:** any real understanding of how to design a motor. That comes later, and it comes
from reading the [Foundation Reader](<03_Foundation_Reader.md>) and then building things.

**If you finish these two hours and think "that looks tedious"** — that is genuinely useful
information, and there is no shame in it. Better to find out now than after three months.
